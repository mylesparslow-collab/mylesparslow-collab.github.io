Field guide · October 2026

Use AI freely, and know where the edges are.

Most AI use is low risk. A handful of habits cover nearly all of the real risk: what you paste in, which account you use, which settings you check, and how you verify what comes back. This guide covers those habits with the sources behind them.

Vendor settings change often. Everything here was checked against the vendors’ own pages on 14 October 2026; where a vendor’s page did not say something, this guide says so rather than guessing. Not legal advice. Companion: the prompting field guide.

01 · The short version

Ten habits that cover most of it

None of these require giving up AI. They are what security teams, bar associations and the vendors themselves keep recommending.

  1. Paste in tiers

    Public stuff goes anywhere. Private stuff goes only to tools you have vetted. Regulated or secret stuff stays out. See the three tiers below.

  2. Use the work account for work

    Surveys keep finding most workplace AI use on personal accounts. Work tiers usually have different terms than free consumer ones.

  3. Check the training switch once

    Every major chat product has one. It takes a minute and applies from then on. Section 04 has the path for each.

  4. Treat “temporary” as “shorter”, not “gone”

    Temporary and incognito chats are typically still held for days (30 for ChatGPT and Claude, 72 hours for Gemini) for safety review.

  5. Don’t send thumbs on private chats

    At several vendors, rating a reply can send the whole conversation for review or training even if you opted out.

  6. Think before you share a link

    Shared chats have been indexed by search engines more than once. Anyone with the link can read it.

  7. Verify anything you will act on

    Open the cited source. Confirm the quote is in it. Fake citations have reached courtrooms, government reports and journals.

  8. Be suspicious of agreement

    Models lean toward telling you what you want to hear. Ask for the strongest case against your idea.

  9. Limit what an agent can touch

    Private data, untrusted content and a way to send data out should not all be in one session. Approve irreversible actions by hand.

  10. Hang up and call back

    A voice or video call can now be faked. Agree on a family safe word and verify money requests on a known number.

02 · What not to paste

Three tiers, not a blanket ban

OWASP’s guidance on sensitive information disclosure is blunt: users should avoid entering sensitive information, and providers should let users opt out of training. A simple sort makes that workable.

Tier 1 · Paste freely

Public or yours to share

  • Published articles, public docs, open-source code
  • Your own writing and ideas you would post anyway
  • General questions with no names or numbers attached
Tier 2 · Approved tools only

Private but not regulated

  • Internal documents, meeting notes, unreleased plans
  • Customer or employee details, in a tool your organisation has approved
  • Proprietary source code
  • Use a business or API tier, or a local model, not a free personal account
Tier 3 · Keep out

Secrets and regulated data

  • Passwords, API keys, private keys, recovery codes
  • Health records and other protected data without an agreement in place
  • Privileged client material without informed consent
  • Government ID, bank and card numbers
Redact, then paste

Most tasks don’t need the real names. Replace people, companies, amounts and account numbers with placeholders (“Client A”, “$X”), get the help, then put the details back yourself.

Why this is not paranoia

Samsung, 2023

Engineers in a Samsung division uploaded internal source code to ChatGPT. The company then banned generative AI tools on its devices and network and told staff not to enter company information.

ChannelNews, citing Bloomberg

Credentials leak by being pasted

Harmonic Security found about 8.5% of business prompts could expose sensitive data in Q4 2024: customer data 45.8%, employee data 26.8%, legal and finance 14.9%, security data 6.9%, sensitive code and keys 5.6%.

THE Journal · vendor survey, sample size not published

03 · At work

Policy, personal accounts and professional duties

The biggest workplace risk is not the tool, it is the account. Surveys from security vendors keep finding the same pattern: lots of sensitive data going into AI through accounts the employer cannot see.

These are vendor-published surveys with different methods, so treat them as the direction of travel, not exact rates. The UK NCSC’s framing is the useful one: it does not tell people to stop, it says to prefer approved tools and asks organisations to understand why staff go around them and offer secure alternatives.

Read your acceptable-use policy

If there isn’t one, ask. The NIST Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1) is the standard reference organisations build policies from.

Lawyers and client confidentiality

ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) says lawyers must assess disclosure risk before entering client data, read the tool’s terms, and get informed consent for self-learning tools. Boilerplate in an engagement letter is “not sufficient”.

National Law Review summary

Health data

HHS says a cloud provider handling protected health information for a covered entity is a business associate and needs a HIPAA business associate agreement. Without one, sending PHI to an AI vendor is generally not permitted. Consumer tiers usually have no such agreement.

HHS · Holland & Hart

The API is a different deal

OpenAI says API content has not been used for training since 1 March 2023, with abuse logs kept up to 30 days. Zero data retention needs prior approval, and Anthropic offers it for API and commercial Claude Code use but not for Free, Pro or Max.

OpenAI · Anthropic

Courts can freeze deletion

In 2025 a court order in the New York Times case forced OpenAI to keep deleted logs. It ended going forward in late September 2025 and was formally lifted on 9 October, but logs already preserved stay preserved.

Engadget

Wrapper apps add a third party

In February 2026 a misconfigured database behind the “Chat & Ask AI” app exposed about 300 million messages from over 25 million users. Prefer the vendor’s own app.

Malwarebytes

Past exposure incidents, for context: a March 2023 ChatGPT bug showed some users other people’s chat titles and, for 1.2% of Plus subscribers in a nine-hour window, partial payment details (OpenAI); ChatGPT’s opt-in “discoverable” shared links appeared in search before OpenAI removed the option in August 2025 (The Register); DeepSeek left an unauthenticated database with chat history and secrets open until Wiz reported it in January 2025 (Wiz).

05 · Local vs cloud

What running a model on your own machine does and doesn’t fix

Local models keep your prompts off a vendor’s servers. That solves a real problem. It does not solve the others.

What local fixes

Where your text goes

  • Ollama’s policy: for local use, “we do not collect, store, transmit, or have access to your prompts.” It still collects some usage metadata and processes prompts on its servers for its cloud-hosted models. Policy
  • LM Studio says chats and documents are never transmitted and the app has no telemetry; update checks send version, OS and IP. Its optional cloud services do send prompts. Policy
  • No training on your data and no retention window to read.
What local does not fix

The rest of the risk

  • Malicious model files. Pickle-format files can run code when loaded; JFrog found about 100 malicious models on Hugging Face. Prefer safetensors and trusted publishers. Hugging Face · THN
  • Exposed servers. Censys and SentinelOne found over 175,000 publicly reachable Ollama hosts in January 2026, many with tool-calling on. Don’t expose yours to the internet. THN
  • Hallucination and prompt injection are properties of the model, local or not.
Rule of thumb

Use local models for the Tier 2 material you would not send to a vendor, cloud models for everything else. If you do run a local server, keep it bound to your own machine and check the project’s docs for its default.

06 · Hallucinations

Why models make things up, and what it has cost people

A hallucination is a fluent, confident statement that isn’t true. It is not a rare glitch, and it is not random.

The plain-English cause

OpenAI’s September 2025 explanation: models are trained and scored mostly on accuracy, so a guess can earn credit while “I don’t know” always earns zero. Standard training and evaluation “reward guessing over acknowledging uncertainty.” Arbitrary facts, like a stranger’s birthday, have no pattern to learn, so the model guesses. In OpenAI’s example, an older model answered nearly every question and was wrong 75% of the time, while a newer one declined 52% of the time and was wrong 26%. OpenAI · paper

Damien Charlotin’s database of court decisions involving AI-fabricated content listed 2,184 cases on 10 October 2026, up from 1,459 in May. The number is live and rising.

Fake packages in code

A study of 16 code models and 576,000 samples found 19.7% of recommended packages did not exist: 5.2% for commercial models, 21.7% for open-source ones. 43% of fake names recurred on every repeat of the same prompt, which lets an attacker register them in advance. Check a package exists and is the real one before installing.

Spracklen et al. · UTSA

AI search is not a citation

The Tow Center tested eight AI search tools on 1,600 queries and found they were collectively wrong more than 60% of the time at identifying news sources. ChatGPT got 134 articles wrong but flagged low confidence only 15 times.

CJR

Rates vary a lot by task

Vectara’s leaderboard has models summarise supplied documents and scores how faithfully they do: from 1.8% to 24.2% unfaithful, May 2026. That is the easy case, with the source in hand. Open-ended questions are usually worse.

Vectara

Sycophancy is a cousin of hallucination

In April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update that had become “noticeably more sycophantic”, validating doubts and reinforcing negative emotions. Its cause was a reward signal built partly on thumbs-up feedback. Ask for criticism explicitly, and be wary of a model that agrees with everything. OpenAI

07 · Verifying output

Spend your checking time where it counts

You can’t check everything. Check in proportion to what happens if it is wrong: a brainstorm needs nothing, a figure in a report needs a source, a legal or medical decision needs a professional.

Stop

Pause before you act on or forward a claim.

Investigate

Who is the source, and would they know?

Find better

Look for coverage from independent sources, not the ones the AI cited.

Trace

Go to the original quote, paper or data and compare it with what the AI said.

Repeat

Ask the same question several times; disagreement is a warning sign.

Escalate

For high stakes, use an official source or a professional.

SIFT, applied to AI

Mike Caulfield’s four moves (Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims) predate chatbots but fit them: pull out the specific claims, dates and sources in an answer and check them against sources the AI did not choose. Caulfield · Northeastern

Ask it again

Oxford researchers showed that asking the same question several times and comparing the meaning of the answers can flag confabulation. It misses errors a model makes consistently, so it’s a signal, not proof. Oxford

Open every link

University library guidance: ask for sources, open the links, check that the source actually supports the claim, and use official or original sources for anything high stakes. University of Arizona

Provenance for images and audio

C2PA Content Credentials can show where media came from, but they are optional, can be stripped, and say nothing about whether content is true; a file without them isn’t automatically suspect. Google’s SynthID watermark detects only Google’s own marks. Neither is a universal detector. C2PA · verify tool · limits of watermarking

A second model can be a useful reviewer, but no source I found specifically recommends it as verification, so treat its agreement as one more weak signal rather than a check.

08 · Prompt injection

When the text the AI reads tells it what to do

Models can’t reliably separate your instructions from instructions hidden in a web page, email or file they are reading. OWASP lists prompt injection first in its 2025 top ten for LLM applications and says it is unclear whether fool-proof prevention exists. The UK NCSC goes further: such attacks “may never be totally mitigated”. OpenAI says the same of its browser agent: unlikely to ever be fully “solved”.

The lethal trifecta

Risk appears when one agent has all three: access to your private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a way to send data out. Remove any one. Simon Willison

EchoLeak (2025)

A crafted email could make Microsoft 365 Copilot leak internal data when the user later asked an ordinary question (CVE-2025-32711). Patched in June 2025, no customer action needed, no known exploitation. THN

AI browsers

Brave showed hidden text in a Reddit comment could make Perplexity’s Comet agent read a one-time code from Gmail and post it. A later fix was reported incomplete. Brave

Malicious tool servers

The first known malicious MCP server, a copy of postmark-mcp, added one line that BCC’d every outgoing email to the attacker (1,643 downloads). THN

Malicious skills

Koi Security found 341 malicious skills among 2,857 audited on one agent skill marketplace, disguised as crypto and Google Workspace tools. eSecurity Planet

What helps

  • Narrow, specific instructions and logged-out mode when browsing (OpenAI’s tips)
  • Read confirmation prompts before approving
  • Limit privileges; be wary of any product claiming to “stop” injection (NCSC)
  • Install skills and MCP servers only from sources you trust, and read them
09 · Voice clones and deepfakes

Verify the person, not the voice

The FTC’s advice is short: scammers can clone a voice from a short clip posted online, so “don’t trust the voice”. Hang up and call back on a number you know is theirs. Wire transfers, crypto and gift cards are red flags.

Pick a safe word

The FBI recommends creating a secret word or phrase with your family to verify identity, and limiting how much of your voice and image is online. FBI IC3

Live video can be faked

In 2024 a Hong Kong employee at Arup joined a video call with deepfaked senior staff and sent transfers totalling about US$25.6 million. Arup said its systems were not compromised. Construction Briefing

Process beats instinct

The FBI’s July 2026 notice says AI content is often hard to identify and voice clones can be paired with spoofed caller ID. Require a second channel and a second approver for payments. FBI IC3

10 · Sources and limits

How this guide was made

Researched in October 2026 with Claude: research agents loaded vendor help pages, privacy policies, papers and news reports, and each link was opened and checked against the claim. Every claim above links to its source.

What could not be confirmed

  • The default state (on or off) of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok training toggles was not stated on the vendors’ own pages. Check the setting yourself.
  • Human review policies for Claude and Meta AI, and Meta’s and Perplexity’s current consumer retention periods, were not found on official pages.
  • Whether the court-ordered handover of 20 million de-identified ChatGPT logs in the New York Times case has completed was not confirmed.
  • The Mata v. Avianca order itself did not load; facts come from Bloomberg Law and CNN.
  • Survey statistics come from security vendors with unpublished or differing methods.
  • I found no official privacy statement for llama.cpp, and did not confirm Ollama’s default network binding.
  • Arup details beyond the amount and the video call, and Samsung’s “three incidents” detail, are widely repeated but not confirmed here.
  • Charlotin’s case count and Vectara’s rankings are live and will have changed.
