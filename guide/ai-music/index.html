Handbook · October 2026

Write AI songs that sound like records.

A working method for making songs with Suno, with ChatGPT (or any chat model) as the lyricist. It is the system behind my fourteen cat songs, generalised: premise first, then the musical world, then a locked song structure, lyrics written to be sung, and a diagnosis step before any rewrite.

Suno changes fast. This handbook is current to Suno v6 (released 9 September 2026). Where advice comes from Suno’s own help centre it says so; where it comes from the community or my own testing, it says that too. See also the Prompting Field Guide.

01 · The short version

Ten rules for AI songs

If you read nothing else. Each one is expanded below.

  1. Decide the premise before the words

    Concept, point of view, tone and the central tension or joke. A song with no premise turns into a list of rhymes.

  2. Keep style and lyrics separate

    The Style field describes the record; the Lyrics field holds the words and section labels. Mixing them confuses both.

  3. Make the instrumental sound like a real record

    Name a real genre with real production detail. Funny or strange lyrics don’t need novelty production, and the contrast often helps.

  4. Lead with the main genre

    Order matters: the first genre tends to dominate. Blend two or three at most and add an era to anchor the sound.

  5. Say what you don’t want in Exclude

    “No drums” in the style text can pull drums in. Suno’s Exclude field is the official way to keep something out.

  6. Lock the structure, then the meter

    Equivalent sections get equal line counts and a set syllable pattern. The model needs a predictable shape to write a melody.

  7. Design the hook on its own

    Three to five syllables, simple words, repeated. The chorus gets its own identity rather than summarising the verses.

  8. Write to be sung, not read

    Keep natural word stress, leave room to breathe, give held notes open vowels. Read every line aloud on the beat.

  9. Aim for about three and a half minutes

    Overlong lyrics get rushed. Cut the weakest lines instead of hoping the model sings faster.

  10. Diagnose before rewriting

    Name what is wrong (melody, chorus, density, tone, rhyme) and fix that cause, rather than regenerating and hoping.

02 · The pipeline

Who does what

A chat model is good at words, structure and self-checking. Suno is good at sound. Keeping each tool on the job it does well, and keeping yourself as the judge between them, is most of the method.

1 · Premise

You and the chat model: concept, singer, tone, the central joke or tension, a list of scenes and images.

2 · Musical world

Chat model drafts the style prompt: genre, tempo, drums, bass, harmony, instruments, vocal, space, mood.

3 · Architecture

Sections, line counts and a syllable pattern per section, locked before any verse is written.

4 · Lyrics + audit

Hook first, then verses as scenes, then a rhyme audit, a meter audit and a length audit.

5 · Generate

Suno: paste the two blocks, generate, listen to both takes, keep the better parts.

6 · Diagnose

You name the fault and its cause, then revise the lyric or the style, or edit one section.

How my songs are made

ChatGPT writes the lyrics and style prompts under a written 17-step system; Suno makes the songs; I choose takes, diagnose and direct revisions. Any capable chat model (Claude, Gemini, a local model) can play the lyricist role with the prompts in section 09.

03 · Premise

Start with the premise, not the rhymes

Before a single lyric, write down five things. If you can’t, the song isn’t ready to write.

Core concept

One sentence. “A cat runs a late-night radio call-in show for other cats.”

Point of view

Who sings, to whom, and what they want. First person almost always sings better than narration.

Emotional tone

Smooth, triumphant, wistful, menacing. The tone chooses the genre more than the topic does.

Central tension or joke

For comedy: the singer treats an absurd premise completely seriously. The joke is never explained; it comes out of the character’s logic.

Scenes and images

Brainstorm ten concrete, physical moments before choosing the best three or four. Verses are built from these, not from adjectives.

Characters, if several sing

Each gets a different worldview, vocabulary and kind of joke. If two lines could be swapped between characters, rewrite them.

04 · The style prompt

Describe the record, not the topic

The Style field (Custom or Advanced mode) is where the music lives. The lyrics already say what the song is about, so the style prompt should only describe how it sounds.

What goes in it

  • Genre and era: main genre first, at most two or three blended, plus an era (“late-70s”, “2010s UK”).
  • Tempo and feel: a BPM or a band (“mid-tempo, ~96 BPM”), swing or straight, half-time.
  • Rhythm section: drum feel and kit sound, bass role.
  • Harmony and instruments: chords (“extended jazz chords”), lead instruments, textures.
  • Vocal: register, timbre, delivery, mic distance, doubles and harmonies.
  • Space and mood: room, reverb, tape, warmth; the feeling it should leave.
  • Arrangement arc: dropouts, a sparse bridge, a bigger final chorus, so the track develops instead of looping.

What keeps it working

  • Length: Suno doesn’t publish limits, but the Style field is widely measured at 1,000 characters on current models and 200 on v4 and older. My records use most of that; plenty of good songs use far less.
  • Positive phrasing: put unwanted instruments and vocals in Exclude, not “no X” in the style text.
  • No artist names: Suno generally rejects them in the style text. Describe what you like about the artist instead: era, instruments, vocal, production.
  • Plain musical language: v6 is documented as understanding vocals, instrumentation, structure, mood and references in plain language.
  • Variety slider at 0 on v6 if you want your exact style tags used; anything higher lets Suno vary them.
Where sources disagree

Community guides often recommend four to seven short descriptors and warn that ten or more muddy the result. My songs use long, precise style prompts close to the limit. Both work: short prompts suit exploring, long ones suit a sound you already hear in your head. If a long prompt drifts, cut it to the five descriptors that matter most and raise Style Influence.

Weak

funny cat song, upbeat, catchy, cool beat, good vocals, no drums

Describes the topic and a wish, not a sound. “No drums” belongs in Exclude.

Strong

Late-70s Trinidad calypso with light soca lift, 112 BPM, straight and bouncy. Live kit with rimshot backbeat and shaker, round melodic bass walking the changes, steel pan lead, nylon guitar strums, warm brass stabs on the turnarounds. Major key, simple I-IV-V with passing chords. Male baritone lead, warm, conversational and completely sincere, close-mic, light vibrato; call-and-response gang vocals on the chorus. Roomy analog mix, tape warmth, gentle plate reverb. Verse 2 drops to bass, pan and voice; bridge strips to guitar; final chorus adds horns and a key-change lift. Ends on a held group "hey!" and a pan flourish.
05 · Lock the structure

Sections first, then meter, then words

Suno writes a melody to the shape you give it. A stable shape gets a confident melody; an uneven one gets a confused melody that rushes or stalls.

Decide the sections

A dependable pop form: intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, final chorus, outro. Write it down before writing lyrics.

Equal line counts

Matching sections get matching line counts, usually 4, 8, 12 or 16. Never let a verse run long because it has more jokes; cut the weaker joke.

A syllable pattern per section

Set it explicitly, such as 8/8/8/8 or 7/10/7/10, and keep the chorus even stricter. The second verse should scan exactly like the first.

Section labels in the Lyrics field

Bracketed labels on their own line tell Suno where each section starts. Suno’s help centre doesn’t publish an official tag list, so treat these as community practice that works reliably rather than a guaranteed command set. Tags guide; they don’t override the Style field.

TagUse it forNotes
[Intro] [Outro]Opening and closing sectionsLeave them empty or give a short line; a long intro lyric often gets sung as a verse.
[Verse 1] [Verse 2]Story sectionsNumbering helps keep verses distinct.
[Pre-Chorus]The lift into the hookShorter lines and rising energy.
[Chorus]The hookRepeat the exact same words each time to encourage the same melody.
[Bridge]ContrastSparse, different chords, a new angle on the premise.
[Instrumental] [Guitar Solo]Breaks with no vocalName the instrument for a solo.
[Fade Out] [End]A clean ending[End] after the last section stops songs from wandering on.
(parentheses)Backing vocals and ad-libsWords in parentheses are usually treated as background voices.

Common failure: a typo or missing bracket and the tag gets sung as a lyric. Since v4.5, Suno’s help centre also suggests putting extra song-specific context in the Lyrics box when the Style field holds the genre.

06 · Lyrics that sing

Written to be sung, not read

A lyric can look clever on paper and still generate badly. These are the rules that made the biggest difference across fourteen songs.

The hook comes first

A short repeated phrase of three to five syllables, with a repeated grammatical construction and simple words. Two of mine: “Kitty computer” and “No teef, big life”.

Verses are scenes

Concrete images, physical detail and character-specific logic. Each verse moves the story to a new place instead of restating the first.

Rhyme with rules

End, internal, multisyllabic and slant rhyme. No word rhymed with itself, no lazy pairs like side and outside, and no bent syntax to force a rhyme. Sound beats spelling.

Keep natural stress

Berklee songwriting faculty teach preserving the stress pattern of speech: verbs usually carry stress, compound words stress the first syllable. Stress on the wrong syllable sounds wrong when sung.

Leave room to breathe

No line much denser than its neighbours. Give long held notes open vowels (“high”, “go”, “day”) rather than closed consonants.

Musical endings

End on a refrain, a stripped hook, a harmony stack or an instrumental fade, not a spoken skit or a punchline the model has to deliver.

Realistic length

About 3:15 to 3:30. Guides that measured the Lyrics field put its limit around 5,000 characters, but around 3,000 is a safer working size; past that, Suno tends to rush.

Embody the joke

Comedy lands when the singer believes every word. Never wink, never explain the premise in the chorus.

Two blocks out

Have the chat model return exactly two copyable blocks, style prompt and lyrics, with no commentary, so nothing stray ends up in Suno.

07 · Generate and choose

Using Suno’s controls well

Each generation costs 10 credits and produces two songs. Listen to both all the way through before changing anything: the second take often fixes what the first got wrong.

Models (v6, Sept 2026)

Explore wild, finish steady

  • v6: the flagship for Pro and Premier, described by Suno as reliable and precise.
  • v6-wild: less predictable and more textured; Suno suggests exploring here, then refining the best idea in v6.
  • v6-mini: the fast model, free to everyone.
  • Max Mode: more effort for more credits. Suno recommends it for songs over two minutes, close covers and consistent vocals across a full track.
  • All pre-v6 models were retired on launch. Old songs stay playable, but extends and covers of them now run on v6 and may sound different.
Sliders and toggles

What each one does

  • Weirdness (Safe to Chaos, 50% is normal): lower for clean, commercial results; higher for experiments.
  • Style Influence (Loose to Strong): raise it when your style prompt is precise.
  • Audio Influence: appears only with an audio upload; how closely to follow it.
  • Variety (new in v6): rewrites your style tags for variety. Set it to 0 to keep full control.
  • My Taste: on by default; biases underspecified prompts toward your history. Explicit style text still wins.

Fix a part without losing the whole

Edit a section

v6 changes one section in plain language and keeps the rest, for example a chorus sung by a choir.

Change a lyric

Swap one word or line without regenerating the song. Ideal for a mispronounced word.

Extend and Cover

Extend continues from a point in the track; Cover re-performs a song in a new style. Extend from the latest version to stay consistent.

Voices and Personas

Keep a singer consistent across songs. Voices (v5.5 onward) need identity verification. A persona keeps timbre and delivery, not melody.

Stems and Studio

Split a song into stems (up to 12 with Auto Split), then fix levels, timing or sections in Suno Studio or your own DAW.

Custom models

Pro and Premier can train up to three on six or more of their own tracks. Keep the training set stylistically consistent.

08 · Diagnose and fix

Name the fault before rewriting

The first six rows come from my own workflow; the rest are widely reported community fixes. Change one thing at a time, so you know what worked.

SymptomLikely causeFix
Melody wanders or stallsUneven syllable counts or line lengthsRe-meter the section to a fixed pattern; match the lines to their neighbours.
Weak chorusToo many words, or it summarises the versesSimplify it, shorten the hook, repeat more.
Rushed versesLines too dense, or too many linesCut density or cut lines; never ask the model to sing faster.
Feels childishNovelty production matching novelty lyricsMake the production more serious and genre-true.
Jokes feel forcedJokes explained instead of embodiedGo back to the character’s perspective and concrete detail.
Rhymes sound weakLazy or self rhymes, patched wordsRewrite the rhyme structure of the section, not single words.
Ignores the styleToo many competing descriptorsCut to the five that matter, main genre first, lower Weirdness, raise Style Influence.
Wrong genre winsGenre orderMove the main genre to the front; anchor with an era.
Song ends abruptly or runs onNo ending definedAdd an [Outro] and [End] after the last section.
Tags sung as wordsTypo or missing bracketCheck every label is in square brackets on its own line.
Unwanted instrument appearsNegative phrasing in StyleRemove it from Style and list it in Exclude.
Singer changes mid-songLong track, no fixed voiceUse Max Mode, a Voice or Persona, and a precise vocal description.
One word mispronouncedUnusual spelling or a nameChange that lyric only, or respell it phonetically.
Extension doesn’t matchExtended from an older takeExtend from the most recent version.

The fifteen checks before generating

Run these on every lyric. Ask the chat model to run them and report only failures, then fix those.

  1. Equal line counts

    Matching verses have the same number of lines.

  2. Intended syllable pattern

    Every line hits the pattern set for its section.

  3. Consistent chorus meter

    The chorus scans the same way every time.

  4. Short, memorable hook

    Three to five syllables, easy to sing back.

  5. No self-rhymes or lazy rhymes

    No word rhymed with itself or its compound.

  6. Internal and multisyllabic rhyme

    Present where it comes naturally.

  7. Even density

    No line much denser than its neighbours.

  8. Scans aloud

    Read on the beat, stresses fall naturally.

  9. Distinct verses

    Each verse is a new scene, not a restatement.

  10. Realistic runtime

    Fits about 3:15 to 3:30.

  11. Joke embodied, not explained

    The premise comes out of the character’s logic.

  12. Musical ending

    Refrain, stripped hook, harmony stack or fade.

  13. Real-record production

    The style prompt describes a believable record.

  14. Distinct voices

    If several characters sing, none are interchangeable.

  15. Clean output

    Two copyable blocks, nothing else.

09 · Prompts to copy

Seven prompts for the lyricist

Written for ChatGPT, and they work in Claude, Gemini or a local model. Fill the {{braces}}. Use the first as a project or custom-instructions prompt so every chat follows the method.

Setup · 1

The songwriter system prompt

Paste into a ChatGPT Project’s instructions, a Custom GPT, or a Claude Project.

You are a professional songwriter and producer writing lyrics and style prompts for Suno. Work in this order and do not skip steps:

1. Premise: core concept, point of view, emotional tone, central joke or tension, and 10 concrete scenes or images.
2. Musical world: a real, specific genre and era. The instrumental must sound like a real record; comic lyrics never need comic production.
3. Architecture: list the sections, a line count for each (matching sections equal), and a syllable pattern per section. The chorus meter is the strictest.
4. Hook: 3 to 5 syllables, simple words, repeated construction. The chorus has its own identity and does not summarise the verses.
5. Verses: each one a distinct scene with physical detail and character-specific logic.
6. Rhyme: end, internal, multisyllabic and slant rhyme. Never rhyme a word with itself or its compound, never bend syntax to force a rhyme.
7. Singability: natural word stress, room to breathe, open vowels on held notes.
8. Length: target 3:15 to 3:30. If long, cut the weakest lines and shorten the bridge.
9. Ending: a refrain, stripped hook, harmony stack or instrumental fade. No spoken skit.

Before answering, silently check: equal line counts, syllable pattern, chorus meter, hook length, no lazy or self rhymes, even density, scans aloud, distinct verses, runtime, joke embodied not explained, musical ending, real-record production, distinct character voices.

Output exactly two code blocks and nothing else:
STYLE: under 1,000 characters covering genre and era, BPM and feel, drums, bass, harmony, instruments, vocal (register, timbre, delivery, mic, harmonies), space and mood, and how the arrangement develops. No artist names. No negative instructions.
LYRICS: section labels in square brackets on their own lines, backing vocals in parentheses, [End] after the last section.
Premise · 2

Brainstorm premises

I want a song about {{subject}}. Give me 8 different premises. For each: a one-line concept, who sings it and to whom, the emotional tone, the central tension or joke, and the genre that would make the strongest contrast or fit. Favour premises where the singer takes something absurd completely seriously. Then recommend one and say why in two sentences.
Style · 3

Turn a vibe into a style prompt

Write a Suno style prompt under 1,000 characters for this sound: {{describe the vibe, or name songs or artists you like}}.
Translate any artist names into what they sound like: era, instruments, drum and bass feel, harmony, vocal register and delivery, production and space. Put the main genre first, blend at most three genres, give a BPM, and describe how the arrangement changes across the song. No artist names and no "no X" phrases; list anything to keep out separately as an Exclude line.
Structure · 4

Lock the architecture

Before writing any lyrics for this premise: {{premise}}
Propose the song architecture as a table: section, line count, syllable pattern per line, and what happens in that section (scene, turn, or hook). Matching sections must have equal line counts and the same pattern. Target 3:15 to 3:30 at {{BPM}} BPM. Also propose three hook options of 3 to 5 syllables. Wait for my choice before writing the lyrics.
Audit · 5

Audit a lyric before generating

Audit these lyrics against the target structure {{structure, e.g. verses 8 lines of 8 syllables, chorus 4 lines 5/7/5/7}}.
For every line, give the syllable count and mark stressed syllables in CAPS. Then list only the failures: lines off the pattern, uneven density, self or lazy rhymes, unnatural stress, verses that repeat each other, a hook longer than 5 syllables, a runtime over 3:30, a joke that is explained rather than embodied, a non-musical ending. Suggest a fix for each failure without changing lines that pass.

Diagnose a generated song

I generated this song in Suno. What I hear: {{e.g. the chorus melody is weak, verse 2 is rushed, it sounds like a children's song}}.
Diagnose the cause before suggesting any rewrite. Possible causes: syllable count and line length (confused melody), chorus too wordy (weak chorus), density (rushed verses), production too playful (childish), jokes explained (forced), rhyme structure (weak rhymes), style prompt overloaded (ignores the style). Characters · 7

A song for several singers

Write a song where {{characters}} each sing a verse about {{premise}}. First, give each character a one-line worldview, a vocabulary list of 8 words only they would use, and the kind of joke they make. Then write the lyrics with each verse labelled by singer, e.g. [Verse 1: {{name}}, low gruff voice]. No line should work if moved to another character. Shared chorus, identical each time.
10 · Rights and releasing

What you can do with the songs

This changed a lot in 2025 and 2026 and is still moving. This is a summary of what the sources say as of October 2026, not legal advice; check the current terms before you release anything.

Suno’s plans

  • Free: non-commercial use only, Suno owns the songs, and 7 lifetime downloads. Subscribing later doesn’t license earlier songs.
  • Pro and Premier: commercial use rights; Suno’s help centre says you own the songs. 20 and 60 downloads a month, starting 3 September 2026, with stems counting as part of their song.
  • Streaming and sharing inside Suno stay unlimited on every plan.

Copyright

  • The US Copyright Office’s January 2025 report says purely AI-generated music isn’t protected, and prompts alone don’t give enough human control.
  • Human-written parts are protected. Suno’s help centre: if you wrote the lyrics, you own them, and you can usually register them separately.
  • So writing or substantially editing the lyrics yourself, rather than only prompting, gives you something you can protect.

Streaming platforms

  • Spotify (September 2025) bans unauthorised voice clones and impersonation, filters spam such as mass uploads and 31-second tracks, and supports DDEX credits that disclose AI use. It says disclosure won’t be penalised.
  • Never release a track that imitates a real singer’s voice.

The licensing picture

  • Warner settled with Suno in November 2025; v6 launched in September 2026 trained on licensed Warner, BMG and Believe catalogues, and all earlier models were retired.
  • Suits from Sony, Universal and others were still pending at v6’s launch.
