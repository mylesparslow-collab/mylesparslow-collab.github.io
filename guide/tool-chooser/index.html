Which AI for which job.

There is no single best AI. There is a best fit for each job, and it changes every few months. This guide matches common jobs (writing, coding, research, images, video, music, private work) to the tools worth trying, with current prices, honest tradeoffs and a source for each claim.

Everything was checked against official pages and independent leaderboards on 14 October 2026. Prices and model names move fast; each section says what was verified and what was not. Where a judgement is mine rather than a measurement, the guide says so.

02 · How to choose

Six rules that outlast any product name

The model names in this guide will be stale within months. These habits will not.

  1. Match the tool to the failure you can afford

    A bad brainstorm costs nothing. A wrong citation, a wrong line of code in production or a leaked document can cost a lot. Spend your caution, and your money, where mistakes are expensive.

  2. Try your own task, not the benchmark

    Every leaderboard has blind spots (section 9). Run two or three candidates on a real piece of your own work and compare the results.

  3. Start free, upgrade for one reason

    Free tiers of Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are enough to compare. Pay when you hit a specific limit: a model you need, a feature such as deep research, or a coding agent.

  4. Prefer a few tools you know well

    The prompting guide’s advice carries across tools, and so does your skill. Switching constantly costs more than the small gains from the newest model.

  5. Check the data terms before you paste

    Consumer chats can be used for training by default on several services (section 8). Business plans and local models change that.

  6. Treat brand names and prices as snapshots

    This year alone Sora shut down, NotebookLM was renamed, Cursor changed its plans after an acquisition and Gemini CLI was folded into another product. Recheck before you subscribe.

04 · Coding tools

Two different kinds of tool

The biggest mistake is picking the wrong kind. App builders make a working app from a description and host it; they suit people who do not code. Agents for existing code read your repository, run commands and produce changes for you to review; they assume you can judge what they hand back.

Benchmarks: use with care

OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified on 23 February 2026, saying 59.4% of the hard problems it audited had flawed tests and that every frontier model showed contamination. On Scale’s SWE-Bench Pro V2 (22 September 2026) the top entries were Opus 5 in Claude Code at 98.0, Fable 5.1 in Claude Code at 92.2 and GPT-6 Astra in Codex at 90.2. The harness changes scores as much as the model does, so these numbers rank a model plus its tool together.

OpenAI · Scale Labs

Does AI make developers faster?

In METR’s 2025 trial, 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with AI, while believing they were about 20% faster. A February 2026 follow-up measured roughly 18% faster for the original developers and 4% for new recruits; METR calls it “very weak evidence” and likely understated, because developers refused to work without AI. Google’s DORA 2025 report found 90% adoption and that AI amplifies a team’s existing habits, good or bad.

METR 2025 · DORA 2025

How to pick

  • Never coded: try an app builder’s free tier on a throwaway idea.
  • Code for work: start with the agent that comes with a subscription you already have (Claude Code or Codex).
  • Team on GitHub: Copilot’s issue-to-PR flow.
  • Want to control cost or models: Cline or Aider with your own key.

How to work with them

The habits that matter most (context files, plan first, give it a check it can run, review in a fresh context) are in the coding agents section of the prompting guide.

05 · Research and sources

AI search is useful and unreliable at the same time

For research, the question is less “which tool” than “how will I check it”. The evidence below is why.

Deep research modes

ChatGPT, Claude (Pro and up), Gemini and Perplexity each run a multi-step web investigation and return a cited report. Gemini and ChatGPT let you edit the plan first. OpenAI’s own advice is to use plain search for quick facts and deep research for depth, and to check that citations support the claims.

OpenAI · Claude

Gemini Notebook (was NotebookLM)

Renamed on 16 July 2026. You load your own sources and answers cite passages from them. Limits per source are 500,000 words or 200MB; free notebooks hold 50 sources. It also makes spoken Audio Overviews. Citations may be missing when a source is very short.

Google · limits

Elicit and Consensus

Built for academic papers. Elicit is free, $12 or $49 a month. Consensus can only read full text of open-access papers; paywalled ones are read by title and abstract. A detailed independent review warns its yes/no “meter” is essentially vote-counting and ignores sample size.

Elicit · Aaron Tay review

Citation accuracy, measured

  • Columbia Tow Center (March 2025): across eight AI search tools, over 60% of answers were wrong. Perplexity 37%, Grok 3 94%. Premium tiers were “more confidently wrong”.
  • EBU and BBC (October 2025): 45% of answers from ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity had a significant issue.
  • Both tested older model versions. Treat them as the shape of the risk, not today’s scores.

CJR · EBU/BBC

Made-up references

  • A February 2025 test of eight free chatbots found 39.8% of 400 requested references wrong or fabricated (these were free versions without deep research).
  • A May 2026 Lancet letter auditing about 2.5 million PubMed papers estimated 1 in 277 early-2026 papers cites a non-existent paper, up from 1 in 2,828 in 2023.
  • Vectara’s leaderboard measures a narrow thing, unsupported additions when summarising a document; frontier models scored 7% to 13.6% there in May 2026.

arXiv 2505.18059 · Vectara

Rule of thumb

Use AI to find and summarise; use the source to confirm. Open every link that supports a claim you will repeat, and search the exact title of any paper you cannot find.

06 · Images, video, music and voice

Creative tools, and who owns what you make

Arena and Artificial Analysis run blind preference votes for images and video. They show which output people pick, not which is right for your project.

Images

ChatGPT Images, Nano Banana, then the specialists

  • Leaders on both arenas: OpenAI’s GPT Image 2.5 models are first and second, with Google’s Nano Banana 2.1 the closest rival (Arena, updated 7 October; Artificial Analysis, no date shown). Midjourney, Firefly, Ideogram and FLUX are outside both top tens.
  • Midjourney V8.2 is the default since 24 July. Plans run $10, $30, $60 and $120 a month; companies earning over $1M a year must buy Pro or Mega.
  • Adobe Firefly says its own models are trained on licensed Adobe Stock and public-domain content. The app also offers GPT Image, Nano Banana and FLUX, which that claim does not cover. Indemnification terms were not verified.
  • Ideogram (Free, $20, $60) targets text in logos and posters; version 4.0 has open weights, with commercial use needing a paid licence.

Music

Suno, with eyes open on rights

  • Suno v6 launched around 9 September, built with licensed partners Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe. Free $0 (no downloads, non-commercial), Pro $8, Premier $24.
  • Ownership: on Pro and Premier, Suno assigns its rights in outputs to you, but does not promise any copyright exists. The US Copyright Office says prompts alone do not make you the author; human selection, arrangement and modification can be protected.

07 · Local models

Running AI on your own machine

Local models keep your prompts on your device, cost nothing per question and work offline. The tradeoff is quality: the gap to the best cloud models is real, and the biggest open models are too large for a home computer.

The quality gap, measured

  • Artificial Analysis Index (read 14 October 2026): top proprietary model, Claude Opus 5.5, scores 58; top open-weights model, MiMo-V2.6-Pro, scores 46. The page shows no date.
  • Epoch AI (29 May 2026): open models trail the closed frontier by about four months.
  • Those gaps are for the biggest open models. Epoch puts models that fit one consumer GPU 6 to 12 months behind (August 2025 analysis).

Artificial Analysis · Epoch · Epoch consumer GPU

What actually runs on a normal computer

The strongest open models (Kimi K3 at 2.8 trillion parameters, DeepSeek V4 Pro at 1.6 trillion) need datacentre hardware. Realistic desktop choices are Gemma 4 (now Apache 2.0), Qwen 3.5, 3.6 and 3.8 up to about 27B, OpenAI’s gpt-oss-20b and Mistral Small 24B. Meta’s newest flagship, Muse Spark, is closed; Llama 4’s licence is custom, not open source.

Longer context needs more memory, and mixture-of-experts models need room for all their parameters. On Apple Silicon only part of unified memory is usable by the GPU; the commonly quoted 75% is an unofficial forum figure. Modal’s sizing note.

Ollama

MIT-licensed; a command line and a local API on macOS, Windows and Linux. Good for connecting models to other tools.

github.com/ollama/ollama

LM Studio

A desktop app with a model browser, using llama.cpp and Apple’s MLX engines, plus an OpenAI-compatible local API. Free for home and work use.

lmstudio.ai

Jan, llama.cpp, MLX

Jan is an open-source chat app that runs offline after the download. llama.cpp is the engine under many apps. MLX is Apple’s framework for Apple Silicon.

Jan · mlx-lm

When local is the right call

Documents you cannot send to a third party, repetitive batch jobs where per-query cost matters, and offline work. A small local model with a narrow job, and a person checking the output, is a sound design. For open-ended hard reasoning, a frontier cloud model is still better.

Beyond the toggle

Opting out of training does not make a chat private from the provider, and some chats are retained for days or years. For client files, medical details and credentials, use a business plan with no-training terms, a local model, or leave them out. The prompting guide’s safety section covers prompt injection.

09 · Reading leaderboards

What the rankings can and cannot tell you

Leaderboards are the fastest evidence you can get and they all mislead a little. Know what each one measures.

Arena (formerly LMArena)

Anonymous head-to-head votes from users, turned into ratings. It measures crowd preference, not correctness. Its own FAQ warns that prompts may be shared in public datasets or with providers, so do not paste sensitive data. A 2025 paper, “The Leaderboard Illusion”, found providers could privately test many variants and publish only the best.

Arena FAQ · arXiv 2504.20879

Artificial Analysis and Epoch AI

Aggregate scores from many evaluations, with price and speed alongside. The index version changes over time, so scores from different months are not comparable. Epoch notes that open models may tune harder for benchmarks, which can understate their gap.

Artificial Analysis · Epoch ECI

Task benchmarks

SWE-Bench Pro and Terminal-Bench test coding agents. Vendors report many of the numbers themselves, and the harness matters as much as the model. Top scores often sit within a few points of each other.

A method that holds up

  • Use Arena for “what do people prefer”.
  • Use Artificial Analysis or Epoch for overall capability, price and speed.
  • Use task benchmarks for coding.
  • Then test two or three candidates on your own real work.
