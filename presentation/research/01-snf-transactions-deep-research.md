## Executive Thesis

Skilled nursing facility (SNF) transactions in late 2026 have ceased to be commoditized real-estate-plus-license transfers; they are multi-jurisdictional regulatory endurance contests where enterprise value is created by specialized clinical verticals (provider-led I-SNPs, locked behavioral health conversions under Proposition 1 and SB 43, subacute ventilator units, and bedside dialysis) and destroyed by unmodeled regulatory traps—specifically California's 6-to-14-month AB 1502 pre-approval freeze, the Office of Health Care Affordability's (OHCA) November 2, 2026 private-equity and REIT sale-leaseback regulations, the December 31, 2025 budget termination of California's SNF Workforce and Quality Incentive Program (WQIP), and post-*Harrod*/*Holland* elder-abuse veil-piercing armed with public CMS-855A and HCAI related-party disclosures. While national seniors housing and post-acute M&A hit an all-time record of 871 announced deals in 2025 (with another 614 U.S. deals announced through Q3 2026) and HUD Section 232 permanent healthcare lending more than doubled to $7.15 billion in FY 2026, only counsel capable of integrating licensure, reimbursement, managed care, REIT/HUD finance, labor, tax, public-agency contracting, and False Claims Act defense under one roof can get complex California and multi-state portfolios across the closing line intact. Because Hanson Bridgett LLP sits at the exact geographic and substantive intersection of California's Senior Housing and Care bar, Sacramento's health regulators, Southern California's publicly traded healthcare REIT and mega-operator corridor, and California's counties and public healthcare districts, the firm is positioned to move upstream from episodic CHOW execution to serve as full-lifecycle strategic, regulatory, and structured-finance counsel across the 2026–2030 consolidation wave.

## 1. The 2025–2026 SNF M&A Market, Valuation Reset, and Buyer-Seller Landscape

### 1.1 Record Consolidation Volume Driven by Institutional Portfolio Trades

Across 2025 and the first three quarters of 2026, transaction volume in the U.S. seniors housing and post-acute care sector broke every historical volume benchmark while shifting structurally from isolated single-asset turnarounds toward institutional, multi-facility SNF portfolio trades. According to transaction tracking from LevinPro LTC (published by Irving Levin Associates and *The SeniorCare Investor*), publicly announced seniors housing and care M&A activity reached an all-time record of 871 transactions in full-year 2025, surpassing the prior record of 721 deals set in 2024 by 20.8% ([Irving Levin Associates, Jan. 12, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/12/3217219/0/en/2025-seniors-housing-and-care-m-a-activity-smashes-multiple-records.html)). Skilled nursing facilities accounted for 41% of all announced deals in 2025 (roughly 357 discrete SNF transactions), while seniors housing—comprising independent living, assisted living, memory care, active adult, and continuing care retirement communities (CCRCs)—represented the remaining 59% ([Irving Levin Associates, Jan. 12, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/12/3217219/0/en/2025-seniors-housing-and-care-m-a-activity-smashes-multiple-records.html)). Across rolling four-quarter institutional capital tracking entering 2026, disclosed and estimated sector transaction dollar volume exceeded $24 billion, returning to levels unseen since the mid-2010s REIT acquisition super-cycle ([JLL Seniors Housing & Care Investor Survey and Trend Outlook, 2026](https://www.jll.com/content/dam/jllcom/en/us/documents/reports/research-reports/26-insights-seniors-housing-care-investor-survey-and-trend.pdf)).

Deal velocity accelerated further across the first nine months of 2026 despite persistent long-end Treasury volatility and tightening state regulatory oversight:

### 1.2 Valuation Bifurcation, Cap Rate Spreads, and California's Underwriting Paradox

Pricing in the 2025–2026 SNF market exhibits extreme K-shaped bifurcation between modern, high-acuity Medicare/managed-care transitional facilities and older, multi-bed ward Medicaid facilities burdened by deferred capital maintenance and professional-liability exposure. In *The Senior Care Acquisition Report* (30th Edition, March 2025), Irving Levin Associates reported that average disclosed national SNF sale prices retreated from $97,700 per bed in 2023 to $83,800 per bed in 2024 due to an elevated share of bankruptcy, receivership, and nonprofit-exit liquidations ([Skilled Nursing News, Mar. 2025](https://skillednursingnews.com/2025/03/nursing-home-bed-prices-decline-in-2024/)). Because quality skew became so pronounced, Irving Levin's 31st Edition (March 2026) segmented proprietary SNF pricing into Class A, Class B, and Class C asset tiers rather than relying on a single blended figure. Across public brokerage and valuation benchmarks in 2025–2026, stabilized Class A and top-tier Class B SNFs trade nationally between $90,000 and $160,000+ per licensed bed, whereas older, rural, or survey-challenged Medicaid-heavy facilities trade between $30,000 and $80,000 per bed ([Haven Senior Investments, 2026](https://havenseniorinvestments.com/questions/how-much-does-it-cost-to-buy-a-nursing-home/); [SeniorCRE Valuation Benchmarks, 2026](https://seniorcre.com/valuation-calculator)). Institutional portfolio trades confirm robust pricing at the top of the market, including National Health Investors' $560 million sale of 35 properties (32 SNFs and 3 independent living facilities, averaging roughly $16 million per property) to National HealthCare Corporation in mid-2026 and CareTrust REIT's $400 million acquisition of a Southwest SNF portfolio in September 2026 ([NHI Press Release, Apr. 21, 2026](https://investors.nhireit.com/news/news-details/2026/NHI-Announces-Sale-of-NHC-Portfolio-for-560-Million/default.aspx); [Skilled Nursing News, Sept. 15, 2026](https://skillednursingnews.com/2026/09/caretrust-reit-acquires-nursing-home-portfolio-for-400-million-adds-600-million-pipeline/)).

Capitalization rates reflect a durable 450-to-600-basis-point risk spread over private-pay senior housing. Whereas investor cap rates for Class A private-pay independent and assisted living compressed to 6.2%–7.0% entering 2026, NIC institutional benchmarks anchor Class A going-concern SNF cap rates near 12.0% ([NIC Academy Benchmark, 2026](https://www.nic.org/nic-academy/alternative-asset-class/); [JLL Investor Survey, 2026](https://www.jll.com/content/dam/jllcom/en/us/documents/reports/research-reports/26-insights-seniors-housing-care-investor-survey-and-trend.pdf)). Across the broader market, stabilized going-concern SNF operating cap rates range from 11.0% to 13.5% on normalized EBITDAR/NOI (widening to 15.0%–17.0% on turnaround facilities), while absolute triple-net (NNN) leased PropCo real estate backed by 1.35x–1.50x portfolio EBITDAR coverage trades 150 to 250 basis points tighter, typically at 8.5% to 10.5% lease yields ([JLL Investor Survey, 2026](https://www.jll.com/content/dam/jllcom/en/us/documents/reports/research-reports/26-insights-seniors-housing-care-investor-survey-and-trend.pdf)).

California SNFs present a striking valuation paradox that out-of-state buyers frequently misread:

- **Structural Supply Moat and Superior Occupancy:** While NIC MAP Vision tracked national SNF occupancy recovering to 86.5%–87.0% by early 2026 ([NIC MAP Vision 4Q25 Fundamentals](https://platform.reverecre.com/api/project-file/2a970706-4424-4c95-ba17-95dc87d372c7/4Q25%20NIC-MAP%20Senior%20Housing%20Market%20Fundamentals.pdf)), California freestanding SNFs consistently run at 88.0% to 90.4% median occupancy across CMS and HCAI datasets, with Medi-Cal representing roughly 60%+ of resident days ([SNFRadar California Benchmark, 2026](https://snfradar.com/r/CA)). Although California repealed general Certificate-of-Need (CON) barriers decades ago, stringent seismic, life-safety, and mechanical codes enforced by the Department of Health Care Access and Information (HCAI, formerly OSHPD) push ground-up SNF construction costs past $400,000+ per bed, creating an impenetrable de facto supply freeze in coastal metros (Los Angeles, Orange County, the Bay Area, and San Diego). Consequently, well-maintained metro California SNFs regularly command $120,000 to $160,000+ per bed, and subacute/ventilator or high-Medicare infill facilities trade above $175,000–$225,000+ per bed.
- **The 2026 California Underwriting Trap (Early Termination of WQIP):** In 2026, the single biggest financial mistake in California SNF M&A is underwriting unadjusted 2024 or 2025 trailing-twelve-month (T-12) EBITDA. Under AB 186 (Chapter 46, Statutes of 2022), California created the Skilled Nursing Facility Workforce and Quality Incentive Program (WQIP, `Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 14126.024`), a ~$280 million-per-year Medi-Cal managed care directed-payment bonus pool originally scheduled to run through December 31, 2026. To close a severe state budget shortfall, California's FY 2025–26 Health Budget Trailer Bill (`AB 116`, Chapter 21, Statutes of 2025) cut WQIP short by one full year, terminating WQIP directed payments for all dates of service after December 31, 2025 ([DHCS SNF WQIP Portal](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/skilled-nursing-facility-workforce-and-quality-incentive-program/); [DHCS APL 26-006](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/file/apl-26-006-pdf/)). Throughout 2026, WQIP exists solely in retroactive reconciliation for CY 2025 dates of service. Offering memoranda distributed by sellers in late 2026 routinely blend 2025 WQIP windfalls into historical cash flow; buy-side counsel and financial advisors must strip 100% of historical WQIP revenue out of pro forma run-rate EBITDA.

## 2. Deal Architecture, Master Leases, HUD Section 232 Surge, and Distressed / Chapter 11 Restructuring

### 2.1 OpCo/PropCo Bifurcation, Unitary Master Leases, and Why REITs Avoid RIDEA on SNFs

Nearly every institutional SNF transaction separates real estate ownership from clinical operations across single-purpose, bankruptcy-remote LLCs (SPEs). PropCo holds fee title, building improvements, fixtures, and real-property-tied bed rights, documented under a real estate Purchase and Sale Agreement (PSA). OpCo holds the state health facility license, Medicare and Medicaid provider agreements, payer contracts, resident admission agreements, workforce, and accounts receivable, transferred via an Operations Transfer Agreement (OTA) ([FTI Consulting Analysis](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/no-place-like-home-treatment-snf-leases-under-365)).

Although the REIT Investment Diversification and Empowerment Act of 2008 (`RIDEA`, `26 U.S.C. § 856(d)(8)(B)`) legally permits healthcare REITs to lease "qualified health care properties"—statutorily defined to include skilled nursing facilities—to a Taxable REIT Subsidiary (TRS) managed by an eligible independent contractor (the "SHOP" structure dominant in private-pay assisted living), healthcare REITs almost universally refuse to use RIDEA for SNFs. Placing SNF operations inside a REIT's TRS pulls direct operational volatility, unionized labor inflation, uncapped elder-abuse tort verdicts, and federal False Claims Act (`31 U.S.C. § 3729`) exposure directly onto the REIT's consolidated income statement and balance sheet. When REITs want upside participation in SNF turnarounds in 2025–2026, they instead deploy triple-net (NNN) master leases with revenue-share resets, preferred-equity investments, or convertible mezzanine loans with purchase options.

Modern institutional SNF master leases are drafted around five non-negotiable creditor-protection pillars:

1. **Indivisible Unitary Lease Construction:** Multi-facility portfolios are combined into a single, integrated, indivisible master lease with unallocated or cross-integrated rent and common expiration dates so a Chapter 11 debtor-tenant cannot assume profitable facilities while rejecting losing facilities piecemeal under `11 U.S.C. § 365` ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/no-place-like-home-treatment-snf-leases-under-365)).
2. **EBITDAR Coverage Tiers and Cash Traps:** Initial rent is underwritten at 1.30x to 1.50x portfolio EBITDAR (after a standardized 5% imputed management fee) with 2.0%–3.0% annual escalators. Financial covenant defaults or cash-sweep traps trigger if trailing-twelve-month coverage dips below 1.10x–1.20x.
3. **Standby Letters of Credit Over Cash Deposits:** Landlords demand 3 to 6 months of base rent (up to 12 months on turnaround deals) in evergreen standby Letters of Credit (LOCs) issued by rated money-center banks rather than cash security deposits, because under established bankruptcy law an issuing bank's obligation under an independent LOC is not property of the debtor's estate and can be drawn notwithstanding the automatic stay of `11 U.S.C. § 362`, paired with OpCo HoldCo guarantees and strict subordination of MSO management fees.
4. **Bed-License Anti-Stripping Covenants:** Master leases declare all licensed bed capacity and operating rights appurtenant to the premises to the maximum extent allowed by state law, strictly barring the tenant from banking, delicensing, reducing, or relocating beds and obligating the outgoing tenant to execute zero-dollar OTA transition filings to PropCo's replacement designee upon expiration or default.
5. **Regulatory Cross-Defaults:** Lease Events of Default trigger prior to actual decertification—upon Immediate Jeopardy (IJ) citations that remain uncured past rapid remediation windows, Special Focus Facility (SFF) termination-track placement, Denial of Payment for New Admissions (DPNA), or default under the OpCo's working-capital accounts receivable (AR) line.

### 2.2 The $7.15 Billion HUD Section 232 Refinancing Wave, July 2025 Express Lane, and ORCF Mechanics

Permanent mortgage insurance under **Section 232 pursuant to Section 223(f) of the National Housing Act (`12 U.S.C. § 1715w`)**, administered by HUD's Office of Residential Care Facilities (ORCF), remains the gold standard of the SNF capital stack: non-recourse, fixed-rate, fully amortizing 30-to-35-year debt up to 80% loan-to-value (85% for nonprofits) at a 1.45x minimum debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR), priced in the mid-5% to mid-6% all-in range (including 0.65% annual Mortgage Insurance Premium) across 2025–2026 ([HUD Handbook 4232.1](https://www.hud.gov/hudclips/handbooks/housing-4232-1)).

As SNF occupancy recovered and borrowers rushed to take out 2021–2024 floating-rate SOFR bridge loans, Section 232 volume more than doubled in twenty-four months: rising from $3.15 billion in federal FY 2024 to $5.96 billion across 337 loans in FY 2025 (+89% YoY), and hitting **$7.15 billion across 452 transactions in FY 2026** (ended September 30, 2026) ([VIUM Capital Press Release, Nov. 2026](https://www.viumcapital.com/press/vium-capital-ranks-among-top-three-hud-healthcare-lenders-for-third-consecutive-year)). Illustrating strategic consolidation among HUD LEAN originators (led by Greystone, VIUM Capital, Lument, NewPoint, Berkadia, and Capital Funding Group), Merchants Bancorp's Merchants Capital announced in November 2026 an agreement to acquire the remaining stake in top-three lender VIUM Capital ([Merchants Bancorp Release, Nov. 2026](https://investors.merchantsbancorp.com/news/news-details/2026/Merchants-Capital-to-Acquire-Remaining-Interest-in-VIUM-Capital-Expanding-Its-Seniors-Housing-and-Healthcare-Lending-Platform/default.aspx)).

To clear ORCF underwriting queues, HUD launched the **Section 232/223(f) Express Lane in July 2025** ([HUD Release No. 25-091, July 2025](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-25-091); [Lument Alert, July 2025](https://www.lument.com/hud-fha-launches-express-lane-for-section-232-223f-financing/)). Stabilized, low-risk SNFs with experienced HUD sponsors, clean survey histories, maximum 70% LTV, and minimum **2.0x DSCR on skilled nursing beds** (1.6x on assisted living beds) qualify for fast-track ORCF commitment issuance. Nevertheless, because direct HUD acquisition loans cannot accommodate M&A auction speed or unseasoned turnaround NOI, over 80% of non-REIT SNF acquisitions still execute on 24-to-36-month floating-rate **Bridge-to-HUD** debt (SOFR + 300–475 bps), refinancing into HUD 232/223(f) after 12 months of post-CHOW seasoning.

Whenever a deal involves assuming an existing HUD-insured loan, swapping operators, or adding an AR revolver, counsel must navigate three exacting **HUD Handbook 4232.1 REV-1** workstreams ([HUD ORCF Forms](https://www.hud.gov/hudclips/forms)):

- **Transfer of Physical Assets (TPA, Section III, Chapter 7):** Conveying fee title to a new Borrower SPE or transferring >50% cumulative ownership or a controlling interest triggers a **Full TPA** (`Form HUD-92266-ORCF`), requiring Active Partners Performance System (APPS / 2530) previous-participation clearance under `24 CFR Part 200 Subpart H`, single-asset borrower organizational covenants, execution of `Form HUD-92466-ORCF` (Borrower Regulatory Agreement), formal HUD legal opinions, and written ORCF preliminary approval prior to closing (smaller passive equity shifts may qualify as a Modified TPA).
- **Change of Operator (CHOP) and Management Agent (Section III, Chapter 8):** Replacing the licensed SNF OpCo tenant requires prior ORCF Change of Operator approval, portfolio-wide CMS Five-Star/SFF vetting, APPS/2530 clearance, execution of `Form HUD-92466A-ORCF` (Operator Regulatory Agreement), `Form HUD-92211-ORCF` (HUD Master Lease Addendum, if master-leased), and `Form HUD-92323-ORCF` (Operator Security Agreement perfecting UCC Article 9 liens over operational personalty and receivables). Replacing a third-party manager separately requires `Form HUD-9839-ORCF` and fee subordination.
- **AR Working-Capital Lines, `Form HUD-92322-ORCF`, and the Double Lockbox:** Because Medicare (`42 U.S.C. § 1395g(c)`) and Medicaid (`42 U.S.C. § 1396a(a)(32)`) federal anti-assignment statutes prohibit governmental payers from wiring reimbursements directly into a lender-controlled account, SNF working-capital revolvers require a two-tier **"double lockbox"**: government remittances land in a provider-controlled Government Receivables account subject to revocable daily sweep instructions (**DAISA**—Deposit Account Instructions and Services Agreement) sweeping immediately into a lender-controlled concentration account (**DACA**—Deposit Account Control Agreement). On HUD-insured buildings, the AR lender obtains a first-priority lien on receivables ahead of the FHA Lender *only* by executing HUD's mandatory **Intercreditor Agreement (`Form HUD-92322-ORCF`)** ([HUD Form 92322-ORCF PDF](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/92322-orcf.pdf)), which bars cross-collateralizing non-HUD facilities, imposes standstill notice windows, and establishes a hard **"Cut-Off Time"** upon uncured default after which post-cut-off receivables revert to the FHA Lender so resident care is never starved of cash.

### 2.3 Distressed SNF Insolvencies, California Receiverships, `§ 333` Ombudsmen, and the Double Federal Circuit Split on `§ 363` Sales

Between 2024 and late 2026, out-of-market master leases, uncapped wrongful-death tort dockets, legacy agency-nursing debt, and cyberattacks forced three mega-chains into Chapter 11 alongside acute-care hospital spillover:

- ***In re Petersen Health Care, Inc.* (Case No. 24-10443, Bankr. D. Del., filed Mar. 20, 2024):** Operating 90+ Midwest SNFs and senior living properties, Petersen collapsed after a double cyber-liquidity freeze—an October 2023 Cactus ransomware attack that encrypted billing systems followed immediately by the February 2024 UnitedHealth/Change Healthcare clearinghouse outage ([HIPAA Journal, Mar. 2024](https://www.hipaajournal.com/petersen-health-care-files-for-bankruptcy-following-ransomware-attacks/)). - ***In re LaVie Care Centers, LLC* (Case No. 24-55507-pmb, Bankr. N.D. Ga., filed June 2, 2024):** Carrying legacy Consulate Health Care tort/FCA overhang (following the $255M+ Eleventh Circuit *Ruckh* FCA judgment), $277 million in pandemic agency labor debt, and $1B+ in liabilities across 43 remaining facilities, LaVie confirmed its Chapter 11 Plan in December 2024 (effective June 1, 2025), rejecting underwater leases, transitioning core Omega master-leased portfolios, and channeling tort claims into a General Unsecured Creditors Trust ([ElevenFlo Case Analysis](https://elevenflo.com/cases/lavie-care-centers-llc-2634/analysis)).
- ***In re Genesis Healthcare, Inc.* (Case No. 25-80185-sgj11, Bankr. N.D. Tex., filed July 9, 2025; Plan Confirmed Oct. 2026):** Filing in Dallas with over $1 billion in debt and 200+ pending malpractice and wrongful-death suits ([Epiq Genesis Docket](https://dm.epiq11.com/case/genesis/info/1)), Genesis obtained confirmation in October 2026 of a controversial ~$1 billion transfer of its 175-facility network to West State Street LLC (affiliated with existing insider/investor New Generation Health), leaving wrongful-death claimants and unsecured creditors an estimated ~26% recovery out of a $200M+ liquidation trust and igniting congressional scrutiny over insider Chapter 11 liability washes ([Skilled Nursing News, Oct. 2026](https://skillednursingnews.com/2026/10/genesis-bankruptcy-plan-limits-compensation-for-affected-nursing-home-residents/)).

In every SNF Chapter 11 case, `11 U.S.C. § 333(a)(1)` (triggered because an SNF is a "health care business" under `11 U.S.C. § 101(27A)`) requires appointment of an independent **Patient Care Ombudsman (PCO)** within 30 days of the petition unless waived under Rule 2007.2—which courts virtually never grant for operating nursing homes (in *Genesis*, the U.S. Trustee appointed three veteran PCOs across regional facility clusters). Filing mandatory clinical-quality reports every 60 days under `§ 333(b)`, the PCO holds de facto veto leverage over a `§ 363` sale: an adverse PCO report flagging food/pharmacy cutoffs or RN flight will stall sale confirmation, whereas affirmative PCO testimony validating buyer transition readiness accelerates court approval. Outside bankruptcy in California, CDPH can bypass slow license revocation by petitioning Superior Court to appoint a health-facility **Receiver** under `Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1325–1335` (or installing an administrative **Temporary Manager** under `HSC § 1325.5`) to avert resident "transfer trauma" ([HSC § 1325](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-2/chapter-2/article-8/section-1325/)). Under `HSC §§ 1329–1332`, a California receiver captures facility receivables, can petition the court to reset unconscionable related-party PropCo leases to fair occupancy value, and primes secured lenders with a state super-priority lien if CDPH Citation Penalties Account funds are advanced.

For distressed SNF buyers, **venue dictates whether a Medicare/Medi-Cal provider agreement can be purchased "free and clear" of historical CMS recoupments**, governed by three intersecting doctrinal splits ([American College of Bankruptcy Healthcare Materials](https://www.americancollegeofbankruptcy.com/file.cfm/29/docs/Panel%203B%20Current%20Issues%20in%20Healthcare%20Cases%20Materials.pdf)):

1. **Executory Contract (`§ 365`) vs. Statutory Entitlement (`§ 363(f)`):** CMS contends—and the majority of courts historically held—that a Medicare provider agreement is an executory contract under `11 U.S.C. § 365`, forcing the buyer/estate to cure 100% of pre-petition CMS overpayments and CMPs in cash at closing. Conversely, in *In re Verity Health System of California, Inc.*, 606 B.R. 843 (Bankr. C.D. Cal. 2019), and *In re Center City Healthcare, LLC* (Bankr. D. Del. Sept. 2019), bankruptcy courts ruled that Medicare/Medi-Cal provider numbers are **statutory entitlements** ("property of the estate" under `§ 541`) transferable under `11 U.S.C. § 363(f)` free and clear of tens of millions in historical overpayments and unpaid state Quality Assurance Fees ([Holland & Knight Analysis](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2019/10/provider-agreements-does-two-make-a-trend)).
2. **Equitable Recoupment Split (3rd Circuit vs. 9th, 1st, and D.C. Circuits):** Because equitable *recoupment* is an equitable defense rather than a "claim" or "interest" stripped by `§ 363(f)`, CMS asserts recoupment post-sale. The Third Circuit (*In re University Medical Center*, 973 F.2d 1065 (3d Cir. 1992)) applies a narrow **"integrated transaction" test**, holding that different Medicare cost-report fiscal years are separate transactions and barring prior-year recoupment. By contrast, the **Ninth Circuit** (*In re Gardens Regional Hospital and Medical Center, Inc.*, 975 F.3d 926 (9th Cir. 2020)), joined by the First and D.C. Circuits, applies the broader **"logical relationship" test**, permitting ongoing Medicare/Medi-Cal fee and audit recoupments across fiscal years unless severed by settlement (while barring unrelated `§ 553` setoffs).
3. **`42 U.S.C. § 405(h)` Bankruptcy Jurisdiction Split (9th & 5th Circuits vs. 1st, 3rd, 7th, 8th, & 11th Circuits):** Because `42 U.S.C. § 405(h)` omits `28 U.S.C. § 1334` from its jurisdictional bar due to a codification quirk, the **Ninth Circuit** (*In re Town & Country Home Nursing Services, Inc.*, 963 F.2d 1146 (9th Cir. 1991)) and **Fifth Circuit** (*In re Benjamin*, 932 F.3d 293 (5th Cir. 2019)) hold that **bankruptcy courts retain full `§ 1334` jurisdiction over CMS Medicare disputes without administrative exhaustion**—whereas the Eleventh Circuit (*In re Bayou Shores SNF, LLC*, 828 F.3d 1297 (11th Cir. 2016)) and four sister circuits bar bankruptcy court intervention. That jurisdictional advantage explains why SNF debtors and stalking-horse buyers in California (Ninth Circuit) and Texas (Fifth Circuit, e.g., *Genesis*) wield vastly stronger leverage to compel tripartite CMS/DHCS settlement stipulations capping historical audit liabilities at `§ 363` sale confirmation.

## 3. California's Triple Pre-Closing Gatekeepers and the Multi-State Mini-HSR Patchwork

Between July 2023 and November 2026, California dismantled the traditional "sign-and-close-into-management" transaction model and replaced it with up to three simultaneous, overlapping pre-closing regulatory gatekeepers: CDPH's Centralized Applications Branch (CAB) under AB 1502, the Office of Health Care Affordability (OHCA) under AB 1415 and OHCA's November 2, 2026 Emergency CMIR Regulations, and the California Attorney General under Corporations Code §§ 5914–5930 for nonprofit facilities.

### 3.1 California AB 1502: The Death of Pre-Licensure IMAs/OTAs and Navigating the 6-to-14-Month CAB Queue

Enacted as Chapter 578, Statutes of 2022 (`Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1253.2, 1253.3, 1265, 1267.5`, alongside resident-notice and worker-retention rules in `§§ 1267.61–1267.62`) and operationalized through **CDPH All Facilities Letter (AFL) 23-17** (June 12, 2023), the Skilled Nursing Facility Ownership and Management Reform Act of 2022 (**AB 1502**) outlawed the historic practice of closing an SNF Purchase and Sale Agreement and Operations Transfer Agreement into an unapproved Interim Management Agreement ("license squatting") while CDPH took years to process licensure ([CDPH AFL 23-17](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHCQ/LCP/Pages/AFL-23-17.aspx); [HSC § 1253.3](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-2/chapter-2/article-1/section-1253-3/); [Hanson Bridgett AB 1502 Alert](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/publication/230123-5100-reform-act-2022)).

For all freestanding SNF and Intermediate Care Facility (ICF) Change of Ownership (CHOW) and Change of Management (CHOM) filings submitted on or after July 1, 2023 (exempting acute-care hospital distinct-part SNFs under `HSC § 1253.3(m)`), **`HSC § 1253.3(a)` strictly bars acquiring, operating, establishing, managing, conducting, or maintaining an SNF without prior written approval from CDPH**. Prior written CAB approval is mandatory before consummating:

- Any transaction transferring operation, management, or possessory lease control of an SNF;
- Any transfer, purchase, or sale of a **5% or greater direct or indirect beneficial ownership interest** in the SNF or its licensee (and post-licensure changes to 5%+ upstream beneficial owners or officers under `HSC § 1267.5(a)` require 30 days' advance notice to CDPH); or
- Any interim (IMA) or long-term management agreement transferring operational control or authorizing a manager to make financial decisions, direct aspects of patient care/quality, or hire, fire, or supervise direct-care staff.

Deal lawyers in 2026 must engineer around six unforgiving statutory traps inside `HSC § 1253.3` and AFL 23-17:

1. **Statutory 120-Day Clock vs. Real-World 6-to-14+ Month Backlog (`HSC § 1253.3(b)–(c)`):** Although both the seller's relinquishment notice (`CDPH 9167`) and the buyer's complete CAB packet (`HS 200`, `HS 215A`, `HS 309`, org charts, 90-day unencumbered cash/liquidity verification doubled from 45 days under `HSC § 1265(i)`, and 5-year 50-state compliance disclosures) are due 120 calendar days prior to anticipated closing, CDPH's 120-day review clock (+ one 60-day agency extension) starts **only when CAB formally deems the packet complete**. Pre-completeness intake queues and cross-state agency verifications routinely consume 3 to 6+ months before the statutory clock starts, yielding real-world sign-to-approval timelines of **6 to 14 months** (and up to 15–18 months for multi-tier PE/REIT chains).
2. **The Fatal 45-Day Deficiency Cure Clock (`HSC § 1253.3(c), (f)(1)`):** Once CAB issues an incompleteness/deficiency notice, the applicant has strictly **45 calendar days** to cure every missing item. Missing that 45-day deadline by a single day mandates **automatic statutory denial**. Furthermore, any material change to disclosed facts while pending (such as a new citation at an out-of-state sister facility) must be reported to CAB within **10 calendar days**.
3. **Automatic 50-State Taint Disqualifiers (`HSC § 1253.3(g)`):** CDPH **must automatically disqualify** an applicant if the applicant *or any 5%+ direct or indirect beneficial owner* owned, operated, or managed any SNF, NF, ICF, assisted living, or community care facility **in California or any other U.S. state or territory** during the preceding 5 years that suffered (a) Medicare or Medicaid termination for noncompliance, license suspension/revocation, court receivership, or temporary management; (b) **two or more upheld Class "AA" or Class "A" resident-death citations** within any 24-month window; (c) placement on the HHS-OIG LEIE exclusion list; or (d) any facility-related felony conviction regardless of age. Additionally, owning, operating, or managing **10% or more of all licensed California SNFs** (~118–120 facilities) triggers presumptive disqualification absent an affirmative CDPH resident-safety exception.
4. **90-Day Doorway Posting, Public CalHealthFind Exposure, and 60-Day Worker Retention (`HSC §§ 1267.61–1267.62`):** At least 90 days before finalizing a CHOW/CHOM, the current licensee must deliver written ownership disclosures to all residents/representatives and post them on all entrance and exit doors ($100/day fine for delay), while CDPH posts pending applications online—inviting active opposition letters from CANHR, the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, and SEIU Local 2015. Upon closing, `HSC § 1267.62` requires the incoming operator to issue primary-language written job offers (10+ days to accept) and retain all incumbent staff (except the Administrator and DON) for **60 days** with zero wage/benefit cuts and termination strictly for cause.
5. **The 120-Day Post-Approval Expiration Cliff (`HSC § 1253.3(d)`):** Once CDPH issues written approval, the parties **must close the operational transfer within 120 calendar days** (extendable once by **60 days** to an absolute ceiling of **180 days**). If a slow HUD TPA, OHCA CMIR, or AG review pushes closing past Day 180, **CDPH approval expires**, forcing the parties back to square one. Both buyer and seller must notify CAB within **10 calendar days** of final transfer (or within 10 days of PSA termination).
6. **Gun-Jumping Sanctions and Narrow Emergency Carve-Outs (`HSC § 1253.3(a)(3), (h)–(j)`):** Operating or managing prior to CAB approval triggers Class "B" fines plus **$500/day continuing penalties**, mandatory public "unlawful operation" notices to residents and the Ombudsman, injunctions (`HSC § 1291`), **$10,000 per material violation** penalties post-licensure (`HSC § 1253.3(i)(2)`), up to $20,000/$100,000 Class A/AA exposure if harm occurs, and a strict **statutory bar against operating during any 20-day administrative appeal** (`HSC § 1253.3(h)`). Expedited interim step-in under `HSC § 1253.3(a)(3)` is restricted solely to bona fide emergencies: `HSC § 1325` receiverships, `§ 1325.5` temporary management, or imminent decertification/closure.

**Cross-Threshold Mapping (5% / 10% / 25% / >50%):** When structuring multi-tiered SNF HoldCos in California, counsel must harmonize four distinct percentage tripwires simultaneously: **5%** beneficial ownership triggers CDPH AB 1502 review and HCAI ACFR related-party reporting (**0%** for LLC members on federal Form CMS-855A); **10%** equity, assets, *or debt* triggers OHCA private-equity/hedge-fund filing (`22 CCR § 97435`); **25%** voting/governance or asset transfer triggers general OHCA Material Change Notices and HUD Modified TPAs; and **more than 50%** ownership/control triggers California **Proposition 13 real-property tax reassessment** (`Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code § 64(c)`), HUD Full TPA review, and Medi-Cal CHOW re-screening.

### 3.2 California OHCA: AB 1415 (Jan. 1, 2026), the November 2, 2026 CMIR Emergency Regulations, and SB 351

Created within HCAI under `Cal. Health & Safety Code § 127500 et seq.`, the **Office of Health Care Affordability (OHCA)** reviews healthcare "material change" transactions 90 days prior to closing (`HSC § 127507`). Because `HSC § 127500.2` defines "health care entity" to include any `HSC § 1250` licensed health facility—**expressly including SNFs and ICFs**—California SNF deals already triggered baseline OHCA filing whenever a party met an **entity size test** ($25M+ California revenue/assets; $10M+ California revenue/assets transacting with a $25M+ entity; **or location inside any California primary-care or mental-health Health Professional Shortage Area [HPSA]**, capturing rural and inner-city SNFs regardless of revenue!) AND a **circumstance test** (`22 CCR § 97435(c)`—$25M+ FMV, $10M or 20% revenue increase, 25%+ asset/governance transfer, **or serial roll-up acquisitions of similar California health care services within the preceding 10 years**).

Following Governor Newsom's **September 28, 2024 veto of AB 3129**—which would have granted the California Attorney General unilateral veto power over private-equity healthcare deals ([Governor's AB 3129 Veto Message](https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AB-3129-Veto-Message.pdf))—the Legislature split its private-equity crackdown into two companion statutes that **both took effect on January 1, 2026**:

1. **SB 351 (Cabaldon, Signed Oct. 6, 2025; Adding `Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1190 et seq.`):** Codifies and sharpens California's **Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM)** bar specifically against private equity groups, hedge funds, and PE-backed MSOs—prohibiting interference with physician/dentist clinical judgment, diagnostic/referral decisions, patient-volume quotas, coding, or clinical competency staffing; **voiding physician non-compete and quality-of-care non-disparagement ("gag") clauses**; and empowering the Attorney General to sue for injunctions and fees ([CalPERS Legislative Analysis of SB 351](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/documents/202507-full-day-3-4-2-attachment-sb-351-offsite-agenda-item/download?inline)). SB 351 directly governs PE-backed SNF chains operating captive SNFist medical groups, physiatry/wound-care PCs, or medical-director MSOs.
2. **AB 1415 (Signed Oct. 11, 2025; Effective Jan. 1, 2026) and OHCA's November 2, 2026 Emergency CMIR Regulations (`22 CCR §§ 97431–97442`, Approved by OAL Oct. 2, 2026):** Rewrote California healthcare deal execution across five critical dimensions ([HCAI Official AB 1415 FAQ](https://hcai.ca.gov/affordability/ohca/assess-market-consolidation/mcn-cmir-faqs/ab-1415-frequently-asked-questions-faq/); [McGuireWoods Nov. 3, 2026 Alert](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2026/11/californias-new-healthcare-transaction-regulations-clarify-filing-requirements-for-pe-funds-hedge-funds-and-msos/); [Sidley Oct. 2026 Alert](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/10/california-adopts-regulations-expanding-healthcare-transaction-review)):
   - **Independent "Noticing Entities" (`HSC § 127507(c)(2), (h)`):** Private equity groups, hedge funds, MSOs, newly formed acquisition SPVs, and entities owning/controlling dormant or suspended licenses now owe **independent 90-day Material Change Notice (MCN) filings**. In a sponsored SNF acquisition, the PE fund, HoldCo, buyer SPV, MSO, and target SNF chain may *each* have separate filing duties—and **OHCA's 90-day clock does not start until every required party and noticing entity has filed**.
   - **The 10% Equity/Asset/Debt Trigger (Sweeping in Private Credit) + Zero Control Safe Harbor:** A PE group or hedge fund must file if post-closing it holds **10% or more of a covered health care entity's or MSO's assets, equity, debt, or liabilities**—pulling mezzanine, unitranche, and private-credit healthcare lenders directly into OHCA filings! Worse, **staying under 10% provides zero safe harbor** if the investor acquires *any* governance, officer-appointment, budget/debt-veto, management-fee, or capital-deployment rights (aggregated across club-deal co-investors).
   - **MSO Triggers:** Affiliated MSOs must file when transacting with a $25M+ health care entity, managing two or more providers generating $\ge \$10\text{M}$ in collective California revenue, or participating in a transfer of control.
   - **Explicit Capture of Healthcare REIT Sale-Leasebacks:** Targeting SNF OpCo/PropCo splits head-on, the November 2, 2026 regulations classify **real estate sale-leasebacks and property transfers** as material changes whenever clinical real estate moves to a third party (such as a **Healthcare REIT**) and the surviving provider must lease or pay rent for the facility—authorizing OHCA to initiate a full **Cost and Market Impact Review (CMIR)** if REIT rent or PE debt leverage could weaken operator solvency or patient care.
   - **Invasive Leverage Disclosures, Public Docket Risk (`22 CCR § 97437`), and 8-to-12-Month CMIR Delays:** Filers must turn over pre- and post-deal debt-to-equity and debt-to-EV ratios, full asset-manager healthcare portfolio lists, investment-committee decks, REIT master leases, and executive transaction bonuses (all public unless justified page-by-page under `22 CCR § 97437`). OHCA has 45 days to waive or 60 days to initiate a CMIR (tolled during information requests, plus a new 30-day HCAI Director appeal remand under the Nov. 2026 rules). If OHCA orders a CMIR, the 90-day review (+30-day extension + limitless info-request tolling), comment/final-report cycle, and mandatory **60-day post-Final-Report waiting period** (with referral authority to the AG under antitrust/UCL `Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200` and applicant reimbursement of OHCA's outside consultants) stretches closing to **8 to 12+ months**.

### 3.3 California Attorney General Nonprofit Health Facility Review and Synchronizing the Three Clocks

Whenever a California nonprofit corporation sells, leases, or transfers control of a 24-hour health facility under `HSC § 1250`—**including freestanding nonprofit SNFs and SNF wings inside CCRCs**—to a for-profit buyer (`Cal. Corp. Code §§ 5914–5919`) or another nonprofit (`§§ 5920–5925`), it must obtain **prior written consent of the California Attorney General** ([CA OAG Nonprofit Health Facility Program](https://oag.ca.gov/health-care); [11 CCR § 999.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-999.5)). (If the seller is a CCRC, the deal *also* requires a fourth approval from the **CDSS Continuing Care Contracts Branch** under `HSC § 1770 et seq.`!) Following completeness, the AG has 90 days (+ one 45-day extension = 135 days, typically **5 to 8 months** end-to-end), commissions an applicant-funded independent Health Care Impact Statement, holds an in-county public hearing, and virtually always imposes **5-to-10-year Conditional Consents** requiring continuous 24-hour SNF licensure, historical Medi-Cal bed-day minimums, 3.5 DHPPD staffing compliance, scheduled CapEx, and annual AG certifications enforceable by specific performance (`Corp. Code § 5926`).

**Staggered Clock Choreography:** Because CDPH CAB takes 6–14+ months *and expires 120–180 days after approval*, counsel cannot file all three agencies blindly on Day 1. Best practice in late 2026 is **staggered sequencing**: file CDPH CAB within 15–20 business days of PSA signing; file AG `§ 5914` ~60–90 days into CAB intake triage; and file OHCA MCNs ~4–5 months post-signing (or Day 1 if heavy REIT/PE leverage creates real CMIR risk), calibrating outside dates, ticking fees, and extension options so all clearances land squarely inside CAB's 120-day post-approval closing window.

### 3.4 Multi-State SNF Portfolio Matrix: Post-Steward Laws and Rolling Closings (2024–2026)

Multi-state SNF portfolios anchored by California buyers or REITs must navigate an aggressive 2024–2026 wave of state mini-HSR and investor-liability laws:

- **Massachusetts (`Chapter 343 of the Acts of 2024` / H.5159, Signed Jan. 8, 2025; and `Chapter 197 of the Acts of 2024` LTC Reform):** Enacted after the Steward Health Care collapse ([MA Session Laws Ch. 343](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2024/Chapter343); [MA Session Laws Ch. 197](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2024/Chapter197); [Sidley Analysis](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/01/massachusetts-expands-oversight-of-healthcare-activities-involving-private-equity-reits)), Massachusetts now mandates (1) 60-day HPC/AG Material Change Notices (`M.G.L. c. 6D, § 13`) for PE control shifts and **real estate sale-leasebacks**; (2) audited PE/MSO/REIT lease reporting to CHIA backed by **$25,000/week** non-reporting fines (while banning new REIT main-campus leases outright for acute hospitals); (3) DPH suitability vetting of **all 5%+ SNF owners AND third-party SNF management companies** plus REIT lease disclosures (`105 CMR 150/153`); and, most explosively, (4) **direct treble-damages Massachusetts False Claims Act liability (`M.G.L. c. 12, §§ 5A, 5B(a)(11)`) on any >10% investor, PE group, or hedge fund** that learns of a portfolio SNF's MassHealth/state FCA violation and fails to self-disclose within **60 days**.
- **New York (`PHL Article 45-A` + `PHL § 2801-a` + `PHL § 2828`):** Pairs **30-day NYSDOH/NYAG notice** for non-CON MSO/ancillary deals adding $\ge \$25\text{M}$ in NY revenue (`N.Y. Pub. Health Law §§ 4550–4552`, [NYSDOH Portal](https://www.health.ny.gov/facilities/material_transactions/)) with grueling **12-to-24+ month Public Health and Health Planning Council (PHHPC)** character-and-competence review (10-year multi-state lookback, NYSE-CON public posting, and 90–120-day advance vetting of internal LLC transfers under `PHL § 2801-a`, [NY Senate PHL § 2801-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBH/2801-A)), plus scrutiny of REIT rents and MSO fees against New York's **70% total facility / 40% direct-care minimum spending and 5% profit-cap law (`PHL § 2828`)**.
- **Oregon (`HCMO`, `ORS § 415.500 et seq.`):** Substantive **Oregon Health Authority prior-approval/veto regime** (30-day preliminary + **180-day comprehensive review**) whenever one party has $\ge \$25\text{M}$ and another $\ge \$10\text{M}$ in revenue ([OHA HCMO Portal](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/hpa/hp/pages/health-care-market-oversight.aspx)).
- **Illinois (`740 ILCS 10/7.2a`, Expanded & Made Permanent via `Public Act 104-0782` / HB 5000 in 2026):** Requires **30-day pre-closing AG notice** for Illinois SNFs (`210 ILCS 45/`) with **zero dollar floor** for in-state deals ($10M IL revenue for out-of-state buyers); in 2026, `PA 104-0782` repealed the 2027 sunset and swept in **upstream private equity sponsors and parent HoldCos** ([Reed Smith PA 104-0782 Alert](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/health-industry-washington-watch/102nqfr/illinois-makes-health-care-transaction-notice-law-permanent-expands-private-equi/)).
- **Minnesota (`Minn. Stat. § 145D.41`), Indiana (`Ind. Code § 25-1-8.5` + 2025 `HEA 1666`), Washington (`RCW Ch. 19.390`), and New Mexico (`HB 586` [2025], `NMSA 1978 § 24A-9-1 et seq.`):** Minnesota mandates **120-day AG/MDH pre-closing notice** for nonprofit-to-for-profit nursing home conversions ([MN MDH Portal](https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/mrktoversight/notices.html)); Indiana imposes **90-day AG notice** ($10M asset floor, capturing PE partnerships of any size) plus **annual 5%+ PE/ownership Secretary of State filings** effective Jan. 1, 2026 under `HEA 1666` ([Bass Berry Analysis](https://bassberry.com/news/indiana-legislation-affecting-health-care-transactions-ownership-reporting-requirements/)); Washington requires **60-day AG notice** ([RCW 19.390](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCWArchive/2025/RCW%20%2019%20.390%20%20CHAPTER.htm)); and New Mexico codified permanent **120-day HCA substantive approval** for hospital-affiliated facility/MSO transfers ([NM HCA Portal](https://www.hca.nm.gov/partners-providers/health-facilities-hcbs-agencies/health-care-consolidation-oversight-act)).

Because a 30-facility multi-state portfolio cannot close simultaneously across Texas (fast CHOW), Illinois (30-day AG), California (6–14 month AB 1502 + OHCA), Oregon (180-day HCMO), and New York (18-month PHHPC), market-standard portfolio PSAs/OTAs now utilize **bifurcated "Rolling Closings"**: closing fast-track states at an Initial Closing while isolating California, New York, Oregon, Massachusetts, and Minnesota facilities as **Deferred Closing Facilities** governed by allocated purchase-price holdbacks, separate master-lease joinder mechanics, passive bridge working-capital notes, and state-specific drop-dead dates.

## 4. Federal CMS-855A Ownership Piercing, Medicare/Medi-Cal CHOW Successor Liability, and 2025–2027 Rate and Staffing Whiplash

### 4.1 Form CMS-855A Attachment 1 ("Additional Disclosable Parties") and Entity-Type Cap-Table Asymmetries

Implementing ACA Section 6101(a) (`42 U.S.C. § 1320a-3(c)`), CMS's Final Rule at **88 FR 80141** (effective January 16, 2024; codified at **`42 CFR §§ 424.502, 424.516(g)`** and Medicaid counterparts `42 CFR §§ 455.101, 455.104`) permanently pierced the opacity of multi-layered SNF holding companies ([Federal Register: 88 FR 80141](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/17/2023-25408/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-disclosures-of-ownership-and-additional-disclosable-parties); [42 CFR § 424.516](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/424.516)). Beyond requiring affirmative flags for every **Private Equity Company (PEC)** and **Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)** and broadening SNF "managing employee" to include Medical Directors, Administrators, DONs, and any individual who directly or indirectly advises or supervises any element of facility practices, finances, or operations, the rule requires exhaustive disclosure of **Additional Disclosable Parties (ADPs)** on **Attachment 1 to Form CMS-855A** (reporting owner/manager changes within 30 days and other ADP changes within 90 days).

Under `42 CFR § 424.502` and CMS's repeatedly updated sub-regulatory manual, ***Guidance for SNF Attachment on Form CMS-855A*** (updated **February 24, 2026**, [CMS SNF Attachment Guidance PDF](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/guidance-snf-attachment-855a.pdf)), transactional counsel must draw sharp lines across facility counterparties:

- **Categorically Reportable ADPs:** Every **PropCo landlord, master lessor, sublessor (Master Tenant), and 5%+ real-property owner**; every third-party or sister **MSO / administrative back-office entity** providing HR, payroll, policies/procedures, accounting, budgeting, or daily treasury cash sweeps; **clinical, mock-survey, MDS, and financial consultants**; outside **CPA/cost-report accounting firms**; and outsourced **Revenue-Cycle Management (RCM) / medical billing companies**.
- **Pure EHR/IT Carve-Out vs. Functional Control Triggers for Therapy and Staffing:** CMS's sub-regulatory FAQs explicitly exclude **pure Electronic Health Record (EHR) software vendors** (such as PointClickCare or MatrixCare) and standard IT support vendors—*unless* they also perform outsourced RCM billing, cash management, or operational consulting. Similarly, per-diem nurse registries supplying shift labor under the SNF's own DON are excluded, whereas a **contract rehabilitation vendor (PT/OT/SLP) or turnkey staffing vendor** that supplies a supervisory Director of Rehabilitation, drafts unit operational policies, or provides PDPM utilization consulting crosses into reportable ADP (and individual managing-employee) territory.

Crucially, once an entity is either the SNF applicant or a reportable ADP, `42 CFR § 424.502` (tracking `42 U.S.C. § 1320a-3(c)(5)`) imposes **dramatically different cap-table disclosure floors depending on entity form**:

- **Limited Liability Companies (LLCs) — 0% Floor (100% Disclosure):** Every single direct and indirect LLC member and every manager must be disclosed **regardless of ownership percentage**—meaning a 0.05% passive syndicated doctor or family-office member in an LLC PropCo or LLC MSO must hand over TIN/SSN and ownership data.
- **Limited Partnerships (LPs) — All GPs + 10% LP Floor:** Every general partner (regardless of percentage) and **only limited partners holding a 10% or greater interest** are reportable. Consequently, structuring passive private-equity or real-estate syndicates as **Delaware Limited Partnerships rather than LLCs** lawfully shields passive <10% investors from public CMS-855A cap-table exposure.
- **Corporations — Officers, Directors + 5% Shareholder Floor:** Passive shareholders under 5% are excluded; nonprofits report officers, directors, and governing body members.
- **Trusts — Trustees Only (Beneficiaries Exempt):** Clarifying widespread early confusion, CMS's February 24, 2026 *Guidance for SNF Attachment on Form CMS-855A* confirms that **only trustees (individual or corporate) must be reported** under a trust's organizational structure; trust beneficiaries are *not* reportable solely as beneficiaries unless they independently serve as trustees or managing employees.

**2026 Enforcement Reality:** In December 2025 (formally memorialized in the February 24, 2026 guidance), CMS **indefinitely suspended the standalone January 1, 2026 off-cycle revalidation deadline** due to PECOS portal bottlenecks (though any SNF whose already-filed revalidation receives a MAC development letter still faces a strict 30-day response clock). Do not mistake that pause for transactional relief: **full completion of Form CMS-855A Attachment 1 remains 100% mandatory in 2026 for every CHOW, initial enrollment, reactivation, 5-year cycle revalidation, and reportable Change of Information**, and all reported data feeds CMS's public searchable database on `data.cms.gov` ([CMS SNF All Owners Public Dataset](https://data.cms.gov/provider-characteristics/hospitals-and-other-facilities/skilled-nursing-facility-all-owners)). Every master lease, credit agreement, MSO contract, and RCM agreement drafted in 2026 must contain an enforceable **CMS-855A ADP Disclosure & Cooperation Covenant** compelling counterparties to deliver upstream TINs and cap tables within 10 business days of request.

### 4.2 Medicare and Medi-Cal CHOW Mechanics, `42 CFR § 489.18` Successor Liability, and Unpaid QAF Traps

Under **`42 CFR § 489.18(a)`** ([eCFR: 42 CFR § 489.18](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-489/subpart-A/section-489.18)), an asset purchase, consolidation into a new legal entity, or **lease/OTA operator swap (`§ 489.18(a)(4)`)** triggers a Medicare CHOW, whereas a **100% corporate stock or LLC membership-interest transfer (`§ 489.18(a)(3)`)** where the provider EIN survives is merely a 30-day **Change of Information (COI)** (carrying 100% corporate-law continuity of all historical liabilities with zero option to reject the provider agreement—though still triggering California AB 1502 prior approval under `HSC § 1253.3`!).

In an OTA/asset CHOW, `42 CFR § 489.18(c)–(d)` automatically assigns the seller's Medicare Provider Agreement and CMS Certification Number (CCN) to the buyer *cum onere*. Under settled federal circuit law—***United States v. Vernon Home Health, Inc.*, 21 F.3d 693 (5th Cir. 1994)** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/21/693/623066/)) and ***Deerbrook Pavilion, LLC v. Shalala*, 235 F.3d 1100 (8th Cir. 2000)** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/235/1100/550329/))—an assignee of a Medicare CCN assumes **strict, joint-and-several administrative successor liability** for the seller's pre-closing Medicare cost-report settlements, extrapolated UPIC/RAC/SMRC audit clawbacks (Qlarant in the Western UPIC jurisdiction), Special Focus Facility status, SNF VBP penalties, and unpaid Civil Money Penalties (CMPs), all of which Noridian recoups effortlessly by offsetting 100% of the *buyer's* post-closing Part A remittances regardless of liability disclaimers in the OTA. Moreover, while punitive False Claims Act treble damages require *scienter* (`31 U.S.C. § 3729(b)(1)`), the ACA's **60-Day Overpayment / Reverse False Claims Rule (`42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d)`; `31 U.S.C. § 3729(a)(1)(G)`)** creates an independent trap: if a buyer discovers credible evidence of pre-closing seller overpayments under its assigned CCN and fails to report and refund them within 60 days of quantification, the buyer commits its *own* FCA violation.

Why, then, do >95% of California SNF buyers still accept CCN assignment? Because **rejecting assignment is financial suicide in California**:

1. Rejecting assignment terminates the seller's CCN at closing and forces the buyer to apply *de novo* under `42 CFR § 489.13`, barring **any** Medicare Part A/B billing or retroactive payment until the facility passes an unannounced initial certification survey;
2. Unlike hospitals, hospices, or home health agencies, **CMS recognizes ZERO national accrediting organizations with "deeming authority" for SNFs**—meaning a SNF buyer cannot pay The Joint Commission or ACHC to bypass CDPH; and
3. CMS's State Operations Manual places initial surveys in **Tier 4 (lowest priority)**, producing a **6-to-12+ month total Medicare revenue blackout** in backlogged Los Angeles and Bay Area CDPH district offices.

Even when accepting assignment, buyers must bridge the **Noridian (MAC Jurisdiction E) post-closing Tie-In gap**: although Noridian reviews PECOS CHOW filings in 15–50 days ([Noridian JE SNF Guide](https://med.noridianmedicare.com/web/jea/enrollment/enrollment-for-specialties/snf)), CMS San Francisco will not issue the official **Tie-In Notice** re-pointing EFT payments until CDPH transmits the post-closing `CMS-1539` packet—creating a **3-to-6+ month post-closing lag** requiring a carefully drafted seller-to-buyer DAISA/DACA sweep bridge (mindful of the 12-month timely filing cap at `42 CFR § 424.44`).

On the **California Medi-Cal** side, three facility-specific rules govern every deal:

1. **CDPH CAB Routing, NOT DHCS PAVE:** Freestanding SNFs **do not** process Medi-Cal enrollment or CHOWs in DHCS's online PAVE portal; under `Welf. & Inst. Code § 14043.15(b)`, `DHCS 9098` and `DHCS 6207` packets route exclusively through **CDPH CAB** alongside the AB 1502 filing ([DHCS Provider Enrollment Options](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/provider-enrollment-options/)), supplemented by parallel 90–150-day credentialing/contract assignments across county **CalAIM Medi-Cal Managed Care Plans (MCPs)**.
2. **Mandatory Medicaid Assignment (`42 CFR § 442.14`; `22 CCR § 51000.45`):** Federal Medicaid law mandates automatic assignment of the seller's Medi-Cal provider agreement upon CHOW—preserving retroactive Medi-Cal billing back to the CHOW effective date, while carrying forward all historical DHCS audit liabilities.
3. **The Unpaid Quality Assurance Fee (QAF) Cross-Withholding Trap (`Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1324.20–1324.30`):** Under **`HSC § 1324.22(d)–(f)`** ([Justia: HSC § 1324.22](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-2/chapter-2/article-7-6/section-1324-22/)), if a seller fails to pay monthly per-resident-day QAF assessments, DHCS's Third Party Liability and Recovery Division (TPLRD) withholds unpaid QAF, interest, and penalties directly from post-closing Medi-Cal checks owed to that facility—**and can cross-offset unpaid QAF against other facilities under common ownership/control** while blocking CDPH license renewal! Pre-closing DHCS TPLRD payoff letters and a dedicated QAF escrow are mandatory on every California SNF deal.

### 5.2 California `SB 650` Tunneling Data, Post-*Harrod*/*Holland* Arbitration Collapse, and Elder-Abuse Veil-Piercing

California is ground zero for nuclear SNF tort exposure because public financial transparency collided directly with two landmark California Supreme Court anti-arbitration decisions:

- **`SB 650` / HCAI Annual Consolidated Financial Reports (`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 128734.1`):** Across 2024–2026 reporting cycles on HCAI's SIERA portal ([HCAI SNF ACFR Portal](https://hcai.ca.gov/data/submit-data/financial-reporting/snf-acfr/)), every California SNF chain must file CPA-audited/reviewed consolidated GAAP financials, standalone P&Ls for every **5%+ related-party landlord, MSO, staffing, therapy, or pharmacy affiliate**, and visual org charts (including unrelated management/real-estate vendors paid >$200,000/yr). Analyzing related-party SNF datasets, UCLA/NBER economists Ashvin Gandhi and Andrew Olenski (*"Tunneling and Hidden Profits in Health Care,"* NBER Working Paper No. 32258, [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32258/w32258.pdf)) proved empirically that **roughly two-thirds (>60%) of true nursing home profits are covertly "tunneled" out of operating facilities via above-market related-party real estate leases and MSO fees**.
- **EADACPA Enhanced Remedies and the *Harrod* (2024) + *Holland* (2025) Arbitration Collapse:** Plaintiffs plead SNF suits under the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act (**EADACPA, `Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 15600 et seq.`**), where clear and convincing proof of custodial reckless neglect (`§ 15657`) bypasses MICRA caps (*Delaney v. Baker*) and unlocks **mandatory unilateral plaintiffs' attorneys' fees**, pre-death pain and suffering, and punitive damages (`Civ. Code § 3294`). Between 2024 and 2025, the California Supreme Court shattered SNF arbitration defenses:
  1. ***Harrod v. Prob. Code § 4600 et seq.`) ([Justia: *Harrod*](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2024/s276545.html)). Because neutral state agency-formation rules fall inside FAA Section 2's saving clause (`9 U.S.C. § 2`), *Chamber of Commerce v. Bonta*, 62 F.4th 473 (9th Cir. 2023) does not preempt *Harrod*.
  2. ***Holland v. Silverscreen Healthcare, Inc.*, Cal. Supreme Court No. S285429 (Aug. 14, 2025):** Even where a competent resident *personally* signed a valid arbitration agreement, the Supreme Court held that *Ruiz v. Podolsky* (`CCP § 1295`) binds non-signatory adult children/heirs to arbitrate wrongful-death claims **only if the "primary basis" sounds in professional medical malpractice rather than custodial neglect under EADACPA** ([CA Courts Official Opinion](https://courts.ca.gov/opinion/published-extended-post/2025-08-14/s285429)). Pleading understaffing, falls, dehydration, or pressure ulcers keeps heirs in front of a California jury.
- **The Triangulated Veil-Piercing Playbook and Captive/RRG Insurance Crisis:** Cross-referencing **CMS-855A Attachment 1 ADPs** + **HCAI `SB 650` ACFR profit flows** + **CDPH `AB 1502` org charts** *before filing suit*, plaintiffs routinely name PropCos, REITs, MSOs, and PE sponsors as direct reckless actors, joint venturers, and alter-ego / single-enterprise co-defendants. ### 5.3 Precision PSA and OTA Drafting Mechanics for 2026 Deals

Because standard **Representations & Warranties Insurance (RWI)** excludes Medicare/Medi-Cal billing audits, `42 CFR § 489.18` recoupments, AKS/Stark/FCA exposure, CDPH survey CMPs, open/IBNR elder-abuse torts, and California wage-and-hour claims, market-standard California SNF PSAs and OTAs replace RWI with **3-to-6-year fundamental-rep survival (backed by parent/fund guarantees)** plus a **Four-Tranche Special Escrow Architecture**:

1. **Government Recoupment & Audit Escrow:** 3%–7% of enterprise value (or 1.5x–3x monthly government revenue) held 18–36 months with unilateral 5-day buyer draw rights if Noridian/DHCS offsets pre-closing overpayments.
2. **Medi-Cal Cost Report, QAF & CY 2025 WQIP Closeout Escrow:** Covering open DHCS cost reports, `HSC § 1324.22` unpaid QAF liens, and CY 2025 WQIP overpayment clawbacks.
3. **Survey, Stacked CMP & DPNA Escrow:** Covering latent 3-survey-lookback CMPs (`42 CFR § 488.430(b)`) and lost census during carryover DPNAs.
4. **PL/GL Tail & Actuarial SIR Collateral Escrow:** Requiring Seller out of closing funds to purchase an irrevocable **3-to-6-year prepaid PL/GL Tail Policy** naming Buyer and PropCo as additional insureds *plus* funding a cash/LOC **SIR Collateral Escrow** so RRG excess layers do not collapse if Seller defaults on underlying $250k–$1M SIRs.

Inside the **Operations Transfer Agreement (OTA)**, five California-specific clauses separate clean closings from post-closing litigation:

- **Post-AB 1502 "Observational Readiness" Covenants:** Prohibiting Buyer from hiring/firing staff, directing care, touching bank accounts, or taking fees pre-CAB approval, while granting sandbox EHR mapping, shadow MCP credentialing, conditional job-offer delivery, and survey-MAC walk-away rights.
- **Specific Identification Over FIFO on A/R Waterfalls:** Rejecting seller-friendly First-In, First-Out (FIFO) waterfalls (which misapply Buyer's clean post-closing Medi-Cal/MCP checks to Seller's denied pre-closing claims) in favor of **Specific Identification by Date of Service** on all EOBs/RAs, prorating straddle stays at the 12:00:01 a.m. Effective Time, and limiting FIFO strictly to unreferenced private-pay checks.
- **Resident Trust Fund Fiduciary Reconciliation (`42 CFR § 483.10(f)(10)`; `22 CCR § 72529`):** Joint petty-cash and bank-ledger audit 2–3 days pre-closing, replacement Resident Trust Fund Surety Bond, bilateral Receipt & Assignment of verified cash-backed balances only, and uncapped dollar-for-dollar Seller indemnity.
- **Accrued Vacation Payout (`Cal. Lab. Code § 227.3`) & Cal-WARN Seamless Hiring (`Cal. Lab. Code §§ 1400–1408`):** Requiring Seller to cash out 100% of vested PTO/vacation at closing to avoid `Lab. Code § 203` 30-day waiting-time penalties (or executing voluntary employee rollover elections backed by a tax-loaded purchase-price credit), while binding Buyer to extend comparable job offers to $\ge 90\%$ of staff with zero break in service so no statutory "layoff" occurs under ***MacIsaac v. Waste Management Collection & Recycling, Inc.*, 134 Cal.App.4th 1076 (2005)** ([Proskauer *MacIsaac* Analysis](https://calemploymentlawupdate.proskauer.com/2006/01/employees-who-were-transferred-from-one-employer-to-another-were-not-laid-off-under-cal-warn-act/)) (essential because Cal-WARN covers 75+ FT/PT establishments and lacks the federal WARN sale-of-business statutory carve-out).
- **SEIU Local 2015 Successorship, *Burns*/*Perfectly Clear* Traps, and Card-Check Neutrality:** Auditing Seller's **SEIU Local 2015** CBA for express **Successorship Clauses** (which allow SEIU to enjoin closing in federal arbitration unless Buyer assumes the CBA) and corporate **card-check neutrality MOAs**, while scripting Buyer's pre-closing employee communications so a non-assuming *Burns* successor (*NLRB v. Burns Int'l Security Services*, 406 U.S. 272 (1972)) never inadvertently forfeits its right to set initial terms by becoming a *"Perfectly Clear"* successor.

Valuation multiples expand when SNF operators and their capital partners layer specialized clinical programs, capitated risk vehicles, and tax-optimized physical-plant retrofits onto existing licensed real estate.

### 6.1 Provider-Led I-SNPs, DMHC Restricted Knox-Keene Licensure, and Value-Based Enterprise Safe Harbors

Even as commercial Medicare Advantage (MA) enrollment growth slowed below 1% nationally entering 2026 and legacy insurers pruned unprofitable county footprints, **Institutional Special Needs Plans (I-SNPs, `42 CFR §§ 422.2, 422.52`)**—restricted to beneficiaries requiring an institutional level of care for 90+ days—continued expanding (~3% annual growth), shifting market share decisively toward provider-owned and provider-partnered consortia ([ATI Advisory, *State of the I-SNP Market*](https://atiadvisory.com/resources/state-of-the-isnp-market-2025/)). Empirical claims data from ATI Advisory show why: by embedding dedicated on-site Nurse Practitioners (NPs) and Physician Assistants (PAs) to treat acute respiratory, urinary, and cardiac exacerbations "in place" inside the nursing home, I-SNPs cut emergency-department visits by ~50% and inpatient hospitalizations by ~53% relative to traditional MA plans—generating substantial medical-loss-ratio (MLR) underwriting surpluses.

Rather than capitalizing a standalone insurance carrier from scratch, mid-sized SNF chains form state-level equity LLCs, captive cells, or risk-sharing pods alongside specialized I-SNP enablers such as **AllyAlign Health**, **American Health Plans (TruHealth)**, **Longevity Health Plan**, and **Curana Health**. Structuring an SNF-led I-SNP or clinical risk joint venture in California, however, triggers state regulatory walls that out-of-state deal teams routinely miss:

- **The Knox-Keene Act & DMHC Restricted License Trigger (`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1340 et seq.`; `28 CCR § 1300.49`):** Under Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) general licensure rules ([28 CCR § 1300.49](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/28-CCR-1300.49); [Hooper, Lundy & Bookman Analysis](https://hooperlundy.com/sweeping-changes-to-knox-keene-licensing-requirements-coming/)), any entity accepting **global risk**—prepaid or periodic capitation covering *both* institutional (Part A hospital/SNF) and professional (Part B physician/NP) services, or sharing in global hospital budget surpluses and deficits—must obtain either a full Knox-Keene license or a **Restricted Knox-Keene Health Care Service Plan License**. A Restricted Knox-Keene license allows the provider-owned entity to accept delegated global risk under subcontract from a full-service MA organization without direct public marketing, while subjecting the JV to strict DMHC Tangible Net Equity (TNE) reserves, quarterly solvency filings, and anti-underservice audits.
- **Risk-Bearing Organizations (`HSC § 1375.4`), DOFR Scrutiny, and CPOM (`SB 351`):** Where a CPOM-compliant professional medical corporation accepts capitation *solely* for professional Part B services while institutional hospital risk remains on the MA carrier's paper, it avoids plan licensure but triggers DMHC **Risk-Bearing Organization (RBO)** financial-solvency grading. DMHC scrutinizes every line of the **Division of Financial Responsibility (DOFR)** matrix to ensure unlicensed SNF MSOs are not covertly taking institutional hospital downside through shared-loss clawbacks, while post-January 1, 2026 `SB 351` (`HSC § 1190`) bars any PE-backed MSO from dictating NP/physician utilization or referral quotas.
- **Stark Law & AKS Value-Based Enterprise (VBE) Safe Harbors (`42 CFR § 411.357(aa)`; `42 CFR § 1001.952(ee)–(gg)`):** Aligning SNFs, attending physicians, and hospitals around shared inpatient savings requires meticulous VBE chartering—establishing an accountable VBE governing body, a defined target patient population, and pre-set clinical outcome metrics under Stark's full-risk, meaningful-downside-risk ($\ge 10\%$), or value-based exceptions (`§ 411.357(aa)(1)–(3)`) and OIG's parallel AKS safe harbors (`§ 1001.952(ee)` care coordination requiring a 15% recipient contribution for in-kind digital tech, `§ 1001.952(ff)` substantial downside risk, and `§ 1001.952(gg)` full financial risk).

### 6.2 Behavioral Health Conversions (`Prop 1` + `SB 43`), Subacute Ventilator Units, and Bedside Dialysis-in-SNF

The highest-margin operational play in California senior care between 2026 and 2030 is acquiring obsolete, low-margin 1960s three- and four-bed ward SNFs and converting wings or entire buildings into locked neurobehavioral, psychiatric, subacute ventilator, or bedside-dialysis facilities:

- **The Dual Behavioral Demand Shock — `SB 43` (Jan. 1, 2026 Statewide) + `Proposition 1` ($6.38B BHCIP Grants):** On **January 1, 2026**, the two-year county deferral window expired statewide on **Senate Bill 43** (`Welf. & Inst. Code § 5008(h)`), expanding Lanterman-Petris-Short (LPS) `5150`/`5250` holds and conservatorships to encompass inability to provide for *personal safety* or *necessary medical care* due to severe mental illness or **standalone severe substance use disorder** ([Orange County HCA SB 43 Presentation](https://www.ochealthinfo.com/sites/healthcare/files/2025-12/SB%2043%20Presentation%20-%20All%20Hospital%20%26%20non%20LPS%20Facilities.pdf)). Flooded emergency departments and county public guardians now pay steep **county behavioral supplemental daily "patch" rates ($100 to $350+ per patient day on top of base Medi-Cal)** plus guaranteed bed-hold retainers to locked step-down operators. Simultaneously, **Proposition 1** (March 2024—combining `AB 531`'s **$6.38 billion Behavioral Health Infrastructure Bond** with `SB 326`'s Behavioral Health Services Act overhaul) deployed **$3.3 billion in Bond BHCIP Round 1 ("Launch Ready") awards in 2025** and rolled out **Round 2 ("Unmet Needs") across 2025–2026** ([DHCS BHCIP Portal](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/behavioral-health-continuum-infrastructure-program/)). Crucially, **for-profit and nonprofit SNF owners alike** are eligible for non-dilutive BHCIP capital grants (paired with local match and 30-year behavioral-health / Medi-Cal deed covenants) to acquire and retrofit facilities into either **SNF Special Treatment Programs (STPs, `22 CCR §§ 72443–72475`)** or DHCS-licensed **Mental Health Rehabilitation Centers (MHRCs, `Welf. & Inst. Code § 5675`; `9 CCR § 781.00`)** (navigating the 16-bed federal Medicaid IMD exclusion for ages 21–64 via county realignment/BHSA contracts).
- **Freestanding Adult Subacute & Ventilator Units:** Tracheostomy- and ventilator-dependent Level-B subacute beds command Medi-Cal rates of **$900 to $1,200+ per patient day** (2.5x–3x standard custodial rates), extended through December 31, 2027 under `SB 165` ([DHCS Subacute Portal](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal-resources/freestanding-skilled-nursing-facilities-and-subacute-units/)).
- **Bedside Dialysis-in-SNF (`CDPH AFL 22-32`):** Delivering bedside home hemodialysis eliminates grueling three-times-weekly ambulance transports and wins high-acuity hospital referrals, but California's rules (**`CDPH AFL 22-32`**, alongside federal `CMS QSO-18-24-ESRD`, [CDPH AFL 22-32](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHCQ/LCP/Pages/AFL-22-32.aspx)) are far stricter than Texas or Ohio: (1) dialysis is an **optional service** requiring prior CDPH license modification; (2) bedside drain/reverse-osmosis water modifications require prior **HCAI** plan approval; (3) specialized **Chronic Dialysis Clinic (CDC/ESRD) nurses** must administer hemodialysis under an affiliation agreement, and **outside CDC nursing hours CANNOT count toward the SNF's 3.5 NHPPD floor (`HSC § 1276.65`)**; and (4) CDC-SNF space/equipment leases must satisfy strict federal and California AKS (`Welf. & Inst. Code § 14107.2`; `Bus. & Prof. Code § 650`) fair-market-value standards.

### 6.3 CalAIM MLTSS, Exclusively Aligned "Medi-Medi" D-SNPs, Co-Located Campuses, and Dual Hospice Moratoria

- **CalAIM Carve-In & Exclusively Aligned Enrollment (EAE) D-SNPs:** Following DHCS's statewide **CalAIM Institutional LTC Carve-In** ([DHCS LTC Carve-In](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/calaim-skilled-nursing-facility-long-term-care-carve-in/)), DHCS expanded **Exclusively Aligned Enrollment (EAE) Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans ("Medi-Medi Plans")** across California in 2024–2026 ([DHCS D-SNP Policy Guide](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/file/dhcs-calaim-d-snp-policy-guide-pdf/)). Because one parent insurer now bears combined risk for *both* Medicare Part A/B acute/rehab care *and* Medi-Cal long-term custodial SNF stays, Medi-Medi Plans aggressively narrow networks toward high-star SNFs and co-located **SNF + RCFE (Assisted Living)** campuses utilizing CalAIM **Assisted Living Facility Transitions (ALFT)** and **Assisted Living Waiver (ALW)** slots (noting Community Supports pay transition setup costs but cannot pay ongoing RCFE room and board, [DHCS Community Supports Guide](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/Documents/MCQMD/DHCS-Community-Supports-Policy-Guide.pdf)), alongside capitated **PACE (`Welf. & Inst. Code § 14591`)** preferred-respite JVs.
- **The 2026–2027 Hospice M&A Trap — Sunsetting State Freeze vs. Extended Federal Medicare Freeze:** SNF chains acquiring downstream California hospices in late 2026 face a perilous dual-moratorium puzzle:
  1. **California State Moratorium Sunsets January 1, 2027, Under Tough June 22, 2026 Rules (`22 CCR §§ 74800–74908`):** Under `HSC § 1751.70` (`AB 177`), California's new-license moratorium expires on *"January 1, 2027, or one year from the date emergency regulations are adopted, whichever is sooner."* Because CDPH adopted sweeping hospice emergency regulations on **June 22, 2026** (**`CDPH AFL 26-18` & `AFL 26-20`**—mandating 90-day capital reserves, bounded Geographic Service Areas, and a 2-hour RN in-person response radius, [CDPH AFL 26-20](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHCQ/LCP/Pages/AFL-26-20.aspx)), **California's state new-license moratorium sunsets on January 1, 2027** ([Epstein Becker Green Analysis](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/california-adopts-sweeping-emergency-regulations-for-hospice-providers-what-the-new-rules-require-and-how-to-prepare)). However, **`HSC § 1750(d)` (`AB 2673`) permanently prohibits any hospice CHOW within 5 years of initial license issuance** (and resets the 5-year clock on each CHOW).
  2. **Federal CMS Medicare Freeze Extended Through May 12, 2027 (`42 CFR § 424.570`):** Even when CDPH reopens state applications on January 1, 2027, **a new hospice still cannot bill Medicare** because **in November 2026 CMS extended its nationwide freeze on new Medicare hospice and home health enrollments for six additional months through May 12, 2027** ([CMS Press Release, Nov. 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-extends-modifies-nationwide-freeze-new-medicare-hospice-home-health-enrollment-six-months-combat); [Ropes & Gray Nov. 2026 Alert](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/11/cms-home-health-and-hospice-moratoria-update-six-month-extension))—and Medicare's **36-Month Rule (`42 CFR § 424.550(b)`)** kills CCN assignment on any majority CHOW within 36 months of enrollment or prior CHOW. Consequently, California SNF roll-ups in 2026–2027 can *only* acquire **seasoned (>5-year-old, clean-audit) legacy hospices** via carefully structured equity acquisitions or expand GSA branch locations under existing seasoned Medicare numbers.

### 6.4 Physical-Plant Modernization: `AB 2511` Budget Suspension, C-PACE Limits, and Post-OBBBA Tax Credits

Modernizing California's 1960s–1970s SNF stock under HCAI (OSHPD 2) jurisdiction hinges on three 2025–2026 legal shifts:

1. **`AB 2511` 96-Hour Backup Power Mandate Suspended (`HSC § 1418.22`):** Requiring 96 hours of on-site generator/microgrid backup power sustaining both medical gear and HVAC cooling (`HCAI PIN 74`, estimated at $1M–$3.2M per SNF), AB 2511 had been pushed to January 1, 2026—until **California's enacted 2025–2026 State Budget Health Trailer Bill formally SUSPENDED enforcement of `HSC § 1418.22`** until DHCS certifies that the Legislature has appropriated funds for the required Medi-Cal per-diem rate add-on ([DHCS Trailer Bill Fact Sheet](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/file/dhcs-tbl-suspend-skilled-nursing-facility-backup-power-requirement-fact-sheet-pdf/); [Assembly Budget Floor Report, July 17, 2025](https://abgt.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2025-07/floor-report-of-the-2025-26-budget-july-17-2025.pdf)). PSAs and REIT master leases in late 2026 must include **springing CapEx allocation covenants** governing who funds PIN 74 retrofits once Sacramento appropriates the rate add-on, while maintaining continuous compliance with active federal CMS emergency rules (`42 CFR § 483.73`).
2. **C-PACE Financing vs. HUD's Categorical Ban:** California C-PACE (`AB 811` / `SB 555`, via CSCDA Open PACE / CalFIRST) provides 20–30 year non-recourse financing for HVAC, solar, battery, and seismic upgrades via property-tax assessments. Because tax assessments prime first mortgages, **HUD strictly prohibits C-PACE liens on active Section 232 / 232/223(f) loans** ([C-PACE Desk Guide](https://cpacedesk.com/guides/c-pace-senior-housing-medical/)). C-PACE must therefore be deployed either on conventional/REIT assets (with first-mortgagee consent and NNN lease tax-pass-through alignment) or in **Bridge-to-HUD value-add phases** paid off upon permanent HUD takeout.
3. **Clean Energy & Depreciation Tax Strategy After the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (`P.L. 119-21`, July 4, 2025):** OBBBA reshaped SNF real-estate tax underwriting as of November 2026 ([U.S. DOE 179D](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/179d-energy-efficient-commercial-buildings-tax-deduction); [SEIA OBBBA Analysis](https://seia.org/research-resources/clean-energy-provisions-big-beautiful-bill/); [RSM US OBBBA Analysis](https://www.rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/obbba-tax-clean-energy)):
   - **`IRC § 179D` Terminated June 30, 2026:** Available in late 2026 *only* for grandfathered SNF retrofits that began construction on or before June 30, 2026.
   - **`IRC § 48E` Solar Cliff vs. Surviving Standalone Battery Storage (BESS):** OBBBA imposed a July 4, 2026 construction-start or December 31, 2027 placed-in-service cliff on `§ 48E` solar credits (risky given 6–12 month HCAI electrical plan checks), **but preserved the 30%+ `§ 48E` Investment Tax Credit for standalone Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS, $\ge 5\text{ kWh}$)** into the 2030s (subject to strict Foreign-Influenced / Specified Foreign Entity [SFE] battery supply-chain exclusions), alongside **`IRC § 6417` Direct Pay** for 501(c)(3) nonprofits/public healthcare districts and **`IRC § 6418` cash transferability** (~$0.90–$0.94/$1.00) for for-profit PropCos.
   - **Permanent 100% Bonus Depreciation (`IRC § 168(k)`):** OBBBA permanently restored **100% Year-1 bonus depreciation** for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025—making engineering-backed **cost-segregation studies** (carving out 5-, 7-, and 15-year personalty, nurse-call/oxygen electrical distributions, and Qualified Improvement Property interior rehabs) a massive Year-1 cash-tax shield on taxable PropCo acquisitions.
