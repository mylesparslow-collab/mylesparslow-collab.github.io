## 1. Substantive `EADACPA` Architecture (`WIC § 15600 et seq.`), the January 1, 2026 `SB 447` Sunset, `AB 251` Spoliation Burden-Shifting, and the Living-vs.-Deceased `MICRA` Split

### 1.1 The Dual January 1, 2026 Shock: Expiration of `CCP § 377.34(b)` (`SB 447`) and Enactment of `WIC § 15657.02` (`AB 251`)

Two statutory events on **January 1, 2026** fundamentally altered pleading, demurrer leverage, and discovery motion practice across California healthcare and senior-living litigation:

- **The Sunset of `SB 447` (`Code Civ. Proc. § 377.34(b)`) Restores `EADACPA`'s Survival Monopoly:** Under California's baseline survival statute (`CCP § 377.34(a)`, [CCP § 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.34.)), a decedent's pre-death pain, suffering, and disfigurement die with the victim. Between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2025, `SB 447` (`CCP § 377.34(b)`) temporarily lifted that bar for all civil actions filed prior to January 1, 2026—giving plaintiffs a four-year "safety net" where estates could recover pre-death pain and suffering on ordinary negligence proved by a mere preponderance of the evidence even if the judge struck their elder-abuse claim. In the 2025–2026 session, Senator Laird's **`SB 29`** ([SB 29 History](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB29)) sought to extend `§ 377.34(b)` past 2026, but fierce opposition from `CMA`, `CHA`, and `CAPP` (defending the 2022 `AB 35` `MICRA` compromise) drove `SB 29` onto the inactive file in September 2025, where it died without revival.
  - **2026 Defense Consequence:** While legacy survival complaints **filed on or before December 31, 2025** retain `§ 377.34(b)` eligibility through trial, **every survival action filed on or after January 1, 2026 is once again barred from recovering pre-death pain and suffering unless the plaintiff proves statutory elder/dependent-adult abuse under `Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657(b)` (or reckless/malicious financial abuse under `§ 15657.5(b)`)**! Winning a demurrer or `CCP § 437c(f)` summary adjudication against an `EADACPA` count in a 2026-filed death case once again wipes out *both* pre-death noneconomic damages *and* unilateral attorney's fees (`§ 15657(a)`).
- **The Simultaneous Counterweight — `AB 251` (`Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657.02`, Effective January 1, 2026):** Enacted as Chapter 433, Statutes of 2025 ([AB 251 Chaptered Text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB251/id/3272406/California-2025-AB251-Chaptered.html); analyzed in Hanson Bridgett's August 2026 senior-living alert, [HB AB 251 Alert](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/publications/260814_5051_ab-251-senior-living)), **`WIC § 15657.02`** targets `EADACPA` physical-abuse and neglect suits against **SNFs, RCFEs, and adult community care facilities**—while **expressly carving out general acute care hospitals (`GACHs`), acute psychiatric hospitals, and special hospitals**. Under `§ 15657.02`, if a court imposes a `CCP § 2023.030` discovery sanction for evidence destruction, or a judge or arbitrator makes written findings of intentional spoliation (altering, concealing, or destroying charting, audit logs, or surveillance video), the tribunal may **drop the plaintiff's `WIC § 15657` evidentiary threshold from `"clear and convincing evidence"` down to `"preponderance of the evidence"`** (and weaken `Civ. Code § 3294(b)` corporate-ratification defenses). In 2026+, plaintiffs pair virtually every `EADACPA` survival claim against a SNF or RCFE with a Day-1 `EHR` audit-log / CCTV preservation demand engineered to set up an `AB 251` spoliation motion.

### 1.2 The `Delaney` / `Covenant Care` / `Winn` Boundary—and `WIC § 15610.23(b)`'s Inpatient Dependent-Adult Trap

Distinguishing `EADACPA` physical abuse or reckless neglect (`WIC §§ 15610.57, 15657`) from ordinary professional negligence (`WIC § 15657.2`) determines whether `CCP § 425.13`'s punitive-damages gatekeeper applies (*Covenant Care, Inc. v. Superior Court*, 32 Cal.4th 771 (2004), holding `§ 425.13` **never** applies to `EADACPA` claims) and whether unilateral plaintiff attorney's fees (`§ 15657(a)`) attach:

- **Reckless Custodial Withholding vs. Substandard Medical Care (*Delaney* & *Sababin* vs. *Cochrum*):** Under ***Delaney v. Baker*, 20 Cal.4th 23, 31–35 (1999)**, `"recklessness"` requires a subjective conscious choice of action with knowledge of serious danger to basic custodial needs (hydration, nutrition, fall protection, turning/repositioning, infection control). Repeated failure to follow an established skin-care plan (*Sababin v. Superior Court*, 144 Cal.App.4th 81 (2006)) or knowing maintenance of systemic regulatory understaffing (*Fenimore v. Regents of UC*, 245 Cal.App.4th 1339 (2016)) raises triable `§ 15657` issues, whereas short-term clinical miscommunication or diagnostic error remains `§ 15657.2` malpractice (*Cochrum v. Costa Victoria Healthcare, LLC*, 25 Cal.App.5th 1034 (2018)).
- **Enforcing *Winn*'s "Robust Caretaking or Custodial Relationship" Gate in 2022–2026 (*Oroville*, *Kruthanooch*, & *Frankland*):** Applying ***Winn v. Pioneer Medical Group, Inc.*, 63 Cal.4th 148 (2016)**, California appellate courts have handed healthcare defendants three powerful demurrer and summary-adjudication shields:
  1. **Home Health & Outpatient Wound Clinics (*Oroville Hospital v. Superior Court*, 74 Cal.App.5th 382 (2022)):** Intermittent in-home nursing visits where family manages daily living needs do **not** create *Winn* custody.
  2. **Acute Hospital ERs & Short Diagnostic Stays (*Kruthanooch v. Glendale Adventist Medical Center*, 83 Cal.App.5th 1109 / 82 Cal.App.5th 818 (2022)):** A two-day acute diagnostic hospital stay (where an 86-year-old suffered MRI burns) lacks the ongoing basic-needs custodial relationship required by *Winn*.
  3. **In-SNF Rounding Attending Physicians (*Frankland v. Etehad*, 112 Cal.App.5th 1001 (Aug. 8, 2025), [Frankland Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2025/b338370.html)):** Even when the patient resides 24/7 inside a custodial SNF, an independent attending physician who rounds periodically to enter medical orders is **not** a custodian under *Winn*—sustaining demurrer without leave to amend!
- **The `WIC § 15610.23(b)` Inpatient "Dependent Adult" Trap for Acute & Psychiatric Hospitals:** Hospital counsel often forget **`Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.23(b)`** ([WIC § 15610.23](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=15610.23.)), which defines `"dependent adult"` to include ***"any person between the ages of 18 and 64 years who is admitted as an inpatient to a 24-hour health facility, as defined in Sections 1250, 1250.2, and 1250.3 of the Health and Safety Code."*** Every adult aged 18–64 formally admitted as an inpatient to a general acute-care hospital (`§ 1250(a)`), acute psychiatric hospital (`§ 1250(b)`), SNF (`§ 1250(c)`), `PHF` (`§ 1250.2`), or chemical-dependency hospital (`§ 1250.3`) is *per se* a covered dependent adult—so if a multi-week inpatient stay crosses *Winn*'s custodial-dependency line (as in ***Samantha B. v. Aurora Vista Del Mar, LLC*, 77 Cal.App.5th 85 (2022)**), full `EADACPA` exposure attaches regardless of age!

### 1.3 Does `MICRA` (`Civ. Code § 3333.2`) Cap `EADACPA` Damages in 2026? The Critical Deceased-vs.-Living Split (*Samantha B.* and *Aud v. RRT Enterprises*)

Defense counsel and coverage adjusters routinely miscalculate trial exposure by assuming `MICRA` caps all `EADACPA` noneconomic awards—or none of them. Verified California law as of November 2026 draws a razor-sharp **deceased-versus-living** divide:

1. **DECEASED Victim Survival Claim (`WIC § 15657(b)`) — CAPPED at `$470,000` in 2026:** When the victim dies prior to judgment, pre-death pain and suffering are recoverable solely via **`WIC § 15657(b)`**, whose second sentence explicitly states: *"However, the damages recovered shall not exceed the damages permitted to be recovered pursuant to subdivision (b) of Section 3333.2 of the Civil Code."* Because `§ 15657(b)` incorporates `Civ. Code § 3333.2(b)` directly into `EADACPA`, survival pre-death pain and suffering are **statutorily capped** at `AB 35`'s resolution-year non-death schedule—**`$470,000` in 2026** (stepping up to **`$510,000` on January 1, 2027**, and reaching `$750,000` in 2033)—even against non-`MICRA` `RCFEs`!
2. **LIVING Victim `EADACPA` Claim (*Samantha B.*, 77 Cal.App.5th 85) & Custodial-Neglect Wrongful Death (*Aud v. RRT Enterprises*, July 22, 2026) — 100% UNCAPPED:**
   - A **living** elder or dependent adult recovers noneconomic damages under `Civ. Code § 3333` without invoking `WIC § 15657(b)`'s survival exception. In ***Samantha B. v. Aurora Vista Del Mar, LLC*, 77 Cal.App.5th 85 (2022)** ([Samantha B. Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2022/b302225a.html)), the Second District affirmed `$6.75M` in uncapped noneconomic verdicts for four living psychiatric inpatients, holding `WIC § 15657(b)`'s `§ 3333.2(b)` cap applies *exclusively* to deceased-victim survival claims.
   - Then, on **July 22, 2026**, in ***Aud v. RRT Enterprises, LP*, __ Cal.App.5th __, 2026 WL 2137687 (2d Dist., Div. 7, No. B341254, [Aud Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/b341254.html))**, the Court of Appeal reversed a trial court order that had slashed a `$1.84M` noneconomic verdict against Country Villa Wilshire SNF, extending the Supreme Court's 2025 *Holland* "dual hats" doctrine to hold that **`Civ. Code § 3333.2` (`MICRA`) does NOT cap noneconomic damages arising from breach of custodial caregiving duties** (supervision, fall prevention, call lights, hygiene, nutrition)—leaving both living custodial-neglect claims and non-signatory heirs' `CCP § 377.60` custodial wrongful-death claims **completely uncapped**!

### 1.4 Corporate Managing-Agent Ratification (`Civ. Code § 3294(b)`), Financial Elder Abuse (`WIC § 15657.5`), and 2023–2026 Nuclear Verdicts

- **`Civ. Code § 3294(b)` Managing-Agent Battles (*White v. Ultramar*):** Under `WIC § 15657(c)`, corporate employers face `§ 15657` fees, survival pain and suffering, and punitive damages only upon proof that an *"officer, director, or managing agent"* (*White v. Ultramar, Inc.*, 21 Cal.4th 563 (1999)—exercising substantial discretionary authority determining corporate policy) had advance knowledge of employee unfitness with conscious disregard, or authorized/ratified the recklessness. Plaintiffs establish SNF Administrator, `DON`, and Regional Director of Operations (`RDO`) managing-agent status via `22 CCR §§ 72501/72327` regulatory duties paired with census-bonus plans, agency-staffing freezes, and nurse *"Assignment Despite Objection"* (`ADO`) protests.
- **Weaponized Financial Elder Abuse (`WIC §§ 15610.30, 15657.5, 15657.01`) vs. *Frankland* (2025):** Plaintiffs love pleading `WIC § 15657.5` because it requires only a **preponderance of the evidence**, imposes **no `Civ. Code § 3294(b)` managing-agent hurdle** for fees/compensatory awards, unlocks **prejudgment writs of attachment (`WIC § 15657.01`)** freezing facility bank accounts, and—in private-pay `RCFE` class actions alleging algorithmic understaffing despite tiered "care-point" fees—won Ninth Circuit class-certification affirmance in ***Heredia v. Sunrise Senior Living, LLC*, 2023 WL 4930840 (9th Cir. Aug. 2, 2023)** (*see also Stiner v. Brookdale*). In individual clinical/SNF bodily-injury suits, however, ***Frankland v. Etehad*, 112 Cal.App.5th 1001 (Aug. 2025)** hands defense counsel a decisive demurrer weapon: repackaging substandard clinical care as "wrongful retention of Medicare/Medi-Cal payments" is **not actionable** under `WIC § 15610.30` (*accord Stebley*, 202 Cal.App.4th 522; *Paslay*, 248 Cal.App.4th 639).
- **The 2023–2026 California Nuclear Verdict & Enforcement Ledger:**
  - **March 3, 2026 — `$110.2 Million` Verdict (*Ludlow/Hernandez v. Formation Capital, LLC & Colony Capital, Inc. [DigitalBridge]*, Sac. Super. Ct. No. 34-2020-00275166):** `~$10.2M` compensatory + **`$100M` punitive damages** after a 100-year-old memory-care `RCFE` resident exited a locked door in 38°F weather and died of hypothermia—piercing directly into the **private equity sponsor (`Formation Capital`)** and **publicly traded `REIT` (`Colony Capital / DigitalBridge`)**.
  - **January 13, 2026 — `$15.75 Million` Verdict (*Williams [Estate of Evans] v. Windsor Vallejo Care Center*, Solano Super. Ct. No. FCS055755):** Fatal Stage IV pressure ulcer developed during a one-week SNF rehab stay.
  - **2024 — `$24.65 Million` Judgment (*Donahue v. MBK Senior Living*, Sonoma Super. Ct.):** `$20.58M` verdict (`$17M` punitives) + **`$4.06M` in `WIC § 15657(a)` plaintiff attorney's fees** at a Petaluma memory-care `RCFE`.
  - **January 2023 — `$30.9 Million` Verdict (*Rios v. Pine Creek Care Center / Plum Healthcare Group*, Sac. Super. Ct.):** `$5.9M` compensatory + `$25M` punitives against corporate operator `Plum Healthcare`.

## 2. The 2024–2026 California Healthcare Arbitration Revolution (*Harrod*, *Holland*, *Hohenshelt*, and `SB 365`)

### 2.1 Signatory Authority After *Harrod* (`15 Cal.5th 939`), `Prob. Code § 4459(d)` Financial DPOAs (*Maxwell*), Conservators (*Enmark*), and Tablet E-Signatures (*Bannister*)

Under **`Health & Saf. Code §§ 1599.65, 1599.81`**, `22 CCR § 72516`, and federal **`42 CFR § 483.70(m)`** *(renumbered from `§ 483.70(n)` effective August 8, 2024 via `89 Fed. Reg. 40876`)*, SNF arbitration agreements must sit on a standalone form, clearly state in bold 12-point type (plus `CCP § 1295(a)–(b)` 10-point bold red warnings for medical-malpractice clauses) that signing is **never a condition of admission or continued care**, be explained in a language the resident understands, and grant a **30-calendar-day rescission window** (`CCP § 1295(c)`; `42 CFR § 483.70(m)(3)`).

In ***Harrod v. Country Oaks Partners, LLC*, 15 Cal.5th 939 (Mar. 28, 2024), *cert. denied*, 145 S. Ct. 274 (Oct. 7, 2024)** ([Harrod Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2024/s276545.html)), the California Supreme Court unanimously held that an agent under a **Health Care Power of Attorney (`Prob. Code §§ 4617, 4671, 4683`)** (and, *a fortiori*, any `Prob. Code § 4711` family surrogate or "Responsible Party") **lacks authority to execute an optional pre-dispute arbitration agreement**, disapproving *Garrison* and *Hogan* and surviving `FAA` scrutiny under *Kindred Nursing*. Post-*Harrod* 2024–2026 appellate battles have crystallized four strict execution rules:

1. **Financial / General DPOA (`Prob. Code § 4459(d)` — NOT `§ 4463`) Survives (*Maxwell v. Atria*):** Practitioners must cite the exact Uniform Statutory Form POA section: **`Prob. Code § 4459(d)` ("Claims and litigation" — power to *"submit to alternative dispute resolution"*)**, read with `§ 4450(d)` (*not* `§ 4463`, which covers tax matters!). In ***Maxwell v. Atria Management Co., LLC*, 105 Cal.App.5th 230 (Sept. 19, 2024)** ([Maxwell Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2024/a165214.html)), where a son held a Financial DPOA with claims/litigation authority while a daughter held the Health Care POA, the First District held post-*Harrod* that the son's signature **validly bound the decedent's survival claims to arbitration** (provided any physician incapacity certificate required for a "springing" DPOA under `Prob. Code § 4129` was already signed per *Young v. Horizon West*, 220 Cal.App.4th 1122).
2. **Conservators of the Person Cannot Sign Optional Arbitration (*Enmark*):** In ***Enmark v. KC Community Care, LLC*, 105 Cal.App.5th 463 (Sept. 25, 2024)**, the Second District extended *Harrod* to hold that an `LPS` conservator of the person lacks authority to sign an optional SNF arbitration agreement absent court-conferred estate/litigation powers (`Prob. Code §§ 2451, 2500`).
3. **Ostensible Agency Requires Affirmative *Resident* Conduct (*Valentine*, *Garcia*, *Rogers*, *Hearden*):** Under `Civ. Code §§ 2300, 2317`, an adult child checking "Representative" boxes—or a demented resident's passive silence or non-rescission within 30 days—never creates ostensible authority (*Rogers v. Roseville SH, LLC*, 75 Cal.App.5th 1065 (2022); *Hearden v. Windsor Redding Care Center, LLC*, 103 Cal.App.5th 1010 (July 2024)). Only a clear written/recorded authorization executed personally by a competent resident suffices.
4. **Facility-Tablet DocuSign Signatures Fail Under *Bannister* (`Civ. Code § 1633.9`):** Under *Ruiz v. Moss Bros.*, 232 Cal.App.4th 836 and ***Bannister v. Marinidence Opco, LLC*, 64 Cal.App.5th 541 (2021)** (involving *Marin Post Acute* SNF), when an admissions coordinator opens DocuSign or `PointClickCare` on a **facility-owned iPad** under staff credentials and taps "Adopt and Sign" for an elderly resident without routing to a resident-controlled private email/SMS with independent authentication, motions to compel fail for lack of `Civ. Code § 1633.9` attribution.

### 2.2 *Holland v. Silverscreen Healthcare* (`18 Cal.5th 364`, Aug. 14, 2025) & Overriding `CCP § 1281.2(c)` via `FAA` Procedural Opt-In Clauses

Even when the resident *personally* signed a valid `CCP § 1295` arbitration agreement purporting to bind heirs, **August 14, 2025** brought a unanimous California Supreme Court landmark in ***Holland v. Silverscreen Healthcare, Inc.*, 18 Cal.5th 364 (`No. S285429`, Kruger, J., [Holland Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2025/s285429.html))**:

- **The SNF "Dual Hats" & "Primary Basis" Rule:** Reversing the Second District and approving *Avila v. Southern California Specialty Care*, 20 Cal.App.5th 835 (2018), the Supreme Court held that *Ruiz v. Podolsky*, 50 Cal.4th 838 (2010) binds non-signatory `CCP § 377.60` wrongful-death heirs **only** when the **primary basis** of their wrongful-death claim is `CCP § 1295` **professional medical negligence** (*see Faiaipau v. THC-Orange County, LLC*, 117 Cal.App.5th 292 [Dec. 19, 2025], compelling arbitration of `LTACH` ventilator/tracheostomy monitoring wrongful death)—**not** when the primary basis is **custodial `EADACPA` neglect** (falls, dehydration, malnutrition, hygiene, basic staffing).
- **Defeating `CCP § 1281.2(c)` Consolidation Back into Court (*Victrola 89* Drafting):** After *Holland*, survival claims go to arbitration while heirs' custodial wrongful-death claims stay in Superior Court—inviting plaintiffs to invoke **`CCP § 1281.2(c)`** so the judge refuses arbitration altogether to avoid conflicting rulings (*Hearden*, 103 Cal.App.5th at 1021). Under *Cronus Investments*, 35 Cal.4th 376 and ***Victrola 89, LLC v. Jaman Properties 8 LLC*, 46 Cal.App.5th 337 (2020)**, a generic `"governed by the FAA"` clause fails to displace `§ 1281.2(c)`, **whereas a clause stating *"Enforcement and procedural administration of this Agreement shall be governed exclusively by the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. §§ 1–16), and the parties expressly exclude Code of Civil Procedure § 1281.2(c)"* strips the Superior Court of discretion to deny arbitration**, forcing mandatory enforcement of the survival arbitration under `9 U.S.C. §§ 3–4`!

### 2.3 `CCP §§ 1281.97 / 1281.98` (30-Day Fee-Payment Default) After *Hohenshelt v. Superior Court* (`18 Cal.5th 310`, Aug. 11, 2025) & `SB 365` Supersedeas Practice

Two procedural traps govern post-motion arbitration survival in 2025–2026:

1. **Patients Are `"Consumers"` (*Williams*), Eliminating Strict Forfeiture Only for Good-Faith Mistake (*Hohenshelt*):** Under ***Williams v. West Coast Hospitals, Inc.*, 86 Cal.App.5th 1054 (2022)**, hospital patients and SNF residents **are `"consumers"` under `CCP § 1280(e)`** even when signing optional arbitration agreements—subjecting facilities and insurers to `CCP §§ 1281.97 / 1281.98`'s 30-day invoice-payment rule. On **August 11, 2025**, in ***Hohenshelt v. Superior Court*, 18 Cal.5th 310 (`No. S284498`, 5–2, Liu, J., [Hohenshelt Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2025/s284498.html))**, the California Supreme Court held `§ 1281.98` is **not preempted by the `FAA`**, while disapproving over a dozen "one-day-late = automatic forfeiture" appellate cases to hold that **inadvertent, excusable late payments can be cured under `Civ. Code § 3275` and `CCP § 473(b)`** upon prompt full payment and compensation of plaintiff delay costs (reserving forfeiture and mandatory `§ 1281.99` sanctions for willful, fraudulent, or grossly negligent nonpayment).
2. **No Automatic Stay on Appeal of Arbitration Denials in Superior Court (`SB 365` / `CCP § 1294(a)`):** Unlike federal court—where ***Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski*, 599 U.S. 736 (2023)** mandates an automatic stay during a `9 U.S.C. § 16(a)` appeal—California's **`SB 365` (`CCP § 1294(a)`, eff. Jan. 1, 2024)** abolished automatic `CCP § 916` stays when appealing an order denying arbitration. Verified through November 2026 (*see Kahn v. Coinbase, Inc.*, 115 Cal.App.5th 518 [Oct. 23, 2025]), **no published decision has held `SB 365` preempted by the `FAA`**. Consequently, unless diverse citizenship allows removal to federal court *before* moving to compel, an appealing SNF or hospital must immediately seek a discretionary `CCP § 128(a)(8)` stay and, if denied, file an emergency **`CCP § 923` / `CRC Rule 8.112` Petition for Writ of Supersedeas** in the Court of Appeal before plaintiff blasts full merits and `EHR` discovery!

### 2.4 `CCP § 1295` Red-Ink Formatting Traps, the `FAA` Heir-Binding Double-Bind, *Reigelsperger*, and Knox-Keene `HSC § 1363.1` Reverse Preemption

Hospitals, surgical clinics, and medical groups relying on **`Code Civ. Proc. § 1295`** ([CCP § 1295](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1295.)) routinely lose motions to compel over four mechanical execution errors:

1. **Exact Verbatim Placement & `#FF0000` Red-Ink Rules (`CCP § 1295(a)–(b)`):** The `§ 1295(a)` paragraph must sit as **Article 1** of the agreement, and the verbatim `§ 1295(b)` warning must appear **immediately before the signature line** (with zero intervening HIPAA or billing checkboxes) in **at least 10-point bold red type** (*Rosenfield v. Superior Court*, 143 Cal.App.3d 198 (1983)). Printing forms on a black-and-white clinic laser printer or rendering a tablet form in dark gray/blue CSS destroys `§ 1295` compliance.
2. **The `FAA` Double-Bind on Non-Signatory Wrongful-Death Heirs:** While a provider that botched `§ 1295(b)`'s red-ink rule can invoke `FAA` preemption (*Doctor's Associates v. Casarotto*, 517 U.S. 681) against the **signatory patient**, abandoning strict `CCP § 1295` compliance **forfeits `§ 1295(e)`'s unconscionability safe harbor AND forfeits *Ruiz v. Podolsky*'s statutory bridge binding non-signatory wrongful-death heirs**—because under *Holland* (`18 Cal.5th 364`), only `CCP § 1295` (never the `FAA`) binds non-signatory heirs! *(Conversely, where `§ 1295` was properly executed and the patient dies inside the 30-day `§ 1295(c)` rescission window, the `FAA` preempts California's *Rodriguez* rule under *Baker v. 3. **Covering Future Unrelated Clinic Visits (*Reigelsperger v. Siller*, 40 Cal.4th 574 (2007)):** Including express *"medical services rendered now or in the future, whether for the current condition or any unrelated future condition"* language in Article 1 binds returning clinic patients years later without requiring re-execution at each appointment.
4. **Knox-Keene Health Plans Cannot Use `FAA` Preemption (`CCP § 1295(f)` & `HSC § 1363.1`):** Health plans are governed by stricter **`Health & Saf. Code § 1363.1`** enrollment-form disclosure rules; because the federal **McCarran-Ferguson Act (`15 U.S.C. § 1012(b)`)** reverse-preempts the `FAA` for state insurance regulation, a single `§ 1363.1` formatting defect permanently voids plan arbitration (*Smith v. PacifiCare Behavioral Health*, 93 Cal.App.4th 139 (2001)).

## 3. `HSC § 1430(b)` Per-Violation Class Actions (`AB 849`), `CDPH` Class `AA`/`A` Citation Trials (`AB 323`), `CMS QSO-26-03-NH` Stacked `CMPs`, and Uninsurable `AG`/`DA` `UCL` Suits

### 3.1 Post-`AB 849` `Health & Saf. Code § 1430(b)` Class & Multi-Violation Litigation (`$500` Per Violation)

In ***Jarman v. HCR ManorCare, Inc.*, 10 Cal.5th 375 (2020)**, a 5–2 California Supreme Court affirmed *Nevarrez* and *Lemaire*, capping `HSC § 1430(b)` statutory damages at `$500` per civil lawsuit. Sponsored by Attorney General Bonta and `CANHR`, **`AB 849` (Stats. 2021, ch. 471, [HSC § 1430](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=1430.))** legislatively overruled *Jarman* for all violations occurring **on or after March 1, 2021 (`HSC § 1430(b)(1)(B)`)**, authorizing **"up to five hundred dollars ($500) for each violation"** plus mandatory prevailing-plaintiff attorney's fees, costs, and injunctive relief. Four surgical defenses govern post-`AB 849` exposure in 2026:

- **Strict `SNF` / `ICF` Facility Boundary (`RCFEs` Categorically Excluded):** `HSC § 1430(b)` applies *exclusively* to `CDPH`-licensed `SNFs` (`HSC § 1250(c)`) and `ICFs` (`§ 1250(d)`). **`CDSS`-licensed `RCFEs` (`HSC § 1569 et seq.`) cannot be sued under `§ 1430(b)`**, and `HSC § 1569.269` contains zero private per-violation fee-shifting counterpart.
- **The `$18.25M/Year` `DHPPD` Staffing Class-Action Math (*Shuts* + `HSC § 1276.65`):** Under ***Shuts v. Covenant Holdco LLC*, 208 Cal.App.4th 609 (2012)**, residents may use `§ 1430(b)` to enforce facility-wide statutory nurse-staffing minimums (**`3.5 DHPPD`** including **`2.4 CNA` hours** under `HSC § 1276.65(c)(1)`). Across a 100-bed SNF missing daily `DHPPD` targets without an approved `CDPH` workforce-shortage waiver, class counsel plead `100 residents × 365 days × $500 = $18.25 million` per year!
- **Statutory Mitigation Factors Omit Net Worth (`HSC § 1430(b)(2)(A)–(C)`):** Unlike `UCL` `BPC § 17206(b)`, enacted **`HSC § 1430(b)(2)`** omits defendant net worth, willfulness, and persistence—directing courts to weigh *only* (A) nature and seriousness of each violation, (B) likelihood and severity of the risk of **indignity, discomfort, or pain**, and (C) **efforts made by the facility to prevent each violation or future violations**. Documenting aggressive recruiting, registry outreach, and `CDPH` waiver filings under `(C)` while proving zero resident discomfort under `(B)` drives per-violation awards down from `$500` toward `$1–$25` (and defeats Rule 23 / `CCP § 382` class predominance where `(B)` requires resident-by-resident clinical proof).
- **`FAA` Preemption of `§ 1430(b)`'s Anti-Jury-Waiver Clause (*Valley View*):** While `HSC §§ 1430(b)` and `1599.81(d)` declare arbitration waivers void, ***Valley View Health Care, Inc. v. Chapman*, 992 F.Supp.2d 1016 (E.D. ## 5. Eroding `$1M` Policies, `SIR` Traps (*Forecast Homes*), Foreign `RRG` Federal Preemption (`15 U.S.C. § 3902`), `Ins. Code § 533` vs. `Cumis`, and `CCP §§ 999–999.5` Policy-Limits Demands

### 5.1 Defense-Within-Limits ("Wasting") Policies, `SIR` Exhaustion Traps, and Out-of-State Risk Retention Groups (`RRGs`)

Because admitted occurrence-based carriers largely exited California long-term care underwriting, `SNF` and `RCFE` operators insure primarily through **surplus-lines claims-made "defense-within-limits" (eroding/wasting) `PL/GL` policies** (`$1M` per claim / `$3M` aggregate) or **member-owned Risk Retention Groups (`RRGs`)**:

- **The Eroding-Limit Vise:** Every dollar billed on `PointClickCare` discovery, clinical experts, or a 16-month `CCP § 1294` arbitration appeal directly depletes the `$1M` indemnity limit—leaving `$550,000` to settle a case carrying uncapped `WIC § 15657(a)` plaintiff attorney's fees (`$4.06M` in *Donahue v. MBK*!).
- **The *Forecast Homes* `SIR` Trap (`181 Cal.App.4th 1466`):** Under *Legacy Vulcan Corp. v. Superior Court*, 185 Cal.App.4th 677 (2010) and ***Forecast Homes, Inc. v. Steadfast Ins. Co.*, 181 Cal.App.4th 1466 (2010)**, surplus-lines Self-Insured Retention (`SIR`, `$50k–$250k+`) endorsements routinely state that **only direct out-of-pocket payment by the Named Insured (`OpCo`) satisfies the `SIR`**. If a single-purpose `OpCo` becomes insolvent or enters Chapter 11 (as in *Windsor Terrace* or *Mariner*), an Additional Insured **`REIT` landlord or `MSO` is contractually barred from funding the `SIR` itself to trigger the carrier's defense** unless that anti-funding restriction was deleted at underwriting!
- **Sweeping Federal `LRRA` Preemption for Out-of-State `RRGs` (`15 U.S.C. §§ 3901–3902`):** Operators insured by `RRGs` domiciled in Vermont, Montana, Nevada, or Hawaii face a major surprise when coverage conflicts erupt: under **`15 U.S.C. § 3902(a)(1)`** and Ninth Circuit precedent in ***ALPS v. Ingaldson Fitzgerald, P.C.*, 838 F.3d 976 (9th Cir. 2016)** and ***Allied Professionals Ins. Co. v. Anglesey*, 952 F.3d 1131 (9th Cir. 2020)**, federal law **preempts California's `Civ. Code § 2860` (`Cumis` independent-counsel mandate), anti-arbitration insurance rules, and `Ins. Code § 11580(b)(2)` direct-action statutes**—allowing a foreign `RRG` to enforce defense-fee recoupment clauses and compel out-of-state binding arbitration of coverage disputes!

### 5.2 Why `Ins. Code § 533` Does NOT Bar `WIC § 15657` Recklessness or Plaintiff Fees—and How Reservations of Rights Trigger `Cumis` Counsel (`Civ. Code § 2860`)

Surplus-lines adjusters routinely issue Reservation-of-Rights (`ROR`) letters claiming **`Cal. Ins. Code § 533`** (*"not liable for a loss caused by the wilful act of the insured"*) excludes *all* enhanced `EADACPA` awards. Policyholder coverage counsel (led at Hanson Bridgett by Miles Holden, Linda Klamm, and Alexander Berline) defeat that overreach using three settled California coverage doctrines:

1. **`WIC § 15657` "Recklessness" Is Insurable Despite `Ins. Code § 533` (*Peterson*, *J.C. Penney*, & *Shell Oil*):** While California public policy bars indemnifying `Civ. Code § 3294` punitive damages (*PPG Industries*, 20 Cal.4th 310) and intentional/inherently harmful sexual or physical battery (*J.C. Penney*, 52 Cal.3d 1009, except as to non-ratifying vicariously liable/negligent-supervision employers under *Arenson*, 45 Cal.2d 81), **`Ins. Code § 533` requires subjective intent to injure or knowledge that harm is *substantially certain*—it does NOT bar indemnifying compensatory damages caused by `WIC § 15657` "recklessness" or conscious understaffing** (*Peterson v. Superior Court*, 31 Cal.3d 147, 158–59 (1982); *Shell Oil Co. v. Winterthur Swiss Ins. Co.*, 12 Cal.App.4th 715, 740–43 (1993)). Furthermore, `§ 533` never excuses the broader **duty to defend** mixed complaints (*Downey Venture*, 66 Cal.App.4th 478, outside `Ins. Code § 533.5` public-prosecutor `UCL` suits).
2. **Insurability of `WIC § 15657(a)` Prevailing-Plaintiff Attorney's Fees:** Absent an explicit manuscript policy exclusion or `EADACPA` sublimit, `§ 15657(a)` fee awards resting on a covered recklessness verdict are recoverable either as insured *"damages"* or under **"Supplementary Payments"** as *"costs taxed against the insured"* (`CCP § 1033.5(a)(10)(B)`; *Cutler-Orosi Unified School Dist.*, 31 Cal.App.4th 617; *Golden Eagle v. Cen-Fed*, 148 Cal.App.4th 976).
3. **Triggering Independent `Cumis` Counsel (`Civ. Code § 2860` & *Gafcon*):** Although a reservation of rights *solely* over punitive damages or excess limits does not trigger `Cumis` (`Civ. Code § 2860(b)`), reserving rights under `Ins. Code § 533` or an Abuse/Intentional-Acts exclusion puts the insured's **state of mind** (covered negligence/recklessness vs. uncovered willful malice/fraud) directly at issue on the special verdict form—empowering panel counsel to steer factual findings and triggering a **mandatory right to independent `Cumis` defense counsel** (*Gafcon, Inc. v. Ponsor & Associates*, 98 Cal.App.4th 1388, 1421–23 (2002); subject to `§ 2860(c)` panel-rate caps unless the carrier breached its duty to defend).

### 5.3 Navigating `Code Civ. Proc. §§ 999–999.5` (`SB 1155`) Time-Limited Policy-Limits Demands: Three Scope Traps That Open `$1M` Lids

Enacted via `SB 1155` ([CCP §§ 999–999.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&division=&title=14.&part=2.&chapter=3.2.&article=), verified unamended through November 2026), California's statutory framework for time-limited policy-limits settlement demands (`>= 30 days` email/certified mail or `>= 33 days` regular mail, releasing **all insureds** and satisfying **all Medicare/Medi-Cal liens** under `§ 999.1(b)–(c)`, with insurer clarification requests protected from being treated as counteroffers under `§ 999.3(b)`) contains **three critical scope traps** in healthcare cases:

1. **Pre-Suit / Pre-Arbitration Only (`CCP § 999(b)(2)`):** Applies *only* before a complaint or arbitration demand is filed.
2. **Represented Claimants Only (`CCP § 999.4(b)`):** *Pro se* family demands are exempt from `§ 999.4(a)`'s substantial-compliance bar.
3. **The `CCP § 999.5(a)` Coverage-Part Trap (`Commercial Premises Liability` = IN; `Professional Liability` = OUT!):** `CCP § 999.5(a)` restricts Chapter 3.2 strictly to *"automobile, motor vehicle, homeowner, or **commercial premises liability** insurance policies."* Consequently, **pure Professional Liability (`PL` / Medical Malpractice) coverage parts are EXEMPT from `CCP § 999`**—meaning a plaintiff's 15-day pre-suit `$1M` demand targeting a SNF or hospital's `PL` coverage part is governed by **common-law bad faith** (*Comunale*, 50 Cal.2d 654; *Johansen*, 15 Cal.3d 9; *Pinto v. Farmers Ins. Exchange*, 61 Cal.App.5th 676 [2021]) and **CAN still open the `$1M` policy lid for a `$10M+` excess compensatory/fee verdict** even though it allowed fewer than 30 days! Conversely, premises-hazard/elopement claims implicating blended `CGL` commercial premises coverage *are* governed by `§§ 999–999.5`, requiring insurers to issue a timely written factual/legal basis under `§ 999.3(c)` whenever declining acceptance.

### 6.5 Courtroom Trial Engineering, Special Verdict Architecture (`CACI 3100–3116`), `CCP § 998` Expert-Fee Leverage, Chapter 11 Mass-Tort Stays, and Post-Trial Punitive Caps

When arbitration is unavailable post-*Harrod* or split post-*Holland* and an elder-abuse or catastrophic hospital case marches toward a Superior Court jury, five courtroom and restructuring levers separate controlled resolutions from eight- and nine-figure nuclear verdicts:

- **Engineering Granular Special Verdict Forms (`CACI VF-3100` to `VF-3107` & `Proposition 51` `Civ. Code § 1431.2` Allocation):**
  - General verdict forms or sloppy special verdicts destroy both appellate review and insurance coverage. Trial and `Cumis` counsel must insist under `CCP § 625` on granular special verdict questions that **separate**:
    1. **Threshold Custodial Relationship (`Winn`) & Conduct Classification (`Delaney` / *Holland* / *Aud*):** Requiring the jury to decide separately whether each challenged act or omission constituted **professional medical negligence (`WIC § 15657.2`)** versus **custodial withholding of basic care (`WIC § 15610.57`)**—preserving both `MICRA` periodic-payment rights (`CCP § 667.7`, raised under `AB 35` to a **`$250,000`** future-damages threshold!) and `Civ. Code § 3333.2` noneconomic caps on clinical counts.
    2. **Insured Recklessness vs. Uninsured Malice/Oppression/Fraud (`Ins. Code § 533` Firewall):** Never allow a compound question asking whether the defendant acted with *"recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice."* Splitting **"recklessness"** (`CACI 3113`, **insurable** under *Peterson* and *Shell Oil*) into its own standalone yes/no question separate from **"malice, oppression, or fraud"** (`CACI 3114–3116`, triggering uninsurable `Civ. Code § 3294` punitive damages and `Ins. Code § 533` defenses) protects the policyholder's indemnity and `WIC § 15657(a)` fee coverage even if the jury finds `EADACPA` liability!
    3. **Entity-by-Entity Managing-Agent Ratification (`WIC § 15657(c)` / `Civ. Code § 3294(b)`) & `Prop 51` Comparative Fault (`Civ. Code § 1431.2`):** Requiring separate clear-and-convincing managing-agent findings as to `OpCo`, `MSO`, and any parent entity (*White v. Ultramar*), while allocating `Prop 51` noneconomic fault percentages across unaffiliated hospitals, outside wound-care specialists, rounding physicians (*Frankland*), and equipment vendors (`Civ. Code § 1431.2` applies to negligence and strict-liability counts, and preserving comparative-fault allocation prevents 100% noneconomic shifting onto a 10%-at-fault defendant).

- **Asymmetric `Code Civ. Proc. § 998` Statutory Offers to Compromise in One-Way Fee-Shifting Cases:**
  - Because `WIC § 15657(a)` and `HSC § 1430(b)` grant **one-way** prevailing-plaintiff attorney's fees, defense counsel cannot recover *attorney's fees* under `CCP § 998` if the plaintiff wins `$1` less than a defense `§ 998` offer (*Mangano* / `CCP § 1033.5`). **However**, serving a realistic, well-timed, individually apportioned **`CCP § 998` offer** (explicitly addressing Medicare/Medi-Cal liens and specifying acceptance mechanics without invalid multi-party conditioning across survival estates vs. `CCP § 377.60` wrongful-death heirs!) achieves two decisive results if the plaintiff fails to beat the offer at trial:
    1. **Cuts Off Post-Offer Plaintiff Statutory Attorney's Fees (`CCP § 998(c)(1)`):** Under `CCP § 998(c)(1)`, a plaintiff who rejects a valid defense `§ 998` offer and fails to obtain a more favorable judgment **"shall not recover his or her postoffer costs"**—which under `CCP § 1033.5(a)(10)(B)` **terminates the plaintiff's entitlement to post-offer `WIC § 15657(a)` or `HSC § 1430(b)` statutory attorney's fees** (*Scott Co. v. Blount, Inc.*, 20 Cal.4th 1103 (1999)), eliminating the single biggest driver of plaintiff trial recalcitrance!
    2. **Unlocks Defense Recovery of Post-Offer Expert Witness Fees (`CCP § 998(c)(1)`):** Allows the court to award the facility its own post-offer geriatric, wound-care, nursing, and `EHR`-forensic expert witness fees (`$75,000–$200,000+`) as an offset against the plaintiff's verdict.

- **Chapter 11 Portfolio Reorganizations, Non-Debtor Stays & State-Court Remand Battles (*Windsor Terrace* & *Mariner Health*):**
  - When uninsurable `AG`/`DA` `UCL` suits (`Ins. Code § 533.5`), uncapped post-*Harrod* elder-abuse dockets, and eroded aggregate insurance towers threaten chain-wide insolvency—as in ***In re Windsor Terrace Healthcare, LLC* (`C.D. Cal. Case No. 1:23-bk-11200-VK`, 18+ California SNFs)** and ***In re Mariner Health Central, Inc.***—operators turn to Chapter 11 bankruptcy (`11 U.S.C. § 362(a)` automatic stay). Three hard bankruptcy-litigation rules govern these workouts after the U.S. Supreme Court's June 2024 blockbuster in ***Harrington v. Purdue Pharma L.P.*, 603 U.S. 204 (2024)**:
    1. **Personal-Injury / Wrongful-Death Trial Preservation (`28 U.S.C. §§ 157(b)(2)(B), 157(b)(5), 1411(a)`):** Bankruptcy judges **lack jurisdiction to liquidate or try personal-injury and wrongful-death tort claims (`28 U.S.C. § 157(b)(2)(B)`)** for distribution purposes (though courts may estimate aggregate tort claims for plan-feasibility purposes), and California Superior Court elder-abuse plaintiffs routinely win **equitable remand (`28 U.S.C. § 1452(b)`)** or mandatory/permissive abstention (`28 U.S.C. § 1334(c)`) plus stay relief limited to collecting available insurance proceeds!
    2. **Death of Non-Consensual Third-Party Releases for Non-Debtor `PE` / `MSO` / `REIT` Owners (*Purdue Pharma*, 603 U.S. 204):** Following *Purdue Pharma*, non-bankrupt parent sponsors, `MSOs`, individual owners, and `REIT` landlords **can no longer buy non-consensual Chapter 11 third-party releases** extinguishing state-court `EADACPA` claims against them unless individual tort claimants **affirmatively opt in / consent** or the affiliates file Chapter 11 themselves.
    3. **Police-Power Exemption for `CDPH` & `AG` Injunctive Enforcement (`11 U.S.C. § 362(b)(4)`):** Under `§ 362(b)(4)`, the automatic stay **does not halt** `CDPH` survey/licensure actions or California Attorney General `BPC § 17200` police-power injunctive/monitorship enforcement (*People v. Mariner Health*), requiring consensual integration of compliance monitors and staffing floors directly into the confirmed Chapter 11 reorganization plan.

- **Post-Trial `JNOV` (`CCP § 629`) & Fourteenth Amendment Due-Process Punitive Caps (*Simon v. San Paolo* & *Roby v. McKesson*):**
  - When a runaway jury returns a 10-to-1 punitive multiplier—such as March 2026's `$100M` punitive / `$10.2M` compensatory *Hernandez/Formation Capital* verdict or `$17M` punitive / `$3.58M` compensatory *Donahue* verdict—certified appellate counsel (embedded at trial before the jury is discharged to cure special-verdict ambiguities under *Keener v. Jeld-Wen*) immediately prosecute post-trial `CCP §§ 629` (`JNOV`) and `657` (remittitur/new trial) motions under California's binding constitutional ceiling:
    - Under ***State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Campbell*, 538 U.S. 408, 425 (2003)**, ***Simon v. San Paolo U.S. Holding Co., Inc.*, 35 Cal.4th 1159, 1182 (2005)**, and ***Roby v. McKesson Corp.*, 47 Cal.4th 686, 718–20 (2009)**, whenever compensatory damages are substantial (`$1M–$10M+`) and already contain a large punitive/emotional-distress component, **Fourteenth Amendment federal due process frequently caps the maximum permissible punitive-to-compensatory ratio at `1:1` (or single digits well below `4:1`)**—and under California law (*Simon*, 35 Cal.4th at 1179; *Adams v. Murakami*, 54 Cal.3d 105 (1991)), a punitive award **must be vacated completely** if the plaintiff failed in Phase II to introduce competent evidence of the defendant's **actual net worth / ability to pay** (gross revenue or `SB 650` facility receipts alone without liabilities are legally insufficient under *Baxter v. Peterson*, 150 Cal.App.4th 673 [2007]!).
