## Executive Thesis

California acute, ambulatory, and managed health care law in late 2026 is caught in an unprecedented structural pincer: federal budget reconciliation (**`OBBBA`, `P.L. 119-21`, July 4, 2025**) is stripping roughly $1 trillion out of national Medicaid over ten years—capping State Directed Payments at 100% of Medicare starting in 2028, phasing California's hospital provider-tax safe harbor down from 6.0% to 3.5% starting October 2027, imposing 80-hour monthly work requirements in January 2027, and forcing Sacramento to replace Proposition 35's $2.50 PMPM commercial MCO tax cap with `SB 125`'s flat $8.85 PMPM tax (left standing by the California Supreme Court on November 10, 2026 in *CAHP & CMA v. DHCS*, `S298916`)—at the exact moment California's Office of Health Care Affordability (**OHCA**) enforces its first binding 3.5% statewide and **1.8% High-Cost Hospital** revenue growth caps, `SB 351` (`HSC §§ 1190–1192`) outlaws traditional MSO clinical and payor-contracting controls, and the immovable **January 1, 2030 acute-care seismic deadline (`HSC § 130065`)** confronts 17% of California hospitals with single-building retrofit bills exceeding an entire year of gross revenue. Meanwhile, CMS's CY 2026–2027 phase-out of the Medicare Inpatient-Only list (`922` surgical codes removed; `1,178` codes added to the ASC list), California's January 2026 launch of full independent **`104 NPs`**, and the `$8 billion` **`BH-CONNECT`** § 1115 waiver are driving massive capital and clinical migration out of inpatient towers into ambulatory JVs, FQHCs under the new PMPM Alternative Payment Methodology, and behavioral/community networks. Because Hanson Bridgett LLP uniquely unites a 20-year *Chambers*-ranked California Health Care and Medical Staff bench with **23-year Band-1 national dominance in multi-party Integrated Project Delivery (`IPD`) hospital construction** (architects of UCSF's $4.3B+ Parnassus Heights and Sutter CPMC Van Ness) and premier **California Local Health Care District (`HSC § 32000`) / Public Agency** authority, the firm is built to capture the exact hospital seismic rebuilds, public-district turnarounds, post-`SB 351` MSO restructurings, and managed-care disputes defining 2026–2030.

## 1. The 2025–2026 Hospital & Health System M&A Inflection, Distressed California Workouts (`AB 112`, `Chapter 9` vs. `Chapter 11`), and the January 1, 2030 Seismic Cliff

### 1.1 National V-Shaped M&A Rebound and For-Profit Flight From California Acute Care

Hospital and health system M&A across 2025 and the first three quarters of 2026 traced a sharp V-shaped curve driven directly by federal Medicaid uncertainty and capital-expenditure triage ([Kaufman Hall 2025 M&A in Review PDF](https://www.kaufmanhall.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/KH-Report_M%2BA_2025-Review_0.pdf); [Becker's Q3 2026 M&A Summary](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-transactions-and-valuation/hospital-ma-on-pace-for-busiest-year-since-pandemic-as-megamergers-mount/)):

- **The 2025 Policy Freeze and Record 43.5% Seller Distress:** Nationwide, announced hospital M&A fell to a modern trough of **46 transactions ($18.5 billion transacted revenue)** in full-year 2025—down from 72 deals ($39.7B) in 2024—as boards froze commitments during congressional negotiations over `H.R. 1` (*One Big Beautiful Bill Act*, signed July 4, 2025). Crucially, a **record 43.5% of all 2025 deals (20 of 46)** involved a financially distressed seller (averaging $345M in annual revenue, with only 2.2% of smaller parties holding `'A-'` or higher credit), and **for-profit chains were sellers in 11 deals (23.9%) while acting as buyer in only 1 deal**.
- **The Q4 2025 – Q3 2026 Mega-Merger Surge:** Once `OBBBA`'s post-2027 Medicaid cuts could be modeled, large nonprofit systems raced to build cross-market scale and balance-sheet fortressing: Q1 2026 logged 22 deals ($14.5B; 68% portfolio divestitures, [Kaufman Hall Q1 2026 PDF](https://www.kaufmanhall.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/KH-Report_M%2BA_2026-Q1.pdf)), Q2 2026 added 18 deals ($7.7B), and Q3 2026 added 16 deals ($8.2B)—pushing **Q1–Q3 2026 cumulative activity to 56 transactions, $30.4 billion in transacted revenue, and 8 mega-mergers** (smaller party >$1B revenue), surpassing all of 2025 with a quarter to spare ([Kaufman Hall Q3 2026 Release](https://www.kaufmanhall.com/news/ma-activity-remains-strong-among-large-health-systems-amid-continued-industry-transformation)).

In California, two unmistakable strategic patterns dominated 2024–2026 hospital dealmaking:

1. **Investor-Owned Chains Dumping High-Seismic/Medi-Cal California Hospitals onto UC Campuses, Counties, and Nonprofits:** Ahead of OHCA's April 1, 2024 MCN start date and the 2030 seismic cliff, **Tenet Healthcare** extracted **$1.525 billion** in a single week in late March 2024—closing **UCI Health's $975 million cash purchase** of four Orange/LA County hospitals (858 beds across Fountain Valley, Lakewood, Los Alamitos, and Placentia-Linda, [UCI Health Release](https://www.ucihealth.org/about-us/news/2024/03/uci-health-acquisition)) on March 27, 2024, and **Adventist Health's $550 million purchase** of San Luis Obispo's Sierra Vista and Twin Cities hospitals on March 29, 2024 ([Tenet Release](https://investor.tenethealth.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2024/Tenet-Completes-Sale-of-Six-Hospitals-in-California/default.aspx)). Simultaneously, **UCLA Health** acquired HCA's 260-bed West Hills Hospital (**UCLA West Valley Medical Center**, decompressing >100% occupancy at Westwood/Santa Monica per [Sept. 2026 UC Regents Item J2](https://regents.universityofcalifornia.edu/regmeet/sept26/j2.pdf)), **UCSD Health** acquired Prime's Alvarado Hospital ($200M), and on **April 1, 2025**, **Santa Clara County** closed a **$150 million definitive rescue purchase** (adjusted from $175M) of East San José's **Regional Medical Center** from HCA to immediately restore downgraded Level II trauma, stroke, and STEMI services ([County of Santa Clara](https://health.santaclaracounty.gov/county-santa-clara-and-hca-healthcare-reach-agreement-county-acquire-regional-medical-center)).
2. **California Mega-Nonprofits Expanding Out-of-State to Escape In-State Antitrust & Payer Concentration:** Oakland-based Kaiser Foundation Hospitals' value-based affiliate **Risant Health** followed its 2024 acquisitions of Pennsylvania's **Geisinger Health** (~$7B revenue) and North Carolina's **Cone Health** (~$2.8B revenue) by signing a definitive agreement in **October 2026** to acquire Oklahoma's largest system, **INTEGRIS Health** (~20 hospitals, ~$3B+ revenue, [KGOU Report, Oct. 21, 2026](https://www.kgou.org/health/2026-10-21/integris-health-to-join-kaiser-permanente-backed-risant-health)). Equally historic, Sacramento-based **Sutter Health** (~$16B+ revenue) announced a **$26 billion cross-market merger** with Minnesota's **Allina Health** in March 2026, securing Minnesota Attorney General oversight clearance in **November 2026** for an early-2027 close ([News From The States, Nov. 2026](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/minnesota-ag-allows-allina-health-acquisition-california-based-sutter-health)).

### 1.2 California's `$300M` Distressed Hospital Loan Program (`AB 112`) and `Chapter 9` vs. `Chapter 11` Restructuring Wars

To stop a cascade of community hospital closures, California enacted **`AB 112` / `AB 118` (`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 129380 et seq.`, 2023)**, establishing a **$300 million Distressed Hospital Loan Program (DHLP)** ($292.5M in 0%-interest, 72-month loans with 18-month initial deferrals, administered by HCAI and CHFFA). After bankrupt **Beverly Hospital** forfeited its initial $5M award upon its ~$34M `§ 363` sale to **Adventist Health White Memorial** in September 2023 (reallocating that $5M to Madera), **16 active California hospitals** absorbed the entire **$292.5 million** pool ([CHFFA Resolution No. 2025-01 PDF, Jan. 30, 2025](https://www.treasurer.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/2025-01.pdf)): Madera ($57.0M), Tri-City ($33.2M), Dameron ($29.0M), El Centro ($28.0M), Pioneers Memorial ($28.0M), Kaweah Delta ($20.75M), St. Rose Hayward ($17.65M—saved via Alameda Health System takeover and an April 2026 Stanford Health Care clinical collaboration), MLK Jr. Community LA ($14.0M), Chinese Hospital SF ($10.35M), Hazel Hawkins ($10.0M), San Gorgonio ($9.8M), John C. Fremont ($9.35M), Palo Verde ($8.5M), Watsonville ($8.3M), Ridgecrest ($5.5M), and Sonoma Valley ($3.1M). Because 18-month deferrals began expiring in May 2025, **CHFFA Resolution No. 2025-01** (`HSC § 129384(c)`) created a two-step lifeline: hospitals failing any of three "Stable Financial Ratios" (>60 Days Cash on Hand, >1.5x DSCR, >2.0x Current Ratio) qualified for a **12-month deferral extension in 2025 (Step 1)**, unlocking eligibility in **2026+ for annual 12-month principal forgiveness (Step 2)** upon proving adherence to state turnaround plans.

Where liquidity still ran out, California hospital insolvencies split sharply between private **Chapter 11 (`11 U.S.C. § 109(d)`)** and public healthcare district **Chapter 9 (`11 U.S.C. § 109(c)`)**:

- ***In re Madera Community Hospital* (`Bankr. E.D. Cal. No. 23-10457` — Reopened March 18, 2025):** Shuttered in December 2022 and in Chapter 11 by March 2023, Madera County's sole acute hospital paired its $57M `AB 112` loan with a court- and AG-stipulated management/purchase-option agreement with for-profit turnaround operator **American Advanced Management, Inc. (AAMI)**, officially **reopening its ER and inpatient beds on March 18, 2025** ([Fresnoland, Mar. 18, 2025](https://fresnoland.org/2025/03/18/madera-community-hospital/)).
- ***In re San Benito Health Care District d/b/a Hazel Hawkins Memorial Hospital* (`Bankr. N.D. Cal. No. 5:23-bk-50544`; *aff'd*, `N.D. Cal. No. 24-cv-02266-JD`, Mar. 21, 2025):** Sets the controlling California precedent blocking strategic municipal hospital bankruptcies. Although `11 U.S.C. § 904` shields a Chapter 9 healthcare district from involuntary liquidation or court receivership, the district must first clear `Cal. Gov't Code § 53760` (`AB 506` neutral evaluation or fiscal-emergency declaration) AND prove cash-flow **"insolvency" as of the petition date under `11 U.S.C. §§ 109(c)(3), 101(32)(C)`**. Following CNA and NUHW union challenges exposing a $15M cash-forecast discrepancy while bills were being paid current, Judge Stephen L. Johnson **dismissed Hazel Hawkins' Chapter 9 petition on March 21, 2024**, and **District Judge James Donato affirmed dismissal in full on March 21, 2025** (with the Board declining a Ninth Circuit appeal in April 2025, [SanBenito.com](https://sanbenito.com/hazel-hawkins-board-declines-to-pursue-bankruptcy-appeal/)). Worse still, although voters approved Measure X in November 2024 for a lease-to-purchase with Michigan's Insight Health, **Insight Health walked away on August 13, 2025**, citing `OBBBA`'s July 2025 federal Medicaid cuts ([Hazel Hawkins Release, Aug. 2025](https://www.hazelhawkins.com/news/2025/august/insight-health-steps-away-from-lease-to-purchase/)).
- ***In re Palo Verde Healthcare District* (`Bankr. C.D. Cal. No. 6:25-bk-17084`, Filed Sept. 30, 2025):** Battered by an Altera EHR billing dispute and cyber disruptions despite an $8.5M `AB 112` loan, remote Blythe's Palo Verde Healthcare District **filed Chapter 9 on September 30, 2025**, suspending inpatient admissions while keeping its ER open with Riverside University Health System support ([Palo Verde Filing Release PDF](https://www.paloverdehospital.org/DocumentCenter/View/276/PV-Hospital---Press-Release---District-Files-Bankruptcy-for-PV-Hospital---Oct-1-updatedpdf)).
- **Non-Bankruptcy Public District Restructuring (*Sharp Tri-City* & `AB 918` *Imperial Valley*):** Conversely, **Tri-City Healthcare District** pivoted after failed UCSD talks to win voter approval and launch **Sharp Tri-City Medical Center on July 1, 2026** backed by $100M+ in Sharp capital ([Sharp Release](https://www.sharp.com/tri-city-and-sharp-healthcare-partnership)), while Imperial County's statutorily mandated `AB 918` consolidation culminated in **October 2026** when El Centro's City Council unanimously approved transferring **El Centro Regional Medical Center** into the unified **Imperial Valley Healthcare District** alongside Pioneers Memorial ([City of El Centro, Oct. 2026](https://www.cityofelcentro.org/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/119)).

### 1.3 The Immovable January 1, 2030 Hospital Seismic Cliff (`HSC § 130065`), Newsom's `SB 1432` Veto, and `AB 869` (`PIN 80`)

Under the Alquist Hospital Facilities Seismic Safety Act (`SB 1953`, **`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 130065`**), every general acute care inpatient building in California must achieve **`SPC-3`, `SPC-4D`, or `SPC-5` structural** AND **`NPC-5` nonstructural** compliance (72-hour independent water, wastewater, and fuel capability to remain operational post-earthquake) **on or before January 1, 2030**, or be **removed from acute inpatient care** under pain of CDPH license revocation ([Justia HSC § 130065](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-107/part-7/chapter-1/article-9/section-130065/)). RAND estimated statewide compliance at $34B–$143B ($100B–$176B inflation-adjusted), averaging **$92 million per retrofitted building**. As Hanson Bridgett partners **Lisa Dal Gallo and Alan S. Bishop** demonstrated in their **Spring 2026 *California Health Law News* study** analyzing HCAI's October 2025 financial dataset, **for 17% of California hospitals, a single $92M building retrofit exceeds 100% of an entire year of gross hospital revenue**—yet as of HCAI's April 16, 2026 ratings list, the majority of California hospitals still operate at least one non-2030-compliant inpatient building ([Dal Gallo & Bishop Spring 2026 CHLN PDF](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/Spring%202026%20CHLN_LisaDalGallo_AlanBishop.pdf)).

Sacramento's message in 2024–2026 was unmistakable: **no statewide bailout is coming.**

- **Veto of Blanket `SB 1432` vs. Targeted `AB 869` (`HSC § 130065.1`):** On September 12, 2024, Governor Newsom **vetoed CHA-backed `SB 1432`** (which sought up to 8 years of statewide relief to 2038, [SB 1432 Veto Message PDF](https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SB-1432-Veto-Message.pdf)), and on September 28, 2024 signed narrow **`AB 869` (Wood, Ch. 801, Stats. 2024; `HSC § 130065.1`)** ([Justia HSC § 130065.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-107/part-7/chapter-1/article-9/section-130065-1/)). **No broader seismic extension passed in 2025 or 2026.** `AB 869` restricts relief strictly to five categories—`AB 112` loan recipients, qualifying small (<50 bed) independent hospitals, rural hospitals, Critical Access Hospitals, and **Health Care District (`HSC § 32000`)** / high-Medi-Cal distressed hospitals—authorizing HCAI (under **HCAI `PIN 80`**, issued March 4, 2025, [HCAI PIN 80](https://hcai.ca.gov/document/pin-80-seismic-compliance-plan-and-ab-869-delays-beyond-2030-deadline/)) to grant up to **3 years (to Jan. 1, 2033)** plus up to **2 discretionary years (hard cap Jan. 1, 2035)**, backed by **$5,000/day fines** for missed milestones.
- **Active 2026–2028 Permit Cliffs:** Following the January 1, 2026 compliance-plan/delay filing deadline (where HCAI's April 2026 tracker shows active *denials* alongside approvals) and the **March 1, 2026** deadline to submit NPC construction documents, non-extended California hospitals now stare down **March 1, 2028**—the final statutory cliff to hold issued HCAI NPC building permits or decommission inpatient towers.

## 2. Corporate Practice of Medicine (`CPOM`), Friendly PC–MSO Restructuring After `SB 351`, `104 NP` Independent Practice, OHCA's `1.8%` High-Cost Hospital Cap & Live MCN Docket, and Federal Antitrust Retreats

### 2.1 California `CPOM`, Moscone-Knox `51%/49%` Equity Rules, `§ 650` Fee-Splitting, and `SB 351` (`HSC §§ 1190–1192`)

California enforces one of America's strictest **Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM)** bars (`Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 2052, 2264, 2400`, [MBC Practice Guide](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Physicians-and-Surgeons/Practice-Information/)): lay corporations and LLCs (`Corp. Code § 17701.04(e)`) hold zero professional medical powers. Outside statutory exemptions—such as **`HSC § 1206(l)` Medical Foundations** (nonprofit entities furnishing care through **$\ge 40$ physicians across $\ge 10$ board-certified specialties**, used by Sutter, CommonSpirit/Dignity, and Cottage), county/UC hospitals, `HSC § 1206(b)` / Section 330 FQHCs, and Knox-Keene HMOs (`HSC § 1395(b)`)—private physician groups must organize under the **Moscone-Knox Professional Corporation Act (`Cal. Corp. Code § 13400 et seq.`)**, where licensed MDs/DOs must own **at least 51% of shares**, enumerated allied licensees (`RNs`/`NPs`, `PAs`, podiatrists, psychologists, PTs, pharmacists under `§ 13401.5(a)(1)–(16)`) may hold up to **49%** without outnumbering physician shareholders (save a 1-MD / 1-allied exception), and any share transfer to a layperson or MSO is **void (`Corp. Code § 13407`)** ([Justia Corp. Code § 13401.5](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-corp/title-1/division-3/part-4/section-13401-5/)).

Historically, private-equity and digital-health platforms navigated `§ 2400` via the **Friendly PC – Management Services Organization (MSO)** model: locking a California-licensed "friendly physician" under a Continuity / Stock Transfer Restriction Agreement while sweeping residual economics through an MSA. Although **percentage-of-gross-revenue MSO fees** survived facial arbitration review under **`Bus. & Prof. Code § 650(b)`** in *Epic Medical Management, LLC v. Paquette*, 244 Cal.App.4th 504 (2015) ([EBG Analysis](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/recent-california-court-decision-provides-useful-guidance-for-management-services-organizations)) where commensurate with non-clinical FMV and divorced from patient steering, aggressive MSO overreach collided with ***AAEM-PG v. Envision Healthcare Corp.*** (`N.D. Cal. No. 3:22-cv-00421`—resolved July 2024 after KKR-backed Envision exited California emergency medicine post-bankruptcy, [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/friendly-pc-model-survives-in-california-after-envision-healthcare-lit))—which became the legislative blueprint for **`SB 351`**.

Signed on **October 6, 2025** (**Chapter 409, Statutes of 2025**, effective **January 1, 2026**) as the companion to `AB 1415` after Governor Newsom vetoed `AB 3129`, **`SB 351` added Division 1.7 (`Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1190, 1191, 1192`)** ([SB 351 Chaptered Text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB351/id/3272245); [EBG SB 351 Analysis](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/california-governor-signs-sb-351-strengthening-the-states-corporate-practice-of-medicine-doctrine)), governing every private equity group, hedge fund, and controlled MSO/DSO interacting with any California physician, dental, or **`1206(l)` foundation** practice:

- **Tier 1 — Direct Clinical & Quota Interference Ban (`HSC § 1191(a)(1)`):** Bars PE/hedge-fund MSOs from interfering with diagnostic/treatment choices, referrals, care management, or **setting clinician schedules, patient-per-hour volume quotas, or required working hours** (`§ 1191(a)(1)(D)`).
- **Tier 2 — Five Non-Delegable Operational Functions (`HSC § 1191(a)(2)(A)–(E)`):** Even with a friendly PC's written consent, a PE/hedge-fund MSO **cannot control or be delegated power to determine**: (A) **ownership or content of medical records**; (B) **hiring, firing, or evaluation of physicians, dentists, allied staff, or medical assistants (`MAs`)** based in whole or part on clinical competency; (C) **parameters for contracting with third-party payors** or other providers (outlawing MSO power-of-attorney payor signature/rate-floor clauses!); (D) **billing and coding decisions**; or (E) **selection and approval of medical equipment and supplies**.
- **Voiding Non-Competes & Quality-of-Care Gag Clauses (`§ 1191(c)–(d)`) vs. `Bus. & Prof. Code § 16601`:** Voids any PE/MSO-linked clinician **non-compete (`§ 1191(c)(1)`)** and any **non-disparagement ("gag") clause (`§ 1191(c)(2)`)** restricting clinician speech regarding quality of care, utilization, ethics, or PE revenue-increasing strategies, backed by **direct California Attorney General injunctive and mandatory fee-shifting enforcement (`§ 1192`)**. While `§ 1191(d)(1)(A)` preserves bona fide **`Bus. & Prof. Code § 16601` sale-of-business non-competes** when a founding doctor sells practice goodwill for genuine FMV consideration, it kills "token rollover unit" and nominal-$100 "friendly PC shareholder" non-compete workarounds (*Bosley Medical Group*), reinforcing `SB 699` / `AB 1076` (`Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 16600.1, 16600.5`).
- **Retail Med-Spa, IV-Hydration & GLP-1 Compounding Sweeps (2024–2026):** Concurrently, the Medical Board, BRN, and Board of Pharmacy cracked down on lay "rent-a-medical-director" LLC med-spas, walk-in IV-hydration lounges administering infusions prior to an individualized Good Faith Exam, and asynchronous "intake-quiz" GLP-1 clinics violating `Bus. & Prof. Code § 2242.1` and post-shortage `FDCA §§ 503A/503B` compounding limits.

### 2.2 Historic January 2026 Launch of California "104 NPs" (`Bus. & Prof. Code § 2837.104`)

In **January 2026**—exactly three years after the January 2023 debut of group-bound **`103 NPs`** (`Bus. & Prof. Code § 2837.103`) under `AB 890`—the California Board of Registered Nursing (BRN) activated the **`104 NP` full independent-practice certification** on BreEZe for NPs completing 3 full-time years (`4,600` hours) as `103 NPs` ([CA BRN AB 890 Portal](https://www.rn.ca.gov/practice/ab890.shtml); [Hooper, Lundy & Bookman 104 NP Analysis](https://hooperlundy.com/california-releases-104-np-application-full-independent-practice-and-considerations-for-nurse-practitioners-and-health-care-organizations/)). For the first time in California history, a **`104 NP`** may practice **outside** physician-group or hospital settings with **zero standardized procedures** within their national board population focus, hold voting medical-staff privileges (`§ 2837.104(c)(1)`), and own 100% of a **Nursing Corporation (`Bus. & Prof. Code § 2775`; `Corp. Code § 13401.5(f)`)** (subject to `SB 1451` [2024] rules barring standalone "Doctor/Dr." usage by DNP holders without immediate NP specification). **Corporate Structuring Trap:** `AB 890` did *not* amend `Corp. Code § 13401.5(a)`—meaning a `104 NP`-owned Nursing Corp still **cannot** own $\ge 50\%$ of a Medical Corporation or employ MDs/DOs to perform outside-NP-scope surgery/medicine!

### 2.3 OHCA's Dual Bite: The `1.8%` High-Cost Hospital Cap (`HSC § 127502`) and Empirical Audit of All 55 MCN Filings (2024–Nov. 2026)

1. **Enforceable 2026 Statewide (`3.5%`) & Seven "High-Cost Hospital" (`1.8%`) Caps:** On April 24, 2024, the Health Care Affordability Board adopted a stepped five-year statewide Total Health Care Expenditures (`THCE`) cap of **3.5% in 2025 (baseline), 3.5% in 2026 (first enforceable year), 3.2% in 2027, 3.2% in 2028, and 3.0% in 2029** ([HCAI OHCA Spending Targets](https://hcai.ca.gov/affordability/ohca/slow-spending-growth/)). Then, on **April 22, 2025**, using HCAI and RAND commercial-to-Medicare price datasets, the Board carved out **seven specific "High-Cost Hospitals"** and slashed their enforceable annual cost-growth cap in half to **1.8% in 2026, 1.7% in 2027, 1.7% in 2028, and 1.6% in 2029** ([Becker's High-Cost Hospital Report](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/california-high-cost-hospitals-sound-alarm-over-deep-rate-cuts/)): **Stanford Health Care**, **Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital**, **Community Hospital of the Monterey Peninsula**, **Dominican Hospital Santa Cruz** (CommonSpirit), **Doctors Medical Center Modesto** (Tenet), AND **two California Public Healthcare Districts**—**Salinas Valley Health Medical Center** and **Washington Hospital Fremont**! Because nursing, drug, and 2030 seismic costs grow 4%–6%+ annually, a `1.8%` cap hands commercial payers regulatory cover to demand real-dollar unit-rate cuts during renewals, backed by `HSC § 127502.5`'s four-rung enforcement ladder culminating in Performance Improvement Plans (`PIPs`) and **uncapped administrative penalties** sized against parent/affiliate reserves.
2. **Complete Empirical Audit of OHCA's Live MCN Docket (55 Filings Through Nov. 2026) & `AB 1415`:** Across OHCA's first 32 months of pre-closing merger review (`HSC § 127507`), **55 completed MCNs** posted through November 2026 ([OHCA Public MCN/CMIR Docket](https://hcai.ca.gov/affordability/ohca/assess-market-consolidation/material-change-transaction-notices-mcn-and-cost-and-market-impact-review-cmir/)): **51 CMIR waivers (92.7%)**—including unconditional waivers for public-district/academic rescues like *Sharp/Tri-City* (Feb. 2026), *UCSD/Palomar JPA* (Mar. 2026), *John Muir/UCSF* (July 2025), *Imperial Valley/El Centro* (Oct. 2025), *Alta LA/Prospect Ch. 11* (Sept. 2025), and MSO deals like *OneOncology* (Sept. 2026) and *Commons Clinic* (May 2026)—versus **1 completed full CMIR** (*Res-Care / Sevita Health*, consuming **nearly 12 full months** through April 8, 2026), **1 pending full CMIR** (*Covenant Care*, >18 months pending), **2 November 2026 initial filings** (*Premier Surgery Center / Cottage* and *Maxor / MedImpact*), AND OHCA's **first-ever "Third Option" on August 12, 2026**: waiving a CMIR in *NAHS Holding / MOJUMU* while issuing an open-ended **Post-Transaction Monitoring Advisory Letter** ([Ropes & Gray Oct. 2026 Alert](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/10/ohca-continues-expansion-of-health-care-transaction-review-authority-updated-regulations)). Paired with **`AB 1415` (Ch. 641, Stats. 2025, eff. Jan. 1, 2026)** and OHCA's **November 2, 2026 emergency regulations (`22 CCR § 97431 et seq.`)**—sweeping in 10%+ PE/hedge-fund equity/debt stakes, $10M+/$25M+ MSOs, and facility sale-leasebacks—4.5 to 6 months must be built into every non-CMIR California deal calendar.

### 2.4 Federal Antitrust Retreats vs. Roll-Up Settlements (2025–2026)

California's regulatory expansion stands in sharp relief against federal judicial rollbacks in 2025–2026:

- **FTC Roll-Up Enforcement Survives (*Welsh Carson* Jan. 2025 Consent Order & *USAP* April 2026 Settlement):** Although S.D. Tex. dismissed minority PE sponsor Welsh Carson under Section 13(b) in May 2024 while keeping Clayton Act § 7 / Sherman Act § 2 serial-acquisition claims alive against U.S. Anesthesia Partners, the FTC voted **5–0 on January 17, 2025** for a 10-year Section 5 administrative consent order freezing Welsh Carson's USAP stake and requiring prior FTC approval/notice on anesthesia and hospital-based physician investments nationwide ([FTC Welsh Carson Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-secures-settlement-private-equity-firm-antitrust-roll-scheme-case)), followed on **April 23, 2026** by an **FTC–USAP settlement in principle** (stayed May 26, 2026, with companion payer/patient class settlements filed Oct. 30, 2026, [FTC USAP Docket](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2010031-us-anesthesia-partners-inc-ftc-v)).
- **Vacatur of the 2025 Federal HSR Overhaul (*Chamber of Commerce v. ## 3. California Managed Care, Knox-Keene Global Risk (`28 CCR § 1300.49`), `RBO` Solvency, `Proposition 35` vs. `SB 125` (`S298916`), Clinical & Payer AI Statutes, No Surprises Act (*TMA III En Banc*), and `$8B` `BH-CONNECT`

### 3.1 DMHC "Global Risk" Enforcement (`28 CCR § 1300.49`), `RBO` Solvency (`HSC § 1375.4`), and Eight-Figure DMHC Fines

Under the Knox-Keene Health Care Service Plan Act (`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1340 et seq.`) and DMHC's **General Licensure Regulation (`28 CCR § 1300.49`; `APL 19-014`)** ([DMHC APL 19-014 PDF](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Portals/0/Docs/OPL/APL19-014%20(OLS)%20-%20Guidance%20Regarding%20General%20Licensure%20Regulation%20(6_13_19).pdf?ver=2021-05-11-140559-583)), any medical group, IPA, MSO, or value-based enabler accepting **global risk**—prepaid capitation or downside risk-sharing covering *both* institutional hospital and professional physician services (outside direct federal `ACO REACH` FFS Medicare contracts with CMS)—must obtain a **Full-Service** or **Restricted Knox-Keene (`RKK`) License** (or a rare `HSC § 1343(b)` Director exemption). DMHC systematically rejects synthetic "hospital risk-pool" side letters shifting >50% institutional deficits onto unlicensed IPAs. While an `RKK` cannot market directly to employers or consumers, it must maintain continuous **Tangible Net Equity (`TNE`, `28 CCR § 1300.76`)**—triggering mandatory monthly financial supervision if TNE dips below **150% of required minimum TNE (`28 CCR § 1300.84.3`)**—and obey the strict **15% administrative cost cap (`28 CCR § 1300.78`)**, capping MSO fee extraction.

Downstream, **Risk-Bearing Organizations (`RBOs`, `HSC § 1375.4`; `28 CCR §§ 1300.75.4–1300.75.4.8`)**—over 200 California IPAs and capitated groups taking professional risk—undergo quarterly public grading on four benchmarks ([28 CCR § 1300.75.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/28-CCR-1300.75.4)): (1) positive `TNE`; (2) positive working capital; (3) a minimum **0.75 cash-to-claims ratio** (including actuarial `IBNR` certifications); and (4) **95% clean-claims payment within 45 working days (`HSC § 1371`)**. Following Cano Health's 2024 Chapter 11 California exit and **Prospect Medical Holdings' January 2025 Chapter 11 bankruptcy**, DMHC ring-fenced Prospect Health Plan (`RKK`) and Prospect Medical Group (`RBO`, ~600,000 enrollees) ahead of **Astrana Health's $708 million rescue acquisition closed July 1, 2025** ([Astrana Closing Release](https://ir.astranahealth.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/261/astrana-health-announces-closing-of-prospect-health-acquisition)). Concurrently, DMHC's Office of Enforcement normalized eight-figure sanctions: **Kaiser Foundation Health Plan's record $200 million settlement** ($50M fine + $150M investment under **`SB 221`**, `HSC § 1367.03(a)(5)(G)`'s 10-business-day behavioral-health follow-up rule, [DMHC Kaiser Portal](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Resources/DMHCReports/PublicReports/KaiserSettlementAgreement.aspx)); **L.A. Care's $55 million joint DMHC/DHCS settlement** finalized November 2024 ([DHCS Release](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/newsroom-office-of-communications/california-announces-settlement-agreements-with-l-a-care-health-plan/)); and **Anthem Blue Cross's $15 million fine on January 30, 2026** (+ 4 years of independent grievance auditing, following $8.5M and $3.5M fines in late 2024, [DMHC Jan. 30, 2026 Release](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Resources/Newsroom/PressReleases/January30,2026.aspx)).

### 3.2 `Proposition 35` Collides With Federal `OBBBA` and `SB 125` — The California Supreme Court's November 10, 2026 Order (`S298916`)

Approved by ~68% of California voters in November 2024 (`Welf. & Inst. Code § 14199.100 et seq.`, [DHCS Prop 35 Overview](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/Budget/Documents/Prop-35-Spending-Plan-Overview.pdf)), **Proposition 35** made California's Managed Care Organization (`MCO`) tax permanent to fund dedicated Medi-Cal provider rate subaccounts (primary/specialty physicians, OB/GYN & Planned Parenthood, emergency departments/physicians, FQHCs, behavioral health, ground ambulance, and GME), locked out General Fund diversions, required an **80% (four-fifths) legislative supermajority** for amendments, and **capped commercial health plan taxes at `$2.50 PMPM`**. After beating back May 2025 budget attempts to strip ~$504M in Prop 56 women's-health/dental funds, the Prop 35 coalition was blindsided on **July 4, 2025** when Congress enacted **`OBBBA` (`P.L. 119-21`)**—outlawing California's asymmetric 75/75 waiver formula (which taxed Medi-Cal member-months at ~$180–$205 PMPM vs. $1.75–$2.50 commercial) effective **December 31, 2026**.

To prevent a multi-billion-dollar Medi-Cal crater in 2027, Sacramento enacted budget trailer bill **`SB 125` (Chapter 24, Statutes of 2026)** in June 2026 ([Wilke Fleury SB 125 Analysis](https://www.wilkefleury.com/blog/the-8-85-fix-california-rewrites-its-managed-care-organization-tax/)), replacing Proposition 35's $2.50 commercial ceiling with a **uniform `$8.85 PMPM` tax across both commercial and Medi-Cal enrollment for 2027–2029** (~$1.5B+ in new commercial premium pass-throughs) while routing proceeds to backfill General Fund Medi-Cal baselines. On October 1, 2026, the California Association of Health Plans (`CAHP`) and California Medical Association (`CMA`) filed an original writ petition in the California Supreme Court (*CAHP & CMA v. DHCS*, **Case No. `S298916`**, [MCO Lawsuit Portal](https://www.mcotaxlawsuitfacts.com/)) alleging unconstitutional nullification of Proposition 35 under `Cal. Const. art. II, § 10(c)`. On **November 10, 2026**, the **California Supreme Court summarily denied the writ petition** ([Cal. Supreme Court Conference Results, Nov. 10, 2026](https://supreme.courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/supremecourt/default/documents/conference%20results%20-%20111026%20-%20R.pdf))—leaving `SB 125`'s `$8.85 PMPM` submission to CMS intact entering 2027 while leaving Superior Court litigation risk alive.

### 3.3 California's Four-Statute Clinical & Payer AI and Prior-Authorization Perimeter (`SB 1120`, `AB 3030`, `AB 489`, `SB 306`)

California now enforces four interlocking healthcare AI and utilization-review statutes binding plans, **delegated RBOs/IPAs**, hospitals, clinics, and health-tech vendors:

1. **`SB 1120` (*Physicians Make Decisions Act*, Ch. 879, Stats. 2024; Eff. Jan. 1, 2025 — `HSC § 1367.01(k)` & `Ins. Code § 10123.135`):** Catalyzed by class actions over Cigna's 1.2-second `PxDx` batch denials (*Kisting-Leung v. Cigna*, E.D. Cal.) and UnitedHealth/naviHealth's `nH Predict` algorithm (*Estate of Lokken*, 766 F. Supp. 3d 835, D. Minn. 2025), `SB 1120` **prohibits plans and delegated RBOs/UM vendors from using AI alone to deny, delay, or modify care based on medical necessity**—requiring individualized patient-record review and determination by a licensed California physician or competent clinician ([CDI SB 1120 Guidance PDF](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0500-legal-info/0200-regulations/HealthGuidance/upload/SB-1120-1-Guidance-Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence-Algorithms-and-Other-Software-Tools-in-Utilization-Management.pdf)).
2. **`AB 3030` (Ch. 848, Stats. 2024; Eff. Jan. 1, 2025 — `HSC § 1339.75`):** Requires hospitals, clinics/FQHCs, and physician practices using generative AI for **clinical patient communications** (excluding scheduling/billing) to display prominent GenAI disclaimers and instructions on how to contact a human clinician—**unless a licensed human provider reads and reviews the draft prior to sending** (`§ 1339.75(c)`, [LegiScan AB 3030](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB3030/id/2979649)).
3. **`AB 489` (Bonta, Ch. 615, Stats. 2025; Eff. Jan. 1, 2026 — `Bus. & Prof. Code § 4999.8 et seq.`):** Extends California healing-arts title bans to AI chatbots, avatars, and apps—strictly barring use of **`"M.D."`, `"D.O."`, `"R.N."`, `"N.P."`, `"doctor"`, `"nurse"`, or `"therapist"`** or implying human licensure, with per-instance enforcement by DCA licensing boards ([AB 489 Analysis PDF](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2025-04/ab-489-bonta-apcp-analysis_0.pdf)).
4. **`SB 306` (Becker, Ch. 408, Stats. 2025 — `HSC § 1367.025` & `Ins. Code § 10133.52`):** Eliminates commercial prior authorization at the plan/RBO service level for any non-Tier-3/4-drug procedure approved **$\ge 90\%$ of the time**—requiring plan/RBO approval-rate data submissions by **December 31, 2026**, regulator exemption-list publication by **July 1, 2027**, and mandatory prior-auth repeal on **January 1, 2028** ([LegiScan SB 306](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB306/id/3272262)).

### 3.4 Payer-Provider Brinkmanship (*CHA v. Anthem*), `AB 72` vs. *En Banc TMA III* (Aug. 2026), and `$8B` `BH-CONNECT`

Following **Scripps Health's sustained 2024–2026 Medicare Advantage exit** (stemming $75M/yr in MA losses across 32,000 seniors, [Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/medicare-advantage/why-scripps-healths-medicare-advantage-exit-paid-off/)) and brinkmanship standoffs across Providence, UCSF, MemorialCare, and Dignity/Blue Shield, the **California Hospital Association sued Anthem Blue Cross in May 2026** (L.A. Anthem's retaliation stems directly from California's bifurcated surprise-billing landscape: while state-regulated DMHC/CDI non-emergency facility claims are governed by **`AB 72` (`HSC § 1371.31`—capping default out-of-network pay at the greater of `125% of Medicare` or CPI-indexed `ACR`)**, **ERISA self-funded employer plans** (~50% of commercial lives) route through federal **No Surprises Act (`42 U.S.C. § 300gg-111`)** baseball arbitration—where on **August 11, 2026, the full *en banc* Fifth Circuit in *Texas Medical Ass'n v. HHS (TMA III)*** struck down federal `QPA` calculation rules nationwide, barring insurers from depressing median QPAs with `$0`/unutilized **"ghost rates"** or excluding retrospective **bonus/incentive payments** ([Polsinelli *TMA III En Banc* Alert](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/en-banc-fifth-circuit-rejects-key-qpa-rules-in-tma-iii)), cementing 75%–85% provider IDR win rates above 300% of Medicare. Meanwhile, in Medi-Cal, `CalAIM` scaled past 453,000 cumulative `ECM` and 528,000 `Community Supports` recipients ([DHCS 2026 Update](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/file/cost-effectiveness-of-ca-medi-cal-community-supports-fact-sheet-2026-update-pdf/)), reinforced by CMS's **December 16, 2024 approval of California's `$8 billion` `BH-CONNECT` § 1115 waiver (2025–2029)**—unlocking conditional **short-term `IMD` (>16-bed psychiatric, $\le 30$-day mean stay) federal matching funds**, `$1.9B` workforce and `$1.9B` county incentive pools, and up to **6 months of Medi-Cal `Transitional Rent`** rolled out statewide in January 2026 ([DHCS BH-CONNECT Release](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/newsroom-office-of-communications/december-16-2024/)).

## 4. FQHCs (`APM`), the Three-Front 340B War, `OBBBA` Medicaid Cuts (`P.L. 119-21`), Outpatient ASC Joint Ventures, Telehealth Cliffs, and Second-Generation California Shield Laws

### 4.1 California's FQHC `APM` Rollout and the Three-Front 340B Litigation War (Through Oct. 2026)

Under `Welf. & Inst. Code § 14132.100` and `42 U.S.C. § 1396a(bb)(6)`, DHCS launched California's **FQHC Alternative Payment Methodology (`APM`)** on July 1, 2024 and expanded clinic cohorts across 2025–2026 ([DHCS FQHC APM Portal](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal/Pages/FQHC-APM.aspx)): shifting participating FQHCs out of rigid face-to-face `PPS` encounter visits (`0521` wrap billing) into predictable **capitated PMPM payments** through MCPs—freeing clinics to deploy clinical pharmacists, CHWs, group visits, and e-consults backed by an annual **one-sided `PPS` floor stop-loss reconciliation** (alongside strict HRSA Section 330 `Form 5A/5B` Change-in-Scope `FTCA`/340B site rules and `100%/200% FPG` Sliding Fee Discount audits under `42 CFR § 51c.303(f)`).

Simultaneously, safety-net hospitals (`DSH`, `CAH`, `RRC`) and FQHCs fought a **three-front `340B` (`42 U.S.C. § 256b`) war** across 2024–2026:

1. **Front 1 — Federal Contract-Pharmacy Restrictions Upheld:** In *Sanofi Aventis U.S. LLC v. HHS*, 58 F.4th 696 (3d Cir. 2023) and ***Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Johnson*, 102 F.4th 452 (D.C. Cir. May 21, 2024)** ([D.C. Cir. Opinion PDF](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2024/05/21-5299-2055482.pdf)), federal appellate courts held that because `§ 256b` is silent on delivery sites, HRSA cannot stop drugmakers from restricting contract-pharmacy deliveries.
2. **Front 2 — State 340B Laws Upheld Outside California vs. California's Stalled `AB 1460` & `Medi-Cal Rx` Trap:** While the Eighth Circuit (*PhRMA v. McClain*, 95 F.4th 1136, *cert. denied*, 145 S. Ct. 768, Dec. 9, 2024) and Fifth Circuit (*Novartis v. Fitch*, Sept. 2025 & Apr. 2026; *cert. pending*, U.S. No. `26-457`) upheld Arkansas and Mississippi contract-pharmacy mandates against preemption challenges, **California's `AB 1460` stalled in the Senate Health Committee in 2025–2026** ([LegiScan AB 1460](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1460))—leaving California covered entities protected only against PBM discrimination (`SB 786`, `WIC § 14105.465`), *not* manufacturer contract-pharmacy cutoffs, while **`Medi-Cal Rx` (`WIC § 14105.46`)** bars contract-pharmacy Medi-Cal 340B billing and forces clinics/hospitals to bill Medi-Cal strictly at **Actual Acquisition Cost (`AAC`)** plus dispensing fee!
3. **Front 3 — The 2026 "340B Rebate Model" Showdown (*Novartis v. Kennedy*, D.C. Cir. July 21, 2026 & *AHA v. Kennedy*, D. Me. Oct. 2026):** After J&J, Eli Lilly, BMS, Sanofi, Novartis, and Kalderos sued to unilaterally replace upfront 340B discounts with post-dispensing cash rebates, the **D.C. Circuit ruled on July 21, 2026 in *Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp. v. Kennedy* (`No. 25-5177`)** that under `§ 256b(a)(1)` (*"as provided by the Secretary"*), **only HHS—never drugmakers acting unilaterally—may authorize a rebate model**. Yet HHS/HRSA then approved its own **revised 340B Rebate Model Pilot Program effective January 1, 2027** for IRA Maximum Fair Price (`MFP`) drugs—prompting the **American Hospital Association to file suit in October 2026 in the District of Maine (*AHA v. Kennedy*)** to block the January 1, 2027 working-capital shift ([HRSA OPA](https://www.hrsa.gov/opa)).

### 4.2 The `OBBBA` (`P.L. 119-21`) Medicaid Cliff & California's Jan. 1, 2026 Undocumented Adult Freeze

Signed July 4, 2025, **Title VII, Subtitle B of `P.L. 119-21` (`139 Stat. 72`, ~`$900B–$1T` 10-year Medicaid cut, [Congress.gov PLAW-119publ21 PDF](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.pdf))** hits California acute/ambulatory providers with three sequential shocks:

- **January 1, 2027 — 80-Hour Monthly Work Requirements (`§ 71119`) & 6-Month Redeterminations (`§ 71117`):** Mandating 80 hours/month of verified work, training, or school (plus semiannual eligibility checks) across California's `4.5M+` ACA expansion adults (subject to at most a 6-month CMS IT transition to June 30, 2027).
- **October 1, 2027 — Provider-Tax Safe-Harbor Step-Down (`§ 71115`, `42 U.S.C. § 1396b(w)`):** Freezing provider taxes as of July 4, 2025 and stepping ACA expansion states' safe harbor (exempting only SNFs/ICFs) **down by `0.5%/year` from `6.0%` starting FY 2028 to a permanent `3.5%` ceiling in FY 2032**—directly eroding California's voter-locked **Hospital Quality Assurance Fee (`HQAF`, `WIC § 14169.50`)**!
- **January 1, 2028 — `100% of Medicare` Cap on State Directed Payments (`§ 71116`, overriding `42 CFR § 438.6(c)` `ACR` ceilings):** Capping new hospital/physician `SDPs` in expansion states at **100% of Medicare** and forcing grandfathered `ACR`-benchmarked California `HQAF`, public-hospital `EPP`, and `QIP` directed payments (often 200%+ of Medicare) **down by 10 percentage points per year starting January 1, 2028**.
- **California's State-Only Undocumented Adult Rollback (`Jan. 1, 2026 Freeze` & `July 1, 2027 $30 Premiums`):** Compounding `OBBBA`, California's FY 2025–26 and FY 2026–27 Budgets **froze new full-scope Medi-Cal enrollment for undocumented adults age 19+ effective January 1, 2026**, eliminated weight-loss GLP-1 Medi-Cal coverage, and scheduled **`$30/month` premiums and adult dental rollbacks for July 1, 2027** ([DHCS Immigrant Eligibility FAQ](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/keep-your-Medi-Cal/Pages/Medi-Cal-Immigrant-Eligibility-FAQs.aspx))—pushing uncompensated post-stabilization charity care back onto hospital EDs (`EMTALA`) and FQHC sliding-fee schedules.

### 4.3 Outpatient Migration (`CY 2026–2027 IPO Phase-Out`), ASC Safe Harbors, California `Lab. Code § 139.3`, and `*Capen v. Shewry*`

In the **CY 2026** and **CY 2027 OPPS/ASC Final Rules** ([CMS CY 2026 Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-2026-hospital-outpatient-prospective-payment-system-opps-and-ambulatory-surgical); [CMS CY 2027 Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cy-2027-medicare-hospital-outpatient-prospective-payment-system-and-ambulatory-surgical-center)), CMS launched a **complete three-year phase-out of the Medicare Inpatient-Only (`IPO`) list by CY 2028**—removing **285 codes in CY 2026** and **637 codes in CY 2027** (`922` total) while adding **`1,178` surgical procedures (`560` + `618`) to the ASC Covered Procedures List (`42 CFR § 416.166`)** and deepening `40%` site-neutral payment cuts on grandfathered off-campus `HOPDs` (drug administration & non-contrast imaging).

Hospital-physician and PE-physician **Ambulatory Surgery Center (`ASC`) joint ventures** in California must thread four needles simultaneously:

1. **Federal Stark (`42 CFR §§ 411.351, 411.355(e)`) & AKS ASC Safe Harbors (`42 CFR § 1001.952(r)(2)–(4)`):** Composite ASC rates are exempt from Stark DHS (`§ 411.351`, with `§ 411.355(e)` protecting separately payable ASC implants), while AKS safe harbor requires zero investor loans, proportional capital returns, **dual one-third tests (`1/3` practice income + `1/3` procedures in-facility)** for multi-specialty ASCs (`§ 1001.952(r)(3)`—disqualifying passive non-operating PCP/cardiologist referrers!), and strict hospital non-steering + FMV space/equipment leases in hospital-surgeon ASCs (`§ 1001.952(r)(4)`).
2. **California `PORA` (`BPC § 650.01`) vs. the Workers' Compensation Trap (`Cal. Lab. Code §§ 139.3, 139.31(i)`):** While California's all-payer `PORA` covers 8 ancillary categories (imaging, lab, PT, radiation oncology) and omits general outpatient surgery (subject to mandatory written 5%/$5,000 patient ownership disclosures under **`BPC § 654.2`** and `BPC § 650` proportional-return rules), **`Cal. Lab. Code § 139.3(b)(5)` explicitly bans physician self-referrals for "outpatient surgery" in Workers' Compensation**—rendering Workers' Comp ASC liens uncollectible unless the surgeon gives prior written ownership disclosure AND obtains **prior written insurer preauthorization under `Lab. Code § 139.31(i)`** ([Lab. Code § 139.31](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=139.31.))!
3. ***Capen v. Shewry* (`155 Cal.App.4th 378 (2007)`) CDPH Licensing Blockade:** Under *Capen*, **CDPH refuses to issue a state surgical-clinic license to any ASC with even 1% physician ownership** (`HSC §§ 1204(b)(1), 1206(b)`). Every physician-owned or hospital-physician JV ASC in California must instead operate as an **accredited Medical Board "Outpatient Setting" (`HSC § 1248 et seq.`)** via `AAAHC`/`Joint Commission`/`QUAD A` accreditation and Medicare deemed status, keeping facility fees inside the ASC LLC and professional `CPT` fees inside surgeons' Moscone-Knox PCs.

### 4.4 Telehealth Cliffs (Dec. 31, 2026 DEA vs. Dec. 31, 2027 Medicare) and California's Second-Generation Shield Laws (`AB 260`, `SB 497`, `HSC § 1317`)

- **37-Day Federal DEA Telemedicine Cliff vs. 2027 Medicare Extension:** While the **Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026** (Feb. 2026) extended **Medicare telehealth flexibilities** (home originating site, **FQHC/RHC distant-site billing**, audio-only, Acute Hospital Care at Home, and deferred telemental in-person rules) **through December 31, 2027** (alongside permanent California Medi-Cal parity at `WIC § 14132.725`), **DEA and HHS's Fourth Temporary Extension for Schedule II–V controlled-substance telemedicine prescribing (`90 Fed. Reg. 61301`, Dec. 31, 2025, [Federal Register Doc. 2025-24123](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-24123)) expires December 31, 2026**—requiring immediate year-end contingency tracking for psychiatric and pain-management clinics.
- **California's Hardened 2025–2026 Shield Package (`AB 352`, `AB 260`, `SB 497`) & *Providence Eureka* (`HSC § 1317`):** After *Purl v. HHS* (N.D. Tex. June 2025) vacated the federal HIPAA Reproductive Health Privacy Rule nationwide, California's **`AB 352` (`Civ. Code §§ 56.101, 56.108, 56.110`)**—requiring `Epic`/EHRs and HIEs (`Carequality`, `Manifest MedEx`) to automatically **segregate and block out-of-state electronic transmission** of abortion, contraception, and gender-affirming records—became hospitals' primary shield, expanded in late 2025–2026 by **`AB 260` (Stats. 2025; [LegiScan AB 260](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB260))** (immunizing clinics/hospitals dispensing **mifepristone** even if FDA revokes approval and authorizing **anonymous clinic-only prescription vial labels** under `BPC § 4076`) and **`SB 497` (Stats. 2025, eff. Jan. 1, 2026; [LegiScan SB 497](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB497))** (extending `CMIA` foreign-subpoena bans to **adult and minor** gender-affirming care alike and erecting a **California court-warrant wall around `CURES` Schedule III testosterone data** at `HSC § 11165.1`). Simultaneously, after the U.S. Supreme Court punted federal `EMTALA` preemption in *Moyle v. United States*, 603 U.S. 324 (2024) and CMS rescinded its federal `EMTALA` abortion memo in 2025, California Attorney General Rob Bonta weaponized **`Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1317`** in ***People v. St. Joseph Health Northern California (Providence Eureka)*** (filed Sept. 2024; **Humboldt Super. Ct. overruled Providence's First Amendment religious demurrer in 2025**, [CA AG Release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-lawsuit-against-providence-st-joseph-hospital))—confirming that Catholic `ERD` and secular California EDs alike must provide stabilizing emergency abortions whenever a patient faces serious health injury even if fetal cardiac activity remains.

## 5. California Local Health Care Districts (`HSC § 32000`) and Multi-Billion-Dollar Hospital `IPD` Construction — Hanson Bridgett's Twin Moats

In private-equity physician roll-ups and mega-cap New York/Delaware hospital mergers, dozens of Am Law 50 firms compete on commoditized playbooks. In California, however, two massive, capital-intensive sub-sectors sit behind statutory and technical walls that out-of-state healthcare boutiques cannot penetrate: **California's ~79 Local Health Care Districts (`Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 32000–32499.4`)** and **collaborative Integrated Project Delivery (`IPD`) / Progressive Design-Build (`PDB`) contracting for multi-billion-dollar 2030 hospital seismic replacements**. Hanson Bridgett LLP dominates the intersection of both.

### 5.1 The Four-Pillar Statutory Gauntlet Governing California Local Health Care Districts (`HSC § 32000 et seq.`)

Represented statewide by the Association of California Healthcare Districts ([ACHD](https://www.achd.org/)), roughly three dozen active hospital-owning California Health Care Districts—spanning major suburban medical centers (El Camino Health, MarinHealth, Salinas Valley Health, Washington Hospital Fremont, Kaweah Health, Palomar Health, Grossmont, Tri-City) to rural critical-access lifelines (John C. Private-sector healthcare counsel routinely detonate district transactions by missing four municipal-law landmines, while pure municipal firms lack clinical `Stark`/`AKS`, `EMTALA`, and Medi-Cal Intergovernmental Transfer (`IGT`) competence:
