## Executive Thesis

California medical staff law operates as one of the most technically unforgiving statutory ecosystems in American healthcare. Unlike most states—where hospital medical staffs function merely as internal operating divisions subordinate to hospital corporate management—California codifies **statutory medical staff self-governance (`Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 2282, 2282.5`)**, treating every acute-care hospital's organized medical staff as a **separate legal unincorporated association (`Code Civ. Proc. § 369.5`)** empowered to elect its own officers, assess and control its own dues treasury, initiate and amend bylaws (subject to governing-board approval that *"shall not be unreasonably withheld"*), and **retain independent legal counsel at the medical staff's expense (`BPC § 2282.5(b)(3)`)**. Surrounding that governance core sits an intricate statutory web: `Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 809–809.9` quasi-judicial fair hearings, asymmetric state (`BPC §§ 805, 805.01, 805.8`) versus federal (`NPDB`, `45 CFR Part 60`) mandatory reporting clocks, post-*Bonni* anti-SLAPP surgery (`CCP § 425.16`), post-*Fahlen* physician whistleblower suits (`Health & Saf. Code § 1278.5`), and `Evid. Code § 1157` discovery immunity that shields committee records in California Superior Court yet **evaporates instantly under `Fed. R. Evid. 501` the moment a plaintiff pleads a single federal claim in U.S. District Court**.

## 1. Medical Staff Self-Governance (`BPC § 2282.5`), System Unification, and the 2027 `AB 1199` Reappointment Transition

### 1.1 The Statutory Dual-Entity Architecture (`Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 2282, 2282.5`, `22 CCR § 70703`, and `CCP § 369.5`)

In California hospitals with five or more physicians, the organized medical staff is neither a committee of the hospital corporation nor a subordinate department of hospital administration. Through decades of judicial precedent (*Anton v. San Antonio Community Hospital*, 19 Cal.3d 802, 824 (1977); *Hongsathavij v. Queen of Angels/Hollywood Presbyterian Medical Center*, 62 Cal.App.4th 1123, 1136 (1998); *Bichai v. Dignity Health*, 61 Cal.App.5th 869, 879–82 (2021)) and legislative codification in **Senate Bill 1325 (Stats. 2004, ch. 699, enacting `Bus. & Prof. Code § 2282.5`, [BPC § 2282.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=2282.5))**, California established a dual-entity check-and-balance model born directly out of the 2002–2004 *Community Memorial Hospital of San Buenaventura* crisis (where hospital administration locked out elected medical staff officers, imposed unilateral loyalty oaths barring outpatient investment, and seized ~$250,000 in medical staff dues):

- **Separate Unincorporated Association Standing (`Code Civ. Proc. § 369.5(a)`):** Under `CCP § 369.5(a)`, an organized medical staff has independent legal capacity to sue and be sued in its own name—including suing its own hospital governing board for writ or injunctive relief under **`BPC § 2282.5(d)`** after exhausting good-faith meet-and-confer procedures (`§ 2282.5(c)`). That statutory teeth was proven decisively in ***Medical Staff of Sharp Memorial Hospital v. Superior Court (Pusterla)*, 121 Cal.App.4th 173 (2004)** and ***Tulare Regional Medical Center Medical Staff v. Tulare Local Healthcare District* (Tulare Cnty. Super. Ct. No. VCU264227, 2018 Stipulated Judgment)**, where a public healthcare district board dissolved its elected medical staff and installed an outside group, only to have the deposed medical staff win court-ordered restoration of its exclusive statutory status, bylaws, officers, and treasury.
- **Core Professional Autonomy & the Anti-Unilateral-Amendment Rule (`BPC § 2282.5(a)`):** The medical staff holds primary statutory authority to establish and enforce clinical criteria for membership and privileges (`§ 2282.5(a)(1)`), run credentialing and peer review (`§ 2282.5(a)(2)`), and **initiate, develop, and adopt medical staff bylaws, rules, and regulations, and amendments thereto, *"subject to the approval of the hospital governing body, which approval shall not be unreasonably withheld"* (`§ 2282.5(a)(6)`)**. Under `22 CCR § 70701(a)(1)(F)` and ***El-Attar v. Hollywood Presbyterian Medical Center*, 56 Cal.4th 976 (2013)** ([El-Attar Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2013/s184203.html)), a hospital governing board possesses residual fiduciary authority to step in *only* if the medical staff abdicates its statutory or bylaw duty to act; a hospital board can never unilaterally rewrite medical staff bylaws simply to gain managerial leverage (*Smith v. Selma Community Hospital*, 164 Cal.App.4th 1478 (2008)).
- **The Three Uninfringeable Treasury & Counsel Rights (`BPC § 2282.5(b)(1)–(3)`):** Medical staff bylaws cannot interfere with the medical staff's independent rights to: (1) select and remove its own medical staff officers (`(b)(1)`), (2) **assess and control medical staff dues** solely for medical staff purposes (`(b)(2)`), and (3) **retain and be represented by independent legal counsel at the expense of the medical staff (`(b)(3)`)**.

### 1.2 Multi-Hospital System Unification: Why Federal `42 CFR § 482.22(b)(4)` Collides with California `Title 22`

As multi-hospital systems expand across California—exemplified by UCI Health's acquisition of four Tenet Orange County hospitals (`UCI Health Community Network`), Sutter Health's system growth, CommonSpirit/Dignity, Providence, and Sharp HealthCare—health-system executives routinely ask counsel to collapse multiple community hospital medical staffs into a single system-wide Medical Executive Committee (`MEC`). Counsel must navigate a sharp federal-state licensing trap:

- **Federal CMS Opt-In / Opt-Out Mandate (`42 CFR § 482.22(b)(4)`):** Under CMS's Conditions of Participation ([42 CFR § 482.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-482/subpart-C/section-482.22)), a multi-hospital system may adopt a unified, integrated medical staff **only if**: (i) the medical staff voters at **each separately certified hospital affirmatively vote to opt in**; (ii) the unified bylaws preserve an ongoing mechanism advising practitioners at each hospital of their right to **vote by majority to opt back out** into a separate staff; (iii) unique local patient populations and clinical service differences are accommodated; and (iv) **unification complies with state licensing law**.
- **California `CDPH` "One `GACH` License = One Medical Staff" Rule (`22 CCR §§ 70701, 70703` vs. `HSC § 1250.8`):** Where two hospital campuses share a **single consolidated `CDPH` General Acute Care Hospital (`GACH`) license** under `Health & Saf. Code § 1250.8` (such as Sutter's CPMC campuses or Alta Bates Summit campuses), a single organized medical staff is lawful as of right. Conversely, across **separately licensed California hospitals**, `CDPH` Center for Health Care Quality enforces `BPC § 2282` and `22 CCR § 70703(a)` (*"Each hospital shall have an organized medical staff..."*) per license and has never issued a blanket `AFL` approving cross-license unification without a facility-specific **Program Flexibility waiver (`22 CCR § 70129` / `HSC § 1276(e)`)**.
- **The Defensible California System Architecture (`CVO` + Harmonized Bylaws + `BPC § 809.08` Information Sharing):** Rather than risking a `CDPH` licensing deficiency or triggering a `BPC § 2282.5` revolt by forcing formal unification across separate licenses, California health systems preserve a separate unincorporated medical staff and `MEC` on each `GACH` license while centralizing administrative intake into an **`NCQA`-certified Centralized Verification Organization (`CVO`)** (noting that effective **July 1, 2025**, `NCQA` shortened `PSV` verification windows from 180 to **120 days** for health plans and from 120 to **90 days** for `CVOs`, while mandating **30-day ongoing monitoring** of `OIG LEIE`/`SAM.gov`/licensing sanctions), adopting harmonized system privilege-delineation cards, and executing **`Bus. & Prof. Code § 809.08` inter-peer-review sharing protocols** so `OPPE`/`FPPE` and disciplinary histories flow across sister-hospital `MECs` without waiving `Evid. Code § 1157` discovery immunity.

### 1.3 Breaking the California Two-Year Reappointment Trap: `AB 1199` (Signed Sept. 30, 2026; Effective Jan. 1, 2027)

For four years after The Joint Commission extended its maximum medical staff reappointment cycle from two years to **three years** (`CAMH MS.06.01.07` EP 9, calculated to the exact calendar day), California acute-care hospitals remained trapped on a **two-year cycle** by `22 CCR § 70701(a)(7)` and `Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 2282(c), 2453(b)`.

On **September 30, 2026**, Governor Newsom signed **`AB 1199` (Patterson, Chapter 876, Statutes of 2026, [AB 1199 Text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB1199/id/3461195); analyzed in Hanson Bridgett's October 2026 client alert by Glenda Zarbock, Lori Ferguson, and Allison Harvey, [HB AB 1199 Alert](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/insights/AB-1199-medical-staffs-three-year-reappointment-cycle))**, taking effect **January 1, 2027**:

- **Statutory Override of `Title 22`:** `AB 1199` amends `BPC §§ 2282(c)` and `2453(b)` to read *"at least every three years"* and adds **`Health & Saf. Code § 1275.6(e)`**, which expressly bars state regulators (`CDPH` / `HCAI`) from enforcing `22 CCR § 70701(a)(7)` to mandate routine reappointments more frequently than once every three years—immediately unlocking 3-year cycles on January 1, 2027 without waiting years for `CDPH` `Title 22` rulemaking.
- **Three Operational Traps During the 2026–2027 Transition:**
  1. **No Pre-2027 Jump-Start:** Any practitioner whose two-year term expires on or before **December 31, 2026** must still be reappointed within two years; extending a cycle past two years in November or December 2026 violates current `BPC § 2282(c)`.
  2. **Permissive, Not Self-Executing:** `CDPH` and Joint Commission surveyors enforce a hospital's *own* written bylaws. Until the medical staff formally votes to amend its bylaws from "two years" to "up to three years" and secures governing-board approval under `BPC § 2282.5(a)(6)`, granting a three-year term remains a surveyable bylaw violation even after January 1, 2027.
  3. **Cohort Staggering & Exact-Day vs. End-of-Month Counting:** Extending every current member straight to three years creates a "2030 cliff" for Medical Staff Services offices; transition amendments should authorize staggered 24-to-36-month bridge appointments while enforcing Joint Commission **exact-calendar-day** expiration tracking (strictly tighter than `NCQA`'s end-of-calendar-month rule).

## 2. California Peer Review Fair Hearings (`BPC §§ 809–809.9`) and the `805` / `NPDB` Mandatory Reporting Matrix

### 2.1 Threshold Coverage Traps (`> 25` Licentiates, 10 Licentiate Classes, and `BPC § 809.7` Public Exemptions)

Practitioners routinely misstate who sits inside California's peer-review statute (`Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 805–809.9`, [Article 11 Index](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=2.&title=&part=&chapter=1.&article=11.)):

- **The `"More Than 25"` Medical Group / IPA Rule (`BPC § 805(a)(1)(B)(iv)`):** Licensed hospitals, licensed clinics, Medicare-certified ASCs (`42 CFR Part 416`), and Knox-Keene health plans (`HSC § 1340 et seq.`) are covered `"peer review bodies"` regardless of size (`§ 805(a)(1)(B)(i)–(ii)`). By contrast, an unlicensed medical group, foundation-contracted medical group, or IPA qualifies under `§ 805(a)(1)(B)(iv)` **only if it consists of or employs *"more than 25 licentiates of the same class"***—meaning **26 or more** physicians (not 25!), unless performing delegated peer review for a Knox-Keene plan.
- **Exact 10 Covered `"Licentiate"` Classes (`BPC § 805(a)(2)`):** Covers (1) physicians and surgeons (`MD`/`DO` plus `BPC §§ 2113/2168` special permittees), (2) podiatrists (`DPM`), (3) clinical psychologists, (4) `LMFTs`, (5) `LCSWs`, (6) `LPCCs`, (7) dentists (`DDS`/`DMD`), (8) licensed midwives, (9) **physician assistants (`PAs`)**, and (10) **independent/transition Nurse Practitioners practicing under `BPC §§ 2837.103` (`103 NP`) or `2837.104` (`104 NP`)**. Standard RNs, `CRNAs`, and legacy standardized-procedure NPs fall *outside* `§ 805(a)(2)` (unless bylaws voluntarily grant hearing rights), though **all** Division 2 healing-arts licensees fall inside `BPC § 805.8` sexual-misconduct reporting!
- **The `BPC § 809.7` UC Regents & County Hospital Carve-Out (and District Non-Exemption):** State hospitals, county hospitals, and University of California (`Regents`) primary teaching hospitals are **statutorily exempt from `BPC §§ 809.1–809.4`** under `BPC § 809.7` (governed instead by UC/county bylaws and 14th Amendment due process, while remaining bound by `BPC § 805` reporting!). Crucially, **Local Health Care Districts (`HSC § 32000 et seq.`) are NOT exempt under `§ 809.7`** and must provide full `§§ 809.1–809.4` hearings (`HSC § 32150`).

### 2.2 Summary Suspensions (`BPC § 809.5`), Judicial Review Committee (`JRC`) Mechanics, and *Natarajan* / *Mileikowsky*

When an action is recommended for a **"medical disciplinary cause or reason"** (`BPC § 805(a)(6)`: competence or conduct *"reasonably likely to be detrimental to patient safety or to the delivery of patient care"*), `BPC §§ 809.1–809.5` impose unwaivable (`§ 809.6(c)`) procedural guardrails:

1. **Summary Suspension Clocks (`BPC § 809.5(a)–(b)` & `§ 805(e)`):** A peer review body may immediately suspend or restrict privileges without prior hearing *only* where failure to act **"may result in an imminent danger to the health of any individual"** (`§ 809.5(a)`). If no authorized medical staff officer is available, a hospital governing body may impose an emergency suspension after reasonable attempts to contact the `MEC`, **but that suspension terminates automatically by operation of law if not ratified by the peer review body *"within two working days, excluding weekends and holidays"* (`§ 809.5(b)`)**. If a summary suspension is lifted on or before **Day 14**, zero `805` report is filed; the instant it crosses into **Day 15 (`> 14 days`)**, a mandatory `BPC § 805(e)` report must be filed **on Day 15** (within 15 days of Day 0 imposition) *while the JRC hearing is still pending*, and full `§ 809.1` hearing rights vest.
2. **Notice, Dual Discovery Clocks (`30 Days` vs. `10 Days`), and `60-Day` Commencement (`§§ 809.1–809.2`):** Notice of a final proposed action must provide **at least 30 days** to request a hearing (`§ 809.1(b)(3)`), and the hearing must commence **within 60 days** of receipt of the request absent good cause/stipulation (`§ 809.2(h)`). Note the statutory split in discovery: reciprocal inspection/copying of all relevant documentary information triggers a continuance right if withheld inside **30 days** before hearing (`§ 809.2(d)–(e)`), whereas witness lists and hearing exhibits carry a **10-day** statutory floor (`§ 809.2(f)`), frequently lengthened to 15–30 days by hospital bylaws (`§ 809.6(a)`).
3. **Mandatory Burdens of Proof (`§ 809.3(b)`) & *Bichai v. DaVita*:** The peer review body always carries the initial duty to present evidence (`§ 809.3(b)(1)`). Incumbent members win unless the `MEC` proves by a **preponderance of the evidence** that the action is *"reasonable and warranted"* (`§ 809.3(b)(3)`), while initial applicants bear the burden of proving current qualifications by a preponderance (`§ 809.3(b)(2)`). In ***Bichai v. DaVita Inc.*, 72 Cal.App.5th 1126 (2021)**, the Fifth District vacated a privilege denial because facility bylaws unlawfully forced an initial applicant to prove the `MEC`'s recommendation lacked *"any substantial factual basis"*—holding `§ 809.3` burdens are immutable statutory floors.
4. **Strict Attorney Parity at `JRC` Hearings (`BPC § 809.3(c)`):** Outside dental professional societies, ***"No peer review body shall be represented by an attorney if the licentiate is not so represented."*** Even when bylaws allow attorneys, if the accused physician elects to appear without an attorney advocate inside the hearing room, the `MEC` is **statutorily barred** from using an attorney presenter and must present through a physician representative (relying on outside medical-staff counsel solely for behind-the-scenes witness prep, exhibits, and briefs).
5. **Hearing Officer Limits (*Mileikowsky* & *Natarajan*) & Plan Corporate Board Limits (*Lin*):**
   - Under ***Mileikowsky v. West Hills Hospital & Medical Center*, 45 Cal.4th 1259 (2009)**, a non-voting `§ 809.2(b)` Hearing Officer **cannot unilaterally terminate a JRC hearing** for discovery/procedural defaults; only the voting physician JRC panel holds terminating authority.
   - Under ***Natarajan v. Dignity Health*, 11 Cal.5th 1095 (2021)** ([Natarajan Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2021/s259364.html)), the Supreme Court disapproved *Yaqub*'s rigid "appearance of bias" rule and held that under `BPC § 809.2(b)` (*"gain no direct financial benefit from the outcome"*), the mere possibility of future ad hoc hearing-officer appointments at sister hospitals in the same health system does **not** per se disqualify a neutral hearing officer absent context-specific proof of an **"intolerable risk of actual bias"** (reinforced where hospitals adopt contractual 3-year re-appointment moratoria at the same campus).
   - Under ***Lin v. Board of Directors of PrimeCare Medical Network, Inc.*, 108 Cal.App.5th 1163 (Feb. 19, 2025)**, the Fourth District held that `BPC § 809.05` grants narrow appellate-override powers *only* to acute-care hospital governing boards; a Knox-Keene health plan or IPA's lay corporate board **acts without jurisdiction (`CCP § 1094.5(b)`)** if it reverses a physician hearing panel's clinical exoneration!

### 2.3 The Four-Regime Mandatory Reporting Minefield (`BPC §§ 805`, `805.01`, `805.8` vs. Federal `NPDB`)

Hospital CEOs and Chiefs of Staff face personal and institutional penalties up to **`$100,000` for willful failure** and **`$50,000` for non-willful failure** (`BPC §§ 805(k)–(l), 805.01(e)–(f), 805.8(d)–(e)`) plus **3-year forfeiture of federal `HCQIA` immunity** (`45 CFR § 60.12(c)`) across four non-identical reporting regimes:

- **Regime 1 — Standard `BPC § 805` (`15-Calendar-Day` Post-Finality or Post-Notice-Resignation Rule):** Filed within **15 calendar days** ([MBC Reporting Portal](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Resources/Medical-Resources/Health-Facility-Reporting.aspx)). Per **California Attorney General Opinion No. 15-301 (`103 Ops.Cal.Atty.Gen. 27`, Apr. 17, 2020, [AG Op. 15-301 PDF](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/opinions/pdfs/15-301.pdf))**, standard adverse actions under `§ 805(b)`—denial/rejection, revocation, or restrictions totaling **`>= 30` cumulative days in any 12-month period** (meaning three separate 10-day restrictions trigger an `805`!)—are reported within 15 days after the **final post-hearing decision**, whereas summary suspensions `> 14 days` (`§ 805(e)`) and resignations, leaves of absence, or non-renewals **after receiving notice of a pending investigation or impending denial (`§ 805(c)`)** fire within 15 days immediately.
- **Regime 2 — Pre-Hearing Egregious Conduct `BPC § 805.01` (`15-Day` Early-Warning Rule):** Must be filed **within 15 calendar days** after the `MEC` makes a final decision or recommendation following a formal investigation finding any of **four egregious categories**—(1) incompetence/gross negligence involving **death or serious bodily injury**, (2) practicing impaired / substance abuse, (3) repeated excessive or unexamined controlled-substance prescribing, or (4) patient sexual misconduct—**"regardless of whether a hearing is held pursuant to Section 809.2"** (`§ 805.01(b)`). Filing an `805.01` immediately empowers the Medical Board (`§ 805.01(c)`) to inspect and copy all underlying investigative charts, exhibits, and opinions!
- **Regime 3 — Zero-Investigation Written Patient Sexual-Misconduct Allegations (`BPC § 805.8` / `SB 425`):** Sweeps far broader than `§ 805`, binding *every* health facility, clinic, group, or university and covering *all* Division 2 healing-arts licensees (including RNs, PTs, respiratory therapists, pharmacists). Whenever a **patient or patient's representative** submits a **written allegation** (including a portal message, email, or grievance form) of sexual abuse or sexual misconduct, the entity **must report to the licensing board within 15 calendar days of receipt—before conducting any investigation or making any credibility finding (`§ 805.8(b)`)**!
- **Regime 4 — Federal `NPDB` (`45 CFR §§ 60.5, 60.12` & *Doe v. Leavitt* Asymmetric Traps):** Reported within **30 calendar days** ([NPDB Guidebook Ch. E](https://www.npdb.hrsa.gov/guidebook/EClinicalPrivileges.jsp)), mandatory for physicians and dentists (permissive for `PAs`/`NPs` on hospital privilege actions). Three acute traps trip up California counsel every month:
  1. **The Unnoticed Investigation Resignation Trap (*Doe v. Leavitt*, 552 F.3d 75 (1st Cir. 2009)):** Under California `BPC § 805(c)`, a resignation during an investigation is reportable *only if the physician had received notice* of the pending investigation. Under federal **`45 CFR § 60.12(a)(1)(ii)`** and *Doe v. Leavitt*, **the physician's awareness of the investigation is irrelevant**—if an `MEC` opens a targeted, practitioner-specific quality inquiry (beyond routine `OPPE`) and the unaware physician resigns or lets privileges expire, **an `NPDB` report is mandatory while a California `805(c)` report is forbidden/inapplicable**! Conversely, withdrawing an *initial* application after notice of impending denial triggers California `§ 805(c)(2)` while generally not triggering `NPDB`.
  2. **Summary Suspension & `30-Day` Boundary Divergence:** A 20-day summary suspension lifted on Day 20 requires a California `805(e)` report (`> 14 days`) and **zero** `NPDB` report (`<= 30 days`); an exactly 30-day restriction triggers California `805(b)(3)` (`>= 30 days`) and **zero** `NPDB` report (`> 30` consecutive days required).
  3. **The `FPPE` Proctoring Trap:** Retrospective chart review or concurrent observation where a proctor merely observes without gating permission to operate is **non-reportable**; requiring **prior case approval or physical presence of a proctor in the OR** before operating for `> 30 days` for competence concerns is a **mandatory reportable restriction** to both `MBC` and `NPDB` regardless of whether bylaws call it "educational proctoring."

### 2.4 2024–2026 MBC Sunset Enforcement (`SB 815`), Shield Laws (`BPC § 805.9` / `AB 260`), and Dr. Lorna Breen Mental-Health Reforms (`AB 2164`)

Three recent legislative waves directly govern credentialing intake and parallel board defense:

- **`SB 815` MBC Sunset Overhaul (`Stats. 2023, ch. 294`, eff. Jan. 1, 2024):** Extended the Medical Board to 2028, created the **Complainant Liaison Unit (`BPC § 2220.09`)** with mandatory pre-closure family/complainant interview offers in death/serious-injury cases (`§ 2220.08`), made a physician's failure to attend an MBC investigative interview within **30 calendar days** standalone unprofessional conduct (`BPC § 2234(g)`), enforced `$1,000/day` (`$10,000` cap) record-subpoena penalties on both physicians and hospitals (`BPC § 2225.5`), and extended license-reinstatement waiting periods from 3 years to **5 years** (`BPC § 2307(b)(1)`).
- **Reproductive & Gender-Affirming Credentialing Shields (`AB 1707` / `BPC § 805.9`, `SB 345`, `SB 487` & Sept. 2025 `AB 260` / `AB 82`):** Enacted via **`AB 1707` (`Bus. & Prof. Code § 805.9` & `Health & Saf. Code §§ 1220.1, 1265.11`)** and **`SB 487` (`HSC § 1375.61`)**, California bars hospitals, clinics, and health plans from denying, restricting, or revoking staff privileges based on out-of-state judgments, convictions, or discipline arising from reproductive or gender-affirming care lawful in California—expanded on **September 26, 2025 by urgency bill `AB 260` (`BPC §§ 687, 850.3`; `HSC § 1265.12`)** (shielding off-label/WHO-protocol medication abortion even if federal FDA `REMS` tighten) and **`AB 82`** (removing testosterone and mifepristone from `CURES` reporting). Hospital credentialing questionnaires must carve out protected out-of-state shield disclosures from automatic "prior discipline" disqualifiers.
- **Replacing Intrusive Mental-Health Questions (`AB 2164` / `BPC § 2090`, eff. Jan. 1, 2025):** Aligning with the **Dr. Lorna Breen Heroes' Foundation** and Joint Commission `MS.06.01.05` guidance, `AB 2164` barred the Medical Board from asking licensure/renewal applicants about past mental-health or substance-use diagnoses/treatment that do **not currently impair** safe practice (absent board-mandated disciplinary orders). To avoid `ADA` (`42 U.S.C. § 12112(d)`), `FEHA`, and `Unruh Act` exposure, California hospitals must scrub legacy *"Have you ever been treated for depression, anxiety, or substance abuse?"* questions from initial and reappointment applications in favor of a single, time-present functional-impairment question.

## 3. Judicial Review (`CCP § 1094.5`), Whistleblower Retaliation (`HSC § 1278.5`), Anti-SLAPP (*Bonni*), Immunities, Antitrust, and *Elam* / `AB 35` `MICRA` Stacking

### 3.1 Two-Step *Westlake* Exhaustion, `CCP § 1094.5(d)` Substantial-Evidence Review, and the 2024–2026 Appellate Canon

For fifty years since ***Westlake Community Hospital v. Superior Court*, 17 Cal.3d 465 (1976)** ([Westlake Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/17/465.html)), California has enforced a dual gate blocking aggrieved clinicians from leaping directly into civil tort lawsuits:

1. **Step 1 (Internal Exhaustion) & Step 2 (Judicial Mandamus Exhaustion):** Unless a hospital denies privileges without affording any quasi-judicial hearing right whatsoever, a physician must **first** exhaust internal `BPC §§ 809.1–809.4` `JRC` and governing-board appellate remedies (*Eight Unnamed Physicians v. Medical Executive Com.*, 150 Cal.App.4th 503 (2007)) and **second** prevail on a Superior Court **petition for writ of administrative mandate (`Code Civ. Proc. § 1094.5`)** setting aside the hospital's final decision before filing any civil action for tort or contract damages.
2. **Deferential Substantial-Evidence Standard (`Code Civ. Proc. § 1094.5(d)`):** After the Legislature overturned *Anton I* (`Stats. 1978, ch. 1348`) and subsequently added Local Health Care Districts (`Stats. 1992, ch. 72`) and municipal hospitals (`Stats. 2000, ch. 402`), **`CCP § 1094.5(d)`** ([CCP § 1094.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1094.5.)) restricts judicial review of private, healthcare-district, and municipal hospital boards (plus `UC Regents` constitutional-agency decisions under *Miklosy*) strictly to whether findings are supported by **substantial evidence in the light of the whole record** (reserving independent-judgment reweighing solely for `HSC § 1316` class-discrimination claims, or vested-privilege county hospital cases outside `§ 1094.5(d)`). Meanwhile, procedural "fair trial" claims (`§ 1094.5(b)`) are reviewed *de novo* on undisputed facts, subject to *El-Attar*'s rule (`56 Cal.4th at 990–97`) that technical bylaw departures do not invalidate peer review absent material prejudice.
3. **Landmark 2024–2026 California Court of Appeal Holdings Reshaping Defense Strategy:**
   - ***Asiryan v. Medical Staff of Glendale Adventist Medical Center*, 100 Cal.App.5th 947 (Mar. 25, 2024)** ([Asiryan Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2024/b316313.html)): Extending *Boermeester v. Carry*, 15 Cal.5th 72 (2023), the Second District squarely held that **`BPC §§ 805–809.9` completely occupy the field and abolish standalone common-law "fair procedure" claims in hospital peer review**, barring physicians from demanding pre-suspension notice beyond `§ 809.5` and affirming defense `§ 809.9` attorney's fees.
   - ***Sujan v. UHS Corona, Inc.*, __ Cal.App.5th __, 2026 WL 2730281 (Sept. 16, 2026)** ([Sujan 2026 Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/e084185.html); see also earlier `§ 809.9` ruling at `101 Cal.App.5th 1038` (2024)): Summarily suspended after a patient death, Dr. Sujan signed a conditional reinstatement agreement prior to Day 15 expressly to avoid a `BPC § 805(e)` Medical Board report, then resigned and sued Corona Regional Medical Center and colleagues for "sham peer review." Affirming summary judgment and upholding a **$313,830 contractual attorney's-fee award under the Medical Staff Bylaws** (holding contractual fee-shifting in bylaws is *not* preempted by `BPC § 809.9`!), the Fourth District held that bargaining for a conditional pre-Day-15 settlement and walking away does **not** trigger any futility exception to *Westlake* exhaustion.

### 3.2 Physician Whistleblower Retaliation (`HSC § 1278.5`), *Fahlen*, *Shaw*, and *Armin*

Because *Westlake* and `CCP § 1094.5(d)` are nearly insurmountable for disciplined physicians, virtually every modern California peer-review lawsuit pleads **Health & Safety Code § 1278.5** ([HSC § 1278.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=1278.5.)):

- **Bypassing *Westlake* Mandamus Exhaustion (*Fahlen*) & Running Concurrent Proceedings (*Armin*):** In ***Fahlen v. Sutter Central Valley Hospitals*, 58 Cal.4th 655 (2014)** ([Fahlen Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2014/s205568.html)), the California Supreme Court held that a physician alleging hospital privilege restriction or termination was retaliatory for reporting unsafe patient care under `HSC § 1278.5` **need NOT prevail on a `CCP § 1094.5` writ** before suing in Superior Court. Extending *Fahlen*, ***Armin v. Riverside Community Hospital*, 5 Cal.App.5th 810 (2016)** held that the physician need not even finish an ongoing internal `§ 809` JRC hearing before filing suit—**while simultaneously holding that individual physicians, `MEC` officers, and peer-review committee members CANNOT be sued personally under `HSC § 1278.5`** (only the health facility entity faces `§ 1278.5` liability!).
- **The `120-Day` (Not `180-Day`) Rebuttable Presumption (`HSC § 1278.5(d)(1)`):** Plaintiffs routinely misquote `§ 1278.5(c)`'s 180-day window (which applies *solely* to retaliation against *patients*). For medical staff members and employees, **`HSC § 1278.5(d)(1)`** creates a **120-day** rebuttable presumption (`Evid. Code § 604` burden of producing evidence) if responsible decision-makers knew of the patient-safety grievance and took adverse privilege or contract action within 120 calendar days.
- **No Jury Trial Right on Standalone `HSC § 1278.5` (*Shaw v. Superior Court*, 2 Cal.5th 983 (2017))—and How Plaintiffs Reach a Jury:** Verified current through November 2026, the Legislature has never overturned ***Shaw v. Superior Court*, 2 Cal.5th 983 (2017)** ([Shaw Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2017/s230230.html)): direct `HSC § 1278.5(g)` actions remain **equitable bench trials** (with prevailing-plaintiff fee-shifting). Because California's `CPOM` bar means most private-practice hospital medical staff members are **not** hospital employees, they cannot append a common-law *Tameny* wrongful-termination claim (*Sistare-Meyer*, 58 Cal.App.4th 10) or `Lab. Code § 1102.5` employee whistleblower claim against the hospital. By contrast, **physicians employed by `BPC § 2401` medical foundations, Permanente/Sutter/affiliated medical groups, `FQHCs`, or county/UC systems CAN obtain a jury trial** (plus `Lab. Code § 1102.5(j)` unilateral attorney's fees and `§ 1102.6` clear-and-convincing defense burdens under *Lawson v. PPG*, 12 Cal.5th 703) against their employing entity, or invoke **`Bus. & Prof. Code § 2056(c)`** (*Khajavi v. Feather River Anesthesia Medical Group*, 84 Cal.App.4th 32 (2000), protecting independent-contractor physician patient advocacy, though `§ 2056` lacks *Fahlen*'s *Westlake* exemption as to hospital privilege revocations).

### 3.3 Post-*Bonni* Surgical Anti-SLAPP Strategy (`CCP § 425.16`) & the `Civ. Code § 47(b)` Prong-2 Kill Shot

For fifteen years after *Kibler v. Northern Inyo County Local Hospital Dist.*, 39 Cal.4th 192 (2006), hospital defense counsel moved under `CCP § 425.16` to strike entire physician retaliation complaints. In ***Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (*Bonni I*), 11 Cal.5th 995 (2021)** ([Bonni I Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2021/s244148.html)), the California Supreme Court drew an exact act-by-act boundary under Prong 1 (`§ 425.16(e)(2)`):

- **What Fails Prong 1 (Non-Communicative Disciplinary Decisions):** The actual **summary suspension, restriction, or revocation of clinical privileges** or termination of a contract is **not** protected speech—even though reached by committee vote following protected peer-review debate. Filing an overbroad motion to strike those core disciplinary claims loses on Prong 1.
- **What Wins Prong 1 AND Wins Prong 2 as a Matter of Law (*Bonni II*, *Wisner*, & *Mounts*):** Every **communicative peer-review act**—filing mandatory `BPC § 805` or `NPDB` reports, `MEC`/`JRC` statements, peer-review initiation notices, `FPPE` evaluations, settlement negotiations, and credentialing reference responses—satisfies Prong 1 under *Bonni I*. Then, on remand in ***Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (*Bonni II*), 83 Cal.App.5th 288, 301–07 (2022)** ([Bonni II Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2022/g052367a.html)), reinforced in ***Wisner v. Dignity Health*, 85 Cal.App.5th 35 (2022)** and ***Dignity Health v. Mounts*, 105 Cal.App.5th 113 (Sept. 17, 2024)** ([Mounts Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2024/b325563-0.html)), the Courts of Appeal held that **`HSC § 1278.5` does NOT abrogate `Civ. Code § 47(b)`'s absolute official-proceeding privilege (or `§ 47(c)`'s common-interest credentialing privilege)**! Thus, targeted *Baral*/*Bonni* surgical anti-SLAPP motions cleanly excise every `805`/`NPDB`-reporting, defamation, and `FPPE`-communication claim from a whistleblower complaint at the pleading stage while recovering mandatory partial defense fees (`CCP § 425.16(c)(1)`).

### 3.4 The Immunity, Antitrust, and Fee-Shifting Matrix (`HCQIA`, `Civ. Code § 43.7(e)` Hospital Gap, Ninth Circuit *Oltz*, and `BPC § 809.9`)

Defense counsel must layer four overlapping statutory shields—because each contains a fatal hole if relied upon alone:

- **Federal `HCQIA` (`42 U.S.C. §§ 11111(a), 11112(a), 11113`):** Grants **both the hospital entity and individual reviewers** rebuttable-presumption immunity from federal and state **monetary damages** (measured purely **objectively** under *Austin v. McNamara*, 979 F.2d 728 (9th Cir. 1992), regardless of alleged personal animus) plus **one-way pro-defendant fee-shifting (`§ 11113`)**. *Critical Holes:* `HCQIA` **never** bars `CCP § 1094.5` writs/injunctions, **expressly excludes civil-rights claims (`42 U.S.C. §§ 1981, 1983`, Title VII, ADA, `FEHA`, `Unruh`)**, and does not cover purely administrative/economic actions unrelated to clinical competence (`§ 11151(9)`).
- **The California `Civ. Code § 43.7(e)` Hospital Carve-Out & *Hassan* Malice Rule (`§ 43.8`):** While `Civ. Code § 43.7(b)` grants qualified immunity (absent malice) to peer-review committee *members*, **`Civ. Code § 43.7(e)` expressly states that `§ 43.7` *"shall not be construed to confer immunity from liability on any... hospital"***! Meanwhile, `Civ. Code § 43.8` informant immunity is only **qualified** and defeated by malice (*Hassan v. Mercy American River Hospital*, 31 Cal.4th 709 (2003))—making `HCQIA` (`§ 11111(a)`) and `Civ. Code § 47(b)` essential to protect the hospital entity itself.
- **Ninth Circuit Rejection of Blanket *Copperweld* Antitrust Immunity (*Oltz* & *American Needle*):** Under *Patrick v. Burget*, 486 U.S. 94 (1988), private peer review lacks state-action antitrust immunity because `CCP § 1094.5` review is not "active state supervision" (whereas Local Health Care Districts enjoy **damages** immunity under the Local Government Antitrust Act, `15 U.S.C. §§ 34–36`). Crucially, unlike the Third, Fourth, and Sixth Circuits, **the Ninth Circuit in *Oltz v. St. Peter's Community Hospital*, 861 F.2d 1440, 1450 (9th Cir. 1988) and *Pinhas v. Summit Health, Ltd.*, 894 F.2d 1024 (9th Cir. 1989) (reinforced by *American Needle v. NFL*, 560 U.S. 183 (2010)) holds that independent-contractor medical staff physicians are separate economic actors capable of conspiring under Sherman Act § 1 and California's Cartwright Act (`BPC § 16720`)**! Moreover, **`Welf. & Inst. Code § 14087.28`** bars Medi-Cal contracting hospitals from pure "economic credentialing" unrelated to individual competence (preserving *Mateo-Woodburn* closed-department contracts and governance conflict-of-interest recusals for competing `*Capen*-compliant` ASC owners, while stripping `HCQIA` immunity if peer review is weaponized purely to punish outpatient competitors).
- **Bilateral `BPC § 809.9` vs. Bylaw Fee-Shifting (*Mir*, *Smith*, & *Sujan*):** Unlike one-way federal `42 U.S.C. § 11113`, California `BPC § 809.9` awards fees to *either* prevailing side for frivolous, unreasonable, unfounded, or bad-faith conduct (*Smith v. Selma Community Hospital*, 188 Cal.App.4th 1 (2010), affirming $117k+ against hospital; contrast *Mir v. Charter Suburban Hospital*, 27 Cal.App.4th 1471 (1994), holding losing on close substantial-evidence review does not trigger fees)—**provided** the underlying action was actually reportable under `BPC § 805` (*Sujan*, 101 Cal.App.5th 1038 (2024)).

### 3.5 Hospital Negligent Credentialing (*Elam*) & Why `AB 35`'s 2026 `MICRA` Cap Stacking ($940k / $1.30M) Supercharged Plaintiff Claims

Under ***Elam v. College Park Hospital*, 132 Cal.App.3d 332 (1982)**, a hospital owes patients a direct corporate duty to screen and periodically evaluate medical staff competence. Under ***Bell v. Sharp Cabrillo Hospital*, 212 Cal.App.3d 1034 (1989)**, an *Elam* claim constitutes "professional negligence" governed by **`MICRA` (`Civ. Code § 3333.2`)**.

Prior to 2023, all defendants shared a single `$250,000` noneconomic cap (*Yates v. Pollock*, 194 Cal.App.3d 195), giving medical-malpractice plaintiffs zero extra noneconomic upside from pleading *Elam* alongside an insured surgeon. **`AB 35` (`Civ. Code § 3333.2(b)–(c), (i)`, effective Jan. 1, 2023)** radically altered *Elam* economics by creating **up to three stackable noneconomic cap buckets** governed by the dollar schedule **in effect at the time of judgment, arbitration award, or settlement (`§ 3333.2(i)`)**:

- **Exact Verified 2026 `MICRA` Cap Schedule (Jan. 1, 2026 – Dec. 31, 2026):**
  - **Non-Wrongful Death Injury (`§ 3333.2(b)` — `$350k` base + `$40k/yr` through 2033):** **`$470,000`** single bucket (`2026`); **`$940,000`** two-bucket stack (Surgeon in Bucket 1 `[§ 3333.2(b)(1)]` + Hospital via *Elam* or nursing negligence in Bucket 2 `[§ 3333.2(b)(2)]`); **`$1,410,000`** three-bucket maximum (adding unaffiliated facility/transport in Bucket 3 `[§ 3333.2(b)(3)]`). *(Stepping up on January 1, 2027 to `$510,000` / `$1,020,000` / `$1,530,000`!)*
  - **Wrongful Death (`§ 3333.2(c)` — `$500k` base + `$50k/yr` through 2033):** **`$650,000`** single bucket (`2026`); **`$1,300,000`** two-bucket stack (Surgeon + Hospital *Elam*); **`$1,950,000`** three-bucket maximum. *(Stepping up on January 1, 2027 to `$700,000` / `$1,400,000` / `$2,100,000`!)*
- **The *Elam* Discovery Paradox (`Evid. Code § 1157`):** Because `AB 35` **doubles** noneconomic recovery (`$470k -> $940k` injury; `$650k -> $1.30M` death in 2026) when a plaintiff establishes independent hospital liability where hospital nurses did nothing wrong, plaintiffs aggressively plead *Elam* on pure surgical errors. Yet in state court, `Evid. Code § 1157(a)` (*Alexander v. Superior Court*, 5 Cal.4th 1218 (1993) [credentialing applications protected]; *Santa Rosa Memorial Hospital*, 174 Cal.App.3d 711 (1985)) blocks plaintiffs from discovering the `MEC`/Credentials file—forcing plaintiffs to prove *Elam* via public `MBC` accusations, prior malpractice dockets, or administrative failures to query `NPDB` or maintain `AB 1199`/bylaw reappointment currency!

## 4. Two High-Stakes Procedural Cliffs: The Federal `FRE 501` Privilege Cliff (`PSQIA` Solution) and Public Healthcare District Hearings (`HSC § 32155`)

### 4.1 The Federal Question Discovery Cliff (*Agster* / `FRE 501`) and Structuring a Federal `PSQIA` (`42 U.S.C. § 299b-22`) Shield

The single costliest litigation mistake in California hospital peer review is assuming `Evid. Code § 1157` protects committee files in federal court:

- **Instant Evaporation of `Evid. Code § 1157` Under `Fed. R. Evid. 501` (*Agster*):** Under `FRE 501` and binding Ninth Circuit precedent in ***Agster v. Maricopa County*, 422 F.3d 836, 839–40 (9th Cir. 2005)** ([Agster Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/f3/422/836/582697/); *accord Virmani v. Novant Health Inc.*, 259 F.3d 284 (4th Cir. 2001)), federal common law recognizes **zero medical peer-review privilege**. Worse, whenever a plaintiff pleads **even one federal claim (`28 U.S.C. § 1331`)**—such as Sherman Act § 1 antitrust (*Pagano v. Oroville Hospital*, 145 F.R.D. 683), `42 U.S.C. §§ 1981` or `1983` civil rights (*Chaudhry v. Smith*, E.D. Cal. 2020), Title VII/`ADA` + pendent `HSC § 1278.5` (*Love v. Permanente Medical Group*, N.D. Cal. 2013), `EMTALA` (`42 U.S.C. § 1395dd`), or False Claims Act `31 U.S.C. § 3730(h)` retaliation—**`Evid. Code § 1157` evaporates across the entire federal lawsuit, including all supplemental California state-law claims**! Removing a physician suit with a federal hook from Superior Court to U.S. District Court hands plaintiff's counsel 10–20 years of comparator peer-review files.
- **How to Build a True Federal Privilege via the Patient Safety and Quality Improvement Act (`PSQIA`, `42 U.S.C. §§ 299b-21–299b-22`, `42 CFR Part 3`):** Hospitals can shield root-cause analyses, morbidity/mortality reviews, and clinical quality evaluations in **both federal and state court** by establishing a written **Patient Safety Evaluation System (`PSES`)** that collects and reports **Patient Safety Work Product (`PSWP`)** to an AHRQ-listed **Patient Safety Organization (`PSO`)**. *Critical Statutory Carve-Out (`42 U.S.C. § 299b-21(7)(B)(ii)`):* Information collected strictly to satisfy **external state/federal reporting mandates** (such as `CDPH` adverse-event reports under `HSC § 1279.1` or `BPC § 805` reports) or original patient medical charts **cannot** become `PSWP` merely by dropping copies into the `PSES`, and formal disciplinary `JRC` hearing exhibits removed from the `PSES` before reporting require careful dual-track architecture so quality-improvement deliberations stay inside the `PSO` vault while formal `§ 809` evidentiary exhibits remain cleanly segregated.

### 4.2 Public Healthcare District Peer Review (`HSC §§ 32128, 32150–32155`), Open-Hearing Demands, and *Sahlolbei* (`Gov't Code § 1090`)

Representing California's ~79 Local Health Care Districts (`HSC § 32000 et seq.`) adds four public-law overlays absent in private nonprofit hospitals:

1. **Statutory Subpoena & Contempt Teeth (`HSC § 32154`):** Unlike private hospital `JRCs` (which lack third-party civil subpoena power under `BPC § 809.2`), **`HSC § 32154`** grants a Healthcare District board or its appointed hearing officer full Administrative Procedure Act subpoena and *subpoena duces tecum* enforcement and contempt powers (`Gov't Code § 11450.10 et seq.`). *(Practitioner note: active subpoena authority sits in `HSC § 32154`; legacy citations to `§§ 32152–32153` in pre-1990 cases reference historical unpopulated code slots.)*
2. **The Physician's Unilateral Right to Demand a PUBLIC Brown Act Hearing (`HSC § 32155`):** While `Gov't Code § 54962` and **`HSC § 32155`** exempt District peer-review and quality hearings from the Ralph M. Brown Act, `HSC § 32155`'s proviso grants the subject physician an explicit right to **request a public hearing**! When a physician plays this tactical card to pressure a District Board: (a) under the second sentence of `§ 32155`, **District Board deliberations still remain strictly in closed executive session**; (b) because the physician cannot waive *patients'* `HIPAA` (`45 CFR § 164.514(b)`) or `CMIA` (`Civ. Code § 56.10`) privacy rights, all public-session exhibits and testimony must use 18-identifier Safe-Harbor de-identification (*"Patient A"*) with unredacted charts shielded under `CPRA` `Gov't Code §§ 7927.700, 7927.705`; and (c) underlying committee files not introduced into open session retain `Evid. Code § 1157` immunity.
3. **Fourteenth Amendment "Stigma-Plus" & First Amendment `§ 1983` Exposure (*Ulrich*):** Because Healthcare Districts (along with County and UC hospitals) are state actors, adverse clinical actions paired with `805`/`NPDB` filings implicate Fourteenth Amendment property and "stigma-plus" liberty interests and First Amendment public-concern speech under **`42 U.S.C. § 1983`** (*Ulrich v. City & County of San Francisco*, 308 F.3d 968 (9th Cir. 2002))—claims completely exempt from `HCQIA` (`42 U.S.C. § 11111(a)(1)`) and state immunities!
4. **Felony `Gov't Code § 1090` (*Sahlolbei*) Traps on District `MEC` Officers & Department Chairs:** Under ***People v. Superior Court (Sahlolbei)*, 3 Cal.5th 230 (2017)**, independent-contractor medical staff officers and department chairs at a Healthcare District are covered `"officers/employees"` under `Gov't Code § 1090` whenever advising the District on physician contracts. Crucially, **`HSC § 32111`'s narrow exception fails on exclusive department contracts and bespoke chair/call stipends** (`§ 32111(b)(1)` requires identical terms/caps across multiple staff contracts, and *any* preliminary RFP/budget input violates `§ 32111(a)(1)`), exposing tainted contracts to voidness *ab initio* and **100% fee disgorgement without quantum meruit offset (`Gov't Code § 1092`)**!

## 5. Exclusive Contracts (*Economy v. Sutter*), Late-Career Screening (*Yale New Haven* Oct. 2026 Trial Order), Disruptive Clinicians, and `104 NP` / `PA` / `CRNA` / Telemedicine / AI Privileging

### 5.1 Exclusive Hospital-Based Contracts ("Clean Sweep") and the $3.87M Lesson of *Economy v. Sutter East Bay Hospitals*

For decades under ***Mateo-Woodburn v. Fresno Community Hospital & Medical Center*, 221 Cal.App.3d 1169 (1990)** and ***Abrams v. St. John's Hospital & Health Center*, 25 Cal.App.4th 628 (1994)**, California hospitals relied on quasi-legislative department closures and contractual "clean sweep" clauses in anesthesiology, emergency medicine, radiology, pathology, hospitalist, and neonatology exclusive agreements to terminate group-affiliated physicians' clinical privileges automatically upon contract expiration or group separation without affording `BPC § 809` hearings.

In ***Economy v. Sutter East Bay Hospitals*, 31 Cal.App.5th 1147 (2019)** ([Economy Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a150211.html)), hospital administration crossed a fatal statutory line:

- **The $3,867,122 Judgment (Including $650,910 Tax-Neutralization Gross-Up):** After an unannounced `CDPH` survey flagged fentanyl documentation deficiencies by anesthesiologist Dr. Kenneth Economy, Sutter's Alta Bates Summit administration skipped the `MEC` and invoked Section 5.4 of its exclusive contract with East Bay Anesthesiology Medical Group (`EBAMG`), instructing `EBAMG` to pull Dr. Economy off the hospital schedule pending continuing education; `EBAMG` then terminated him when he refused to resign. Affirming a **$3,867,122** judgment against Sutter—including a landmark **$650,910 tax-neutralization gross-up** offsetting the marginal income-tax penalty of a lump-sum lost-earnings award—the First District held:
  1. **No Outsourcing of `BPC § 809`:** Whenever a hospital directs, demands, or pressures an exclusive contracting group to remove an *individual* physician from hospital practice for competence, charting, safety, or conduct (`BPC § 805(a)(6)`), that directive is the functional equivalent of suspending or revoking privileges, triggering mandatory `BPC §§ 809.1–809.4` notice/hearing rights and `BPC § 805` reporting.
  2. **`BPC § 809.6(c)` Voids Contractual Waivers:** Under `BPC § 809.6(c)`, statutory hearing rights for `805`-reportable actions **cannot be waived** in medical staff bylaws or exclusive group contracts.
  3. **No Duty to Mitigate Under Un-Adjudicated Taint:** A physician removed on un-adjudicated patient-safety accusations without a `JRC` hearing has no duty to mitigate damages by applying to other hospitals where credentialing forms would force self-reporting of the tainted removal.

### 5.2 Late-Career Practitioner Screening After the October 23, 2026 *EEOC v. Yale New Haven Hospital* Summary-Judgment Denial

Across the country, hospitals watching aging surgical workforces adopted mandatory age-70 or age-72 neuropsychological and ophthalmologic screening policies. **Fact-Check Correction Verified Through November 2026:** Contrary to persistent industry rumors conflating separate health-system settlements, ***EEOC v. Yale New Haven Hospital, Inc.*, No. 3:20-cv-00187 (D. Conn.) has NOT settled**. On **October 23, 2026 (`Dkt. No. 343`, [YNHH SJ Order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/connecticut/ctdce/3:2020cv00187/137965/343/))**, U.S. District Judge Kari A. Dooley **denied cross-motions for summary judgment** and ordered a federal **jury trial** on the EEOC's `ADEA` (`29 U.S.C. § 623`) and `ADA` (`42 U.S.C. § 12112(d)(4)(A)`) claims—ruling that genuine factual disputes exist as to common-law "employee" status, whether blanket age-70 testing satisfies `ADEA`'s narrow `BFOQ` defense (*Criswell*), and whether non-individualized cognitive screening is "job-related and consistent with business necessity" under the `ADA`.

In California, blanket age-70 screening policies carry **substantially higher legal exposure than in Connecticut** because California law closes the federal "independent contractor" escape hatch across four statutes:
1. **`FEHA` Anti-Harassment Protection for Independent Contractors (`Gov't Code § 12940(j)(1)`):** Explicitly protects *"a person providing services pursuant to a contract"* against age (`40+`) or disability/perceived-disability harassment.
2. **Unruh Civil Rights Act (`Civ. Code §§ 51, 51.5, 52(a)`) & *Payne v. Anaheim Memorial Medical Center*, 130 Cal.App.4th 729 (2005):** Treats a California hospital as a "business establishment"vis-à-vis non-employee staff physicians, incorporating any `ADA` violation as an automatic Unruh violation (`Civ. Code § 51(f)`) carrying statutory/treble damages and prevailing-plaintiff fees—without *Westlake* mandamus exhaustion where no evidentiary hearing applies!
3. **Employed Foundation / Academic / County Clinicians (`Gov't Code § 12940(e)–(f)`):** Directly governed by `FEHA`'s strict business-necessity bar on medical/psychological examinations and California's broader "limits" disability standard (`Gov't Code § 12926.1`).
4. **Competence-Only Licensure Mandate (`BPC § 2282(b)` & `22 CCR § 70715`):** Restricts privilege determinations to demonstrated individual competence, judgment, and performance.

**2026 California Best Practice:** Repeal standalone chronological-age (`70+`) cognitive-testing policies immediately. Fold physical and cognitive fitness triggers into **age-neutral, universal `OPPE` (`TJC MS.08.01.03`) and `FPPE` protocols** governing *every* practitioner from age 30 to age 85, requiring specialized neuropsychological or fitness-for-duty evaluation *only* upon documented individualized clinical or behavioral indicators routed through the confidential **Medical Staff Wellbeing Committee (`22 CCR § 70703(d)`)**.

### 5.3 Disruptive Clinicians vs. Protected Advocacy ("Manner vs. Message") and Post-Diversion Wellbeing Committees

Because California's Medical Board Physician Diversion Program sunsetted permanently in 2008, the hospital **Medical Staff Wellbeing Committee (`22 CCR § 70703(d)`**, operating under **California Public Protection & Physician Health [`CPPPH`]** guidelines) serves as the sole confidential rehabilitation harbor for M.D.s in California. Voluntary self-referrals, monitoring agreements, and medical leaves initiated *prior* to any `MEC` disciplinary investigation remain shielded by `Evid. Code § 1157(a)` and trigger neither `BPC § 805` nor `NPDB` reporting—provided privileges are not involuntarily restricted `> 14 days`, the clinician does not resign after notice of an `MEC` investigation (`§ 805(c)`), and no post-investigation `§ 805.01(b)(2)` impaired-practice finding occurs.

Simultaneously, when Joint Commission `LD.03.01.01` disruptive-behavior complaints collide with physician complaints about nurse staffing or broken OR equipment (`HSC § 1278.5`), medical staffs must enforce strict **"Manner vs. Message" Bifurcation**: (1) log, investigate, and answer the substantive **patient-safety message** in writing through Quality/Risk Management, while (2) addressing interpersonal **manner** (profanity, throwing instruments, intimidation) separately through progressive peer coaching (*Vanderbilt "Cup of Coffee"* model) so the record refutes `HSC § 1278.5(d)(1)`'s 120-day retaliation presumption.

### 5.4 Privileging `104 NPs`, `PAs` (`SB 697`), `CRNAs` (*CANA v. CDPH*), Telemedicine by Proxy, and AI-Guided Robotics

Four non-physician and technology credentialing fronts require immediate 2026–2027 bylaws updates:

- **Independent `104 NPs` (`BPC § 2837.104` / `SB 1451`, Live January 1, 2026):** Three years after `103 NP` certifications began in January 2023 (and following `SB 1451` cleanup amendments effective Jan. 1, 2025, [HB SB 1451 Alert](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/insights/SB-1451-requirements-103-104-nurse-practitioners)), California's first **`104 NPs`** became eligible on **January 1, 2026** for **organized medical staff membership (`BPC § 2837.104(a)(2)`)** and **departmental voting rights (`§ 2837.104(a)(3)`)** without standardized procedures. Crucially, `§ 2837.104(f)–(g)` mandates that any `104 NP` practicing inside a licensed hospital or clinic **must comply with the facility's medical staff and governing-body bylaws**, and `CDPH` maintains that `Title 22` (`22 CCR §§ 70703(b), 70706.1` & `42 CFR § 482.12(c)`) still preserves hospital bylaw control over inpatient admitting co-management. Furthermore, remember that `103` and `104 NPs` **are statutory `"licentiates"` under `BPC § 805(a)(2)`**, entitling them to full `BPC § 809` fair-hearing rights and triggering mandatory `805` reports to the `BRN` upon disciplinary restriction!
- **Streamlined Hospital `PA` Practice Agreements (`SB 697` / `BPC § 3502.3(a)(2)(B)`):** Replaces legacy Delegation of Services Agreements and mandatory chart co-signature ratios; inside an acute-care hospital (`organized health care system`), a PA Practice Agreement need only be signed on behalf of the medical staff by **one authorized physician** (Chief of Staff, CMO, or Department Chair), incorporating the PA's approved **Delineation of Privileges (`DOP`)** while maintaining `BPC § 3516(b)` active 1:4 concurrent clinical supervision limits (with PAs likewise holding `BPC § 805(a)(2)` licentiate status).
- **`CRNA` Scope vs. `CDPH` `Title 22` Enforcement & the October 1, 2026 *CANA v. CDPH & OAL* Lawsuit:** Although California opted out of the federal Medicare physician-supervision requirement in 2009 and ***California Society of Anesthesiologists v. Brown*, 204 Cal.App.4th 390 (2012)** held `CRNAs` may administer anesthesia pursuant to a physician/dentist/podiatrist *order* without supervision (`BPC § 2725(b)(2)`), `CDPH` surveyors cited Modesto hospitals (**Doctors Medical Center** and **Stanislaus Surgical Hospital**) in 2024 under `22 CCR §§ 70703` and `70231` for placing `CRNAs` onto organized medical staffs (canceling ~1,000 surgeries) and denied `Title 22` rulemaking petitions in September 2025. On **October 1, 2026**, the California Association of Nurse Anesthesiology (`CANA`) sued `CDPH` and `OAL` in **San Francisco County Superior Court** ([Becker's Oct. 2026 Coverage](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/anesthesia/crnas-sue-california-over-hospital-staffing-restrictions/)). Pending judicial resolution, California hospitals using non-supervised `CRNAs` must privilege them through a separate **Advanced Practice / Allied Health Staff** structure (documenting physician anesthesia orders under `BPC § 2725(b)(2)` and physician anesthesia-service medical direction under `22 CCR § 70405`) rather than core voting medical staff seats.
- **Telemedicine Credentialing by Proxy (`42 CFR §§ 482.12(e), 482.22(a)(3)–(4)` & `BPC § 2290.5(i)`):** Originating California hospitals may rely on a distant-site Medicare hospital or qualified Distant-Site Telemedicine Entity (`DSTE`) *only* when six elements align: (1) explicit authorization in the originating hospital's Medical Staff Bylaws; (2) written `CoP`-compliant proxy agreement; (3) **unrestricted California licensure** for every distant-site tele-clinician (`BPC §§ 2052, 2290.5(a)(3)`—proxy never waives state licensure!); (4) local `MEC` recommendation and Governing Body privilege grant; (5) **bi-directional adverse-event and `OPPE` reporting** shielded by a `BPC § 809.08` / `Evid. Code § 1157` covenant; and (6) documented `BPC § 2290.5(b)` patient telehealth consent.
- **Privileging AI-Guided Robotic & Interventional Platforms (`TJC` / `CHAI RUAIH` 2025–2026 + `AB 3030` / `AB 489`):** Following The Joint Commission and Coalition for Health AI (`CHAI`) **Responsible Use of AI in Healthcare (`RUAIH`)** framework (launched late 2025–2026) and California's `AB 3030` (`HSC § 1339.75` GenAI clinical-communication disclosures) and `AB 489` (`BPC § 4999.8` synthetic clinician-title ban), AI-guided surgical/endoluminal robotics and autonomous triage tools must never hide inside "Core Privileges." Medical staffs must require **Special Procedure Privileges** conditioning initial and `AB 1199` 3-year renewal privileges on indication-specific FDA `510(k)`/`PMA` clearance, simulation + proctored `FPPE` case minimums, training on algorithmic failure/manual conversion, and **automatic delta-credentialing triggers upon major vendor AI model updates**.
