Where the checks come from

Opinion text on file

Quotations are matched only against these texts, never against a model's memory. A case with no text here shows "no text on file", and the gate asks the lawyer to check it. Paste the opinion from Westlaw or Lexis to add it.

Check your own brief

Paste the text of a brief or motion. Claude lists the citations, this page checks them against the index and the opinions on file, and Claude reads the opinion text for the support check. Nothing is stored. Until the firm confirms the data terms of its Claude plan, use public filings or invented text.

Add an opinion's text

For a case with no text on file. Paste the opinion and give its citation; the quote and support checks rerun for every citation to that case.

1. Read the briefClaude lists every case, its quotations and the point it is cited for. The page then confirms each one appears in the brief exactly as listed, and a pattern sweep catches any reporter citation Claude skipped.
2. Does the case exist?Each volume, reporter and page is looked up in CourtListener's citation table. A cite that points to a different case, or to nothing, blocks filing. A volume and year that cannot go together is flagged too.
3. Are the quotes real?Each quotation is matched word for word against the court's own opinion text. A near miss is shown as a word-by-word comparison so the lawyer sees exactly what changed.
4. Does it say that?The page pulls the passages of the opinion that bear on the brief's point and Claude judges support from those passages alone. Claude's quote of the court is checked word for word too.
5. A lawyer signsBlocked rows must be fixed or removed. The signed table records who checked what, against which source, on which date.

What it does not do

  • It never clears a citation from a model's memory. No source text means no green.
  • It is not a citator and does not Shepardize. Whether a case was overruled, reversed, depublished or is uncitable under rule 8.1115 needs KeyCite or Shepard's, which the firm already licenses. That check plugs in at step 2 in production; until then, the sign-off requires the lawyer to confirm it was done.
  • It does not replace Westlaw Quick Check or Lexis Brief Analysis. Its index covers about 256,000 opinions from four courts, not every reported decision.
  • Slip opinions carry slip page numbers, so pin cites are confirmed only where the text on file has reporter pagination.
  • CourtListener's record of official California citations is incomplete for recent years. A recent case missing from the index is flagged for a Westlaw pull, not called fake, unless no case by that name exists either.

At the firm

  • Runs on what the firm licenses. A Claude seat for the reading, CourtListener's free public data for the existence check, and Westlaw or Lexis for opinion text and citator status in production.
  • One fixed step before filing. Docketing or the filing assistant runs it on the final draft; the responsible lawyer signs the table, and it goes in the matter file.
  • Fits the State Bar of California's generative AI guidance. AI output is checked against authoritative sources and a lawyer stays responsible for every citation.
