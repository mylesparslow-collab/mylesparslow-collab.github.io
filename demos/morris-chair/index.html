Morris's Chair

William Morris complained that his handmade furniture served only the rich. This page prices a handmade chair, shirt and book against the factory version, in hours of an American factory worker's pay, from 1878 to today.

The Morris chair, 1910 and now

Stickley's $28–33 Morris chair took about 141 hours of pay in 1909–13. Stickley's Morris chair today takes 165 hours on sale and 254 at list price. Amish-built chairs sold online take 64 to 73. The factory chair fell from 29 hours at Sears to under 8 at IKEA and Ashley. Across all three handmade chairs the gap went from about five times to about ten. Against Stickley alone it is about 22 times.

Shirts, made to order and ready-made

Custom shirts in ads from 1882 to 1923 cost 7.6 to 17 hours of pay. Where one store priced both, the custom shirt cost 1.6 to 3.3 times the ready-made one. A custom shirt from a New York maker today is 8.6 hours; a Uniqlo oxford is 1.7.

Method

The prices come from three places:

  • Sears and Montgomery Ward catalogues on archive.org, 1878–1929, plus Sears, Ward and Spiegel from 1932 to 1972.
  • Stickley, Roycroft, Kelmscott and Morris & Co. catalogues, and custom-shirt newspaper ads from Chronicling America.
  • Today's makers and shops.

The wage is the average hourly pay of US manufacturing production workers, built from three series:

  • Long (1960) for the 1880s.
  • Historical Statistics of the United States to 1969.
  • BLS figures from 1970, scaled to one another where they overlap.

Hours are the price divided by that year's wage. British prices are converted at gold par for historic rows and at the 2025 average rate for modern ones.

Of the 99 prices, 94 were read off the page image or the live page. In a random recheck of 12 of those, 8 matched the page and none mismatched. The other 4 couldn't be reached: three Chronicling America pages now sit behind a bot challenge, and one shop's site refused the request.

Limits

  • "Handmade" means how the seller sold the thing. Stickley's catalogues claim only hand sanding. The custom shirts are made to order, and no ad claims hand sewing.
  • There are no prices from the 1940s, no book prices from 1930 to today, and no handmade chair between 1913 and today.
  • Today's Stickley is a different firm from Gustav Stickley's. The IKEA chair does not recline, and the Ashley price is an outlet price.
  • Where the wage series are joined matters before 1939. Joining them at 1939 instead of 1970 adds about 23% to every earlier hour figure. Ratios within a year don't change.
  • Stickley's and Kelmscott's buyers were mostly not factory workers. The hours say what the object would have cost one.
