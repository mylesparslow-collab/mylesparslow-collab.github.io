Conflicts Desk Information Sheet

Overview

Conflara is a conflicts-checking and new business intake system built for a mid-size law firm. It replaces email and PDF intake with one place where a request is entered, every party is searched, each hit is decided by the conflicts team, approvals are routed to the right people, waivers and ethical walls are recorded, and the matter is opened in Aderant.

What leadership should take away:

  • It covers the whole life of a matter. Quick checks, new business intake, pro bono intake, conflicts search and review, approvals, waivers, engagement letters, ethical walls, lateral hires, matter opening and closing, all in one system with one audit trail.
  • The firm keeps its data. Conflara can run entirely on the firm’s own servers, with sign-in through the firm’s Microsoft 365 (Entra ID) accounts. Nothing is sent outside the firm unless an administrator deliberately turns that on.
  • AI is private and advisory. An optional AI model runs on the firm’s own hardware. It suggests and helps draft; a conflicts analyst or lawyer always decides. It never records a decision, signs anything or clears a hit.
  • Confidentiality is enforced on the server. People screened by an ethical wall, or kept out of a confidential matter, are never sent that record at all. Every change is written to a tamper-evident audit trail.
  • It works alongside Aderant. Aderant stays the billing system. Conflara reads clients, matters, entities and billing status from Aderant and sends newly opened matters back.
  • It has been load-tested at the firm’s size. A simulated firm of 229 people (200 lawyers) working at once, with 50,000 to 250,000 party names, saw every change reach every screen in about one to two seconds, with no failed requests and no data errors.
  • Conflara never signs for anyone. Engagement letters and waivers count as signed only when a person uploads the signed copy and records who signed it.

This sheet describes the standard configuration. Optional features that a firm can switch on later are noted where relevant.

How a matter moves

A new matter goes from a lawyer’s first question to an open, billable matter in eight steps, and Conflara shows everyone what the request is waiting on at each one.

  1. Quick check (optional). A lawyer or secretary sends just the names of the parties, before there is a full form. It gets its own number (QC-2026-0001) and is searched at once. The conflicts team reviews the hits and sends the results back. If the work goes ahead, the same record is converted into a full intake, keeping every decision already made.
  2. New business intake. The requester fills in the client, the matter, the parties and their roles, the practice area and records area, offices, lawyers, fee estimate and billing details, plus the firm’s own questions. The form shows a live preview of possible conflicts for each party as it is typed. Pro bono matters have their own intake with eligibility questions and no fee fields. A request can be saved as a draft, edited, withdrawn or cloned.
  3. Conflicts search. On submission every party is searched against the firm’s clients, adverse parties, related companies, former and prospective clients, declined matters, pending requests and lawyers’ declared relationships. Each search gets a permanent number (S-2026-000123) that appears on reports, so an analyst can cite exactly which search was run. New parties are also screened against sanctions lists.
  4. Conflicts review. The conflicts team sees the hits grouped by searched party and ranked by risk, with the reason for each ranking and any earlier decisions on the same party. They decide each hit (clear, not the same entity, waiver needed, screen, escalate, decline), ask responsible lawyers for clearance, record waivers and put up ethical walls. A conflicts summary goes back to the lawyer.
  5. Approvals. The request goes through the firm’s approval route: for example the practice group leader, finance, and the general counsel when a request is restricted, high-fee, high-risk, or touches cannabis or political work. Each approver can approve, approve with conditions, or return it for more information. Deadlines and overdue flags keep it moving. An approver with a declared interest in a party is routed around automatically, and an alternate signs instead.
  6. Matter opening. Once approved, the conflicts team opens the matter. It gets its client.matter number (for example 011072.0001), new parties are added to the database, the Aderant record is prepared, and, if switched on, the matter’s folders are created in the document management system.
  7. Billing hold until the paperwork is complete. Opening does not wait for paperwork, but billing does. Until the signed engagement letter, signed waivers and any required documents (such as a HIPAA business associate agreement) are uploaded, and every remaining conflicts item is closed, the matter shows Open · billing on hold and the conflicts team keeps a task to finish it.
  8. After opening. Change monitoring re-checks the matter’s parties when the firm’s data changes and lists any new hit nobody has reviewed. When the matter ends it is closed with a retention date.

What people see while they wait

Every request shows a next-step line that says, in plain words, what it is waiting on and who has it. Lawyers get a task list on their home page, and emails go out for approvals, walls and conflicts summaries. Optional emails (mentions, new intakes, waiver replies) can be turned off per person.

Features

Conflara covers conflicts, intake, approvals, waivers, walls, laterals and matter opening in one system; this section lists what each area does.

Conflicts search

  • Name matching that catches variants: misspellings, sound-alike names, nicknames (Bill and William), aliases, former names, and partly typed words (“merid sol” finds Meridian Solar). Rare words count more than common ones, so “Meridian” weighs more than “Holdings”.
  • Corporate families and personal links: parents, subsidiaries and affiliates up to the ultimate parent and back down, and links such as spouses and officers.
  • Everything the firm knows is searched: current and former clients, adverse and related parties, prospective clients turned away (ABA and California Rule 1.18), declined matters, requests still in progress, and lawyers’ declared relationships.
  • Search numbers: every search, re-run and report gets a permanent number (S-2026-000123) that can be cited and looked up.
  • Single, batch and Boolean search: one name, several wildcard searches at once (numbered B-2026-0001), or an exact rule-based search with AND, OR, NOT, phrases and fields such as role, open date and practice area.
  • Confidential searches: a search can be marked confidential so only the person who ran it and designated conflicts staff can see it.
  • Duplicate search warning: when most of a new search’s parties match a search from the last 12 months, Conflara says so and links the earlier one. It never stops a search; the threshold is set by the firm.
  • Searched before: wherever a name is entered, Conflara lists earlier searches for that party and what came of them (became a matter, quick check only, closed, still in progress).
  • Sanctions screening: new parties are checked against the OFAC list and other watch lists the firm loads.
  • Party types explained: a small “What do these types mean?” chart appears wherever party roles are shown.

Intake

  • Guided new business intake in a few short steps, with a live preview of possible conflicts for each party as it is typed.
  • Quick check: just the party names, searched before there is a full form, later converted in place into a new intake with every decision carried forward.
  • Pro bono intake with eligibility questions, a supervising lawyer and no fee fields, and its own numbering (by default a pro bono client range from 900001).
  • Practice area and records area on every new matter form; the records area sets the folders the matter gets in the document management system.
  • Firm questions without programming: administrators add the firm’s own questions, show them only for certain practices, offices or matter types, make fields required, preview, and publish versions.
  • Practice screens: Conflara notices when a request looks like health care, cannabis, an insurance company as client, a public body, or political or campaign work. It shows the right questions (for example HIPAA status) and can add approval steps through the firm’s routing rules. It never decides anything on its own.
  • Required documents: when the answers call for them, Conflara tracks a HIPAA business associate agreement, joint representation consents and founder acknowledgements.
  • Reading a document or email for parties: a pasted email or matter description can suggest parties, which the requester confirms one by one.
  • Public client intake form that feeds the same queue, protected against spam, and never showing conflicts results to the public.
  • Comments on parties, drafts, edit and resubmit, withdraw, clone, delegates, and @mentions in comments.
  • Name suggestions in attorney and staff fields, accepted with Tab or Enter. Nothing fills in by itself.

Conflicts review and clearance

  • Hits grouped by searched party, ranked by risk with a plain reason, and shown with earlier decisions on the same party.
  • One-click and bulk decisions with notes: clear, not the same entity, waiver needed, screen, escalate, decline.
  • Clearance requests sent to the responsible lawyers on matching matters.
  • Lawyer relationship disclosures (California Rule 1.7(b) and (c)): declared family, board, investment, former-employer and personal ties are shown as FYIs when a party matches.
  • A conflicts summary to the lawyer, and an attorney-readable conflicts report with a PDF download.
  • An optional learned hit order that suggests which hits to read first, based on the firm’s own past decisions. It is off by default, needs at least 200 past decisions, keeps high-risk hits on top, and never hides or decides a hit.

Approvals and workflow

  • Approval routes built from the firm’s rules: practice group leader or office managing partner, finance, general counsel for restricted, high-fee or high-risk requests, and any other steps the firm defines.
  • Approve, approve with conditions, or return for more information; deadlines and overdue flags; delegates.
  • Routing around an interest: an approver with a declared tie to a party is not asked to approve that request. An alternate signs instead, both are told, and the audit trail records it.
  • Finance checks for credit, retainers and rate discounts.
  • A task board, a workflow dashboard, and a next-step line on every request.
  • One person works on a form at a time; conflicts staff and administrators can take one over after confirming.

Waivers, engagement letters and client guidelines

  • Waiver records with informed written consent; requests sent to clients by a secure link; reminders and tracking.
  • Waiver drafts from templates. The conflicts team sends the draft to a lawyer, who edits it and approves or returns it from a task.
  • Engagement letters from the firm’s editable templates, with California fee-agreement checks (Business and Professions Code 6147 and 6148), exported to Word.
  • Letters never name another client unless that client has agreed to be named; a letter that does cannot be marked ready.
  • Drafts are edited by typing into the document itself, and every save is logged.
  • Signed copies only: a letter or waiver counts as signed only when someone uploads the signed copy and records who signed it. Conflara has no sign or countersign button.
  • Outside counsel guidelines per client, broken into key terms (adverse work, staffing, AI use, billing) and flagged on that client’s requests.

Ethical walls

  • Walls that keep named people out, or that let only the team in, over single matters or whole clients.
  • Enforced inside Conflara on the server: a screened person is never sent the walled matter, its files, notes or audit lines. Blocked attempts are logged.
  • Acknowledgments and periodic re-acknowledgment, wall notices, Rule 1.10 certifications, and fee exclusion.
  • Lists of who is screened, ready for the document management and time-entry systems, and connectors for applying walls in iManage, SharePoint and Microsoft 365 (to be confirmed against the firm’s systems before use).

Lateral hires

  • A secure questionnaire for the candidate’s prior clients and matters.
  • Batch search of what they list, California screening rule checks (Rules 1.10(a)(2), 1.11 and 1.12), walls put up before the start date, and a pre-start checklist.

Matter opening, numbering and closing

  • Client.matter numbering in the Aderant style (011072.0001), with special numbers available to the conflicts team when a matter must open under a chosen number (with a reason, checked to be free, and recorded).
  • Opening checklist split into what is needed to open and what is needed before billing; the billing hold described in “How a matter moves”.
  • Matter workspaces and folders created in the document management system when a matter opens, if switched on.
  • Closing with retention dates.

Change monitoring and outside company data

  • Change monitoring re-checks approved requests, open matters and watched clients whenever the firm’s data changes and each night, and lists only new hits nobody has reviewed. A person marks each one.
  • Company families and names from public registers (GLEIF, SEC EDGAR, OpenCorporates) and from the firm’s own Dun & Bradstreet or Capital IQ exports. Nothing is added to a party until an analyst picks it.

Reports and audit

  • Standard reports and a report builder, with spreadsheet export that is protected against formula tricks.
  • An ethics and insurer pack for a chosen period.
  • A searchable audit log of every change, and a “who opened walled records” log that includes blocked attempts.

Ease of use

  • A command palette (Cmd or Ctrl + K) to jump to any page, request, matter, party, wall or search number.
  • Personal preferences: day or night mode, menu at the side or top, text size, density, reduced motion, start page, font choice (including an easy-reading font), and five color schemes including high contrast. The color schemes are checked against the WCAG AA contrast standard.
  • Role walkthroughs, a glossary of conflicts terms, short tooltips on terms, printable quick-start sheets per role, and a first-week checklist that guides an administrator to a live firm.

AI assistance

Conflara’s AI is optional, runs on the firm’s own hardware by default, and only suggests or helps draft: a conflicts analyst or lawyer reviews everything and makes every decision.

What it helps with

Task What the AI does What a person does
Hit triage Suggests whether a hit is likely the same entity and likely a conflict, with a one-line reason, shown beside the rule-based risk with a ✦ mark The analyst decides. The suggestion never records a decision
Finding parties in text Suggests parties and roles from a pasted email or matter description The requester accepts or rejects each one. Names that are not in the text are thrown out
Conflicts memo Helps draft a memo from the decisions already recorded The analyst edits it
Import column matching Suggests which column of an export feeds each field The administrator confirms. Only real column names are accepted
File summaries Helps summarise an uploaded document A person checks it
Engagement letter and guideline terms Lists key terms from a letter or a client’s outside counsel guidelines A lawyer checks them
Waiver first drafts Helps fill a waiver template A lawyer edits and approves it
Next-step wording Rewords the next-step line in plainer language Shown for information only

Search results, risk levels, reports, approvals, walls and signed documents never depend on the AI. Without a model connected, Conflara works fully; the AI buttons simply say a local model is needed.

Where it runs

  • On the firm’s own hardware (default). The model runs on a server or workstation inside the firm, through standard local model software (Ollama, or any compatible local server such as LM Studio, llama.cpp or vLLM). Client names never leave the firm’s network.
  • The browser cannot choose where AI calls go. Conflara’s server sends every AI call to one fixed address set by IT, and refuses a model outside the firm’s private network unless an administrator deliberately allows it.
  • Background triage. When a model is connected, the server can triage new hits in the background after each intake, so suggestions are waiting when the analyst opens the request.
  • Sizing. As an estimate, one modern desktop-class machine with a capable graphics processor or Apple silicon can keep up with the intake volume of a firm of about 200 lawyers, because triage runs in the background rather than while someone waits. The pilot would confirm the right machine.

External AI provider (off unless the firm chooses it)

A firm can choose to use an outside AI provider instead. It takes two separate steps: IT configures the provider on the server, and then an administrator accepts a written data-handling notice in the app. Both are recorded in the audit trail, and switching it off is one click.

Even when it is on, Conflara never sends:

  • restricted or highly confidential requests, or confidential ones unless an administrator allows it;
  • requests the requester or conflicts team marked “Keep this request away from outside AI services”;
  • anything with a hit on a matter or party behind an ethical wall;
  • anything touching a client whose outside counsel guidelines bar AI use, or its corporate family;
  • health information requests and matters;
  • import files.

Each task sends a fixed, minimal set of fields (for example, hit triage sends no matter names or descriptions), and the server writes “Sent to external AI” to the audit trail with the fields sent before anything leaves. The firm should still check the provider’s own terms, and its clients’ engagement letters and guidelines, before turning this on.

Safeguards

  • Documents and emails are treated as untrusted text, so instructions hidden in them are not followed. Hidden characters are stripped and lengths are capped.
  • The model has no tools and no internet access. Its answers must fit a fixed format, and anything that does not is discarded.
  • AI notes follow the same walls and confidentiality rules as everything else. A screened person never sees an AI note written from a matter they are walled from.
  • Every AI use is recorded with the task and model name. The text sent and received is not stored.
  • Every AI suggestion is marked ✦ on screen, with a reminder to check it.

What the firm should know

  • AI suggestions can be wrong. They are a second pair of eyes for the conflicts team, not a substitute for its review.
  • How well a given model suits the firm’s data is something the pilot would measure, by comparing its suggestions with the conflicts team’s own decisions.
  • The AI never signs, approves, clears a hit, puts up or lifts a wall, or sends anything to a client.

Security and confidentiality

Conflara is designed around one rule: a person screened from a matter must never receive it. The server decides what each person is sent, every change is recorded in a tamper-evident log, and the firm keeps its data on systems it controls.

Who can see what

  • Each person gets only their own view. The server builds what each person receives. Lawyers, secretaries and approvers get a task-only view: their own requests and the records their tasks need. The conflicts team and administrators see the wider database they need to do their work, still subject to confidentiality levels.
  • Walls are enforced before data leaves the server. A screened person’s browser never receives the walled matter, its parties, files, notes, comments, AI notes or audit lines. Hiding is not done by the screen, so it cannot be bypassed by looking at the page’s data.
  • Confidentiality levels. Any client, matter or request can be marked normal, confidential or highly confidential, with a list of people allowed in by name. Highly confidential records are open only to the record’s own lawyers, the people named, designated conflicts analysts and administrators. A matter takes the higher of its own level and its client’s.
  • What others see of a hidden record. When a search hits a record someone may not see, they get only “Match on a confidential record. Contact the conflicts team,” with no name, matter or count.
  • Restricted requests are visible only to the people on them and the conflicts team.
  • Every opening of a walled or confidential record is logged, including blocked attempts, in a “who opened walled records” log.
  • Losing access takes effect at once. Disabling an account or changing someone’s role ends their sessions and stops their live updates immediately.

Sign-in and sessions

  • Sign-in through the firm’s Microsoft 365 accounts (Entra ID), so multi-factor authentication, device rules and conditional access follow the firm’s existing policy.
  • Accounts are created by an administrator; nobody gets in just because they have a firm account, unless the firm chooses that.
  • Sessions expire after 30 minutes idle and 12 hours in total (both adjustable), and signing out clears the browser.
  • In server mode the browser keeps no firm data on the computer.

The audit trail

  • Every change (who, what, when) is appended to a log that the database itself refuses to edit or delete.
  • Each entry is linked to the one before by a keyed fingerprint, so any change to past entries is detected. The server checks the whole chain when it starts and every 15 minutes, and stops accepting changes if the check fails.
  • A daily copy of the latest fingerprint, stored outside the database, lets the firm detect even the deletion of the newest entries.
  • The log alone can rebuild all of the firm’s data, and Conflara checks that it does.

Protecting the data

Area How it is protected

Database PostgreSQL on the firm’s server, reached only from the app over a private network, with an app account that cannot change the database’s structure. Encryption at rest is provided by the server’s disk or the managed database service

Getting support without sharing client data

An administrator can produce a support bundle that holds versions, settings switches, record counts and recent error messages, with every name, email, id and piece of free text replaced by a placeholder. Conflara checks the bundle against every name in the firm’s records before it can be saved, the administrator reads it in full first, and saving it is recorded. Nothing is sent automatically. When support needs to see a problem happen, Conflara can build a test firm of made-up records with the same size and shape as the firm’s data.

How it has been tested

  • Over 1,000 automated tests, including tests that inspect everything each kind of user is sent and check that no walled or highly confidential name, number or file appears in it.
  • Tests that feed the AI hostile documents designed to trick it, and confirm nothing but the advisory note changes.
  • Internal security testing of a production-mode install covering walls, confidentiality, sign-in, file handling and attempts to act as another user.
  • Automated dependency and code scanning on every change; the production dependencies currently show no known vulnerabilities.

Recommended before go-live

  • An independent penetration test against the firm’s own install, including sign-in through the firm’s Entra tenant and the firm’s reverse proxy.
  • Conflara does not hold a SOC 2 report or other certification. For a self-hosted install the firm’s own controls cover the servers; a SOC 2 readiness mapping exists for the hosted option.
  • The firm’s general counsel to review the built-in engagement letter, waiver and consent wording, which ships marked as drafts for a lawyer’s review.

Data and integrations

The firm’s existing conflicts records come in through a checked import, Aderant stays the billing system and exchanges data with Conflara, and the document management system can receive matter folders and walls.

Bringing in existing records

The import works from exports the firm already has (CSV or Excel), from Aderant, Clio, another conflicts system or any spreadsheet with one row per party on a matter.

  1. Choose the file. Presets recognise Aderant Expert and Clio layouts; any other export is matched by its column headings.
  2. Match the columns. Conflara guesses which column feeds each field and a person confirms or corrects each one.
  3. Match the role values. The old system’s words for client, adverse party and so on are mapped to Conflara’s roles.
  4. Dry run. Before anything is saved, Conflara shows the new parties, the matches to existing records, possible duplicates to merge, and any warnings (for example a matter that closes before it opens).
  5. Import, with undo. A recent import can be undone while nothing else depends on its records. An import can also be tried first in the test firm.

What comes across: parties and their ids, types, roles, aliases and parent companies; clients; matters with numbers, names, status, open and close dates, last billed date, responsible lawyer and practice area; and comments on parties. The old system’s search logs and past clearance memos are not imported, so the firm would keep its previous system’s archive available for reference.

Aderant

Aderant remains the firm’s system of record for billing; Conflara never changes billing data. There are three ways to connect, from least to most setup:

Method How it works What the firm’s Aderant team does
Upload Someone exports reports from Aderant and uploads them on Conflara’s Aderant page; the columns are checked before anything is brought in Export the reports
Shared folder A scheduled job at the firm drops the same reports into a folder each night; Conflara reads new ones and writes import files for newly opened matters Set up read-only report queries and a job to load Conflara’s import files
Live connection Conflara talks to Aderant’s programming interface directly Obtain API access and documentation from Aderant; Conflara’s connection is matched to it and tested against a test Aderant environment before use

What moves each way:

  • From Aderant: Entity Manager names (with parent companies, aliases and former names), Expert clients and matters with their parties, and matter billing status (last bill date, last time entry, unbilled time and balance due, shown only to the conflicts team).
  • To Aderant: newly opened matters with their client, matter number, office and lawyers, new entities, and whether the matter is on billing hold and why.
  • Round trip: each party Conflara sends carries Conflara’s id, so when Entity Manager’s export comes back the two records are linked even if Aderant’s team typed the name differently.
  • One number: a matter numbered 011072.0001 in Conflara goes to Aderant under the same client and matter code.

The nightly pull never merges records on its own; a close name is created as a new party and listed for review.

To confirm with the firm’s Aderant team first: the exact column names in the firm’s exports (a first step that shares column names only, no client data), and how the firm’s Aderant marks a bill hold.

Document management

  • Matter folders. When a matter opens, Conflara can create its workspace and the folders its records area calls for, in iManage or SharePoint. Folders already there are left alone, and failures are shown in plain words with a Try again button. It is off until an administrator turns it on, and should first be tried with one matter against a test library.
  • Filing. Documents attached in Conflara can be filed to iManage or SharePoint. Confidential, highly confidential and walled records are not filed unless an administrator allows each case.
  • Walls in other systems. Conflara can apply its ethical walls to iManage workspaces, SharePoint folders and Microsoft 365 groups, in plan, read-only or enforce mode, and produces screened-person lists for the time-entry system. These connections are to be tested against the firm’s own systems before they enforce anything.
  • NetDocuments and Box are not connected yet; folders there would be made by hand.

Microsoft 365

  • Sign-in through Entra ID.
  • An Outlook add-in can start an intake from an open email, suggesting parties from it. It reads only the open message and sends it only to the firm’s own Conflara server. It is to be tested in the firm’s Microsoft 365 before use.

Outside company data

  • Public registers: GLEIF (global legal entity identifiers), SEC EDGAR (US public companies and their subsidiaries) and OpenCorporates (with the firm’s own account).
  • The firm’s own Dun & Bradstreet or S&P Capital IQ exports, imported in the browser and kept with the firm’s data; nothing is sent to those providers.
  • An analyst links a party to its record and picks the names, parents and subsidiaries to add. Each night Conflara re-reads linked records and lists what changed (a new parent, a new name, a company that merged or lapsed).
  • Only the name typed into a search, or a register’s own id, ever leaves the firm.

Email

Notifications go through the firm’s own mail server. Emails about sensitive requests name them by number only, and a person can switch off optional emails for themselves.

Deployment and IT

Conflara runs on one server inside the firm, alongside a PostgreSQL database and, optionally, a local AI model; a firm of about 200 lawyers was load-tested on a modest four-core, 15 GB machine.

Every request from a person passes through the firm’s HTTPS proxy to the Conflara server, which checks it and is the only part that talks to the database and the firm’s other systems.

Ways to run it

Option What it is Best for
Firm server (recommended) The app, PostgreSQL and the optional AI model in containers on a firm server or virtual machine, behind the firm’s HTTPS proxy The whole firm
Desktop app A Windows or macOS application that either connects to the firm server, or runs everything on one computer for a single user Remote users of the firm server; a single conflicts analyst or an evaluation
Hosted One install run by the vendor for this firm alone, in a data region the firm picks (US, Canada, EU, UK or Australia) A firm that prefers not to run servers

One install serves one firm. Data is never shared between firms.

What firm IT provides

  • A Linux server or virtual machine with Docker. The load test used 4 processor cores and 15 GB of memory for the app and database together.
  • An HTTPS reverse proxy (for example nginx, Caddy or Azure Application Gateway) and a certificate.
  • An Entra ID app registration for sign-in (a short, documented set of steps), and Conditional Access if the firm wants multi-factor or device rules.
  • A mail relay for notifications.
  • Encrypted storage for nightly backups, kept off the server.
  • If AI is wanted: a machine for the local model (see the AI assistance section).
  • Optional: a shared folder for Aderant reports, and service credentials for iManage or SharePoint.

All settings are environment variables, documented one by one. In production mode the server refuses to start with an unsafe setting and says which one to fix.

Reliability

Conflara was tested against six standard reliability criteria:

Criterion What it means for the firm
A repeated action counts once A double click or a retried connection never records something twice
The change and its audit entry are saved together Nobody ever sees a change that was not fully saved and logged
The log can rebuild everything The audit log alone reproduces the firm’s data exactly, and Conflara checks this
Lost access ends at once A disabled or demoted person stops receiving data immediately
Two people editing at once If two people change the same thing at the same moment, the first is kept and the second is told who changed it, so nothing is silently overwritten

Performance at the firm’s size

The load test played all 229 people of a simulated 200-lawyer firm at once, each with their own sign-in and live screen, making changes at set rates for a minute at a time. A typical firm of that size has about 50,000 party names.

Party names Changes per second across the firm Time for a change to reach every screen (typical / slow) Server memory
50,000 1 0.4 s / 0.5 s 1.0 GB
50,000 10 0.4 s / 0.7 s 1.2 GB
250,000 1 0.7 s / 1.0 s 2.4 GB
250,000 5 0.9 s / 1.6 s 3.0 GB

One change a second is about 29,000 changes in an eight-hour day, well above what a firm of about 200 lawyers records on an ordinary day (an estimate). Every run had no failed requests, every simultaneous edit of the same item was resolved correctly, and the audit log verified and rebuilt the data exactly. After a restart with everyone’s browser open, all 229 people were back in within 9 seconds at 50,000 names and within a minute at 250,000. A firm with a million or more names would need a larger server.

Trying changes safely

A separate test firm with fictional data lets the conflicts team and administrators try approval routes, intake questions, imports, templates and AI settings without touching real records. Test data never reaches live data, never sends email and never calls an outside AI. When a change works, an administrator reviews the differences and promotes it to the live firm, and the promotion is recorded.

Desktop app for IT

  • Installs per user or for all users, silently if wanted, with a first-run setup.
  • An IT policy file can fix settings for every user, such as the firm server’s address.
  • In single-computer mode, data stays in a folder on that computer, keys are kept in the operating system’s secure store, and the app accepts connections only from that computer. Full-disk encryption should be on.

Rollout and FAQ

The safest path is a pilot that proves Conflara finds what the firm’s current process finds, on the firm’s own past searches, before anyone relies on it.

A pilot in five stages

  1. IT and security review. Firm IT reviews this sheet and the security documentation, and installs Conflara on a test server inside the firm.
  2. Load a copy of the records. The conflicts team imports a copy of the firm’s party and matter records through the checked import, and connects Aderant by report upload.
  3. Side-by-side test on past searches. Past searches with known outcomes (a few hundred, including those that found real conflicts) are run again in Conflara. The conflicts team labels each hit without knowing which system found it. The bar is no missed conflicts, few missed relevant hits, and no flood of false alarms. A scoring tool for this comparison is included.
  4. Parallel running. For a set period, new requests go through both the current process and Conflara, so the conflicts team can compare speed and results on live work.
  5. Go-live decision. Leadership decides on go-live, which features to switch on (AI, folder creation, wall enforcement in other systems), and the firm’s approval routes and templates.

If the firm prefers not to load real records at first, the pilot can start with names only, or entirely on fictional data in the test firm.

To confirm before go-live

  • Independent penetration test of the firm’s install
  • Sign-in tested through the firm’s Entra tenant and Conditional Access
  • Aderant export column names confirmed with the firm’s Aderant team, and how a bill hold is marked
  • Matter folder creation tried on one matter in a test iManage or SharePoint library
  • Wall enforcement in iManage, SharePoint and Microsoft 365 tried in read-only mode first
  • General counsel review of engagement letter, waiver and consent templates
  • Approval routes, practice screens and intake questions set to the firm’s policies
  • Backup storage, restore drill and audit fingerprint storage agreed with IT
  • Choice of AI: none, local model (and which machine), or external provider

Frequently asked questions

Does Conflara replace Aderant? No. Aderant stays the billing system and the source of client, matter and entity records. Conflara reads from it and sends opened matters back.

Who owns and holds the data? The firm. On a firm server, the data never leaves the firm’s systems. It is kept in a standard PostgreSQL database and can be exported to spreadsheets at any time.

Can our previous conflicts history come across? Parties, clients, matters and their details can be imported from any export. Earlier search logs and clearance memos stay in the previous system’s archive for reference.

Does the AI make conflicts decisions? No. It suggests and helps draft, every suggestion is marked ✦, and a person decides. The firm can run Conflara with no AI at all.

Does any client information go to an outside AI company? Not unless the firm deliberately turns that on, in two separate steps. Even then, walled, restricted, highly confidential and health information records are never sent, and every call is logged.

Can a lawyer see a matter they are walled from? The server never sends it to them, in any screen, search, export or AI note. Attempts to open walled records are logged.

What happens if two people edit the same thing? One person works on a form at a time. If two people change the same detail at the same moment, the first is kept and the second is told who changed it.

Does Conflara sign engagement letters or waivers? Never. People upload signed copies and record who signed.

What if the server stops? Every change is saved to the database with its audit entry before anyone sees it, so a restart loses nothing. Nightly encrypted backups and a tested restore cover the loss of the server itself.

How are people trained? Each role gets a short walkthrough the first time they use Conflara, plus a glossary, printable quick-start sheets, and a test firm with fictional data to practise in.

How is support handled without exposing client data? Support works from a redacted support bundle and, when needed, a made-up test firm of the same size. Support terms and service levels would be agreed with the firm.

What about licensing and cost? Commercial terms are discussed separately from this sheet.
