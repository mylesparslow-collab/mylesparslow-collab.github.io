Arrhenius’s 2011 impossibility theorem, as printed, has a countermodel

Gustaf Arrhenius’s Sixth Impossibility Theorem says no ranking of populations can meet five plausible conditions at once. Its proof needs one condition to be read more strongly than it is printed. Two later papers noticed the gap. This page gives a ranking that meets all five conditions as printed, so the statement as printed is false.

Source Arrhenius 2011, pp. 7–9 Gap noted by Thomas 2016 · Thornley 2022 Checked Lean 4, no sorry, standard axioms Status not peer-reviewed

The theorem

Five conditions, and the one that is printed weaker than the proof uses

Populations are lists of lives, each at a whole-number welfare level: negative means not worth living, 1 to 3 is barely worth living, high levels go well. A ranking says which populations are at least as good as which.

EDEgalitarian Dominance

A perfectly equal population is better than a same-size population where everyone is worse off.

GNEPNon-Extreme Priority

Some number of very good lives, plus one life at a level, is at least as good as the same number of barely positive lives plus one life a step better.

NENon-Elitism

Slightly lowering one life can be outweighed by raising enough lower lives.

WNSWeak Non-Sadism

Adding lives worth living is at least as good as adding lives not worth living.

WQAWeak Quality Addition

As printed: for every background population there exist a negative level and a number m such that a few very good lives beat any number of barely positive lives plus m lives at that negative level. The proof needs the negative level and m chosen before the background, once for all backgrounds.

Teruji Thomas (2016, §9) and Elliott Thornley (2022, fn. 7) point out that the proof fails at this quantifier order. Neither says whether the statement as printed is still true. The ranking below shows it is not.

The countermodel

Barely positive lives count only once the debt is paid

Score each population with one number and rank by it. Suffering is a debt. Lives that go very well pay it down. Lives barely worth living add nothing until the debt is covered, and then they count in full.

V = H − D + [H ≥ D] · L
Htotal welfare of lives at level 4 or above
Dtotal size of the negative welfare, the debt
Ltotal welfare of lives at levels 1 to 3

When the high lives cover the debt, V is plain total welfare. When they don’t, the barely positive lives drop out. That is the whole trick. For WQA as printed, the negative level can depend on the background, so it can always be picked deep enough to leave the debt unpaid, and then no number of barely positive lives helps. If the negative level has to be fixed first, a background with enough high lives covers it, and enough barely positive lives win.

Where the proof breaks

Step (12) of Lemma 1.3, with the background empty

With X empty, WQA gives the negative level −1 and m = 1, and the trade-off condition gives n = 1. Step (12) then claims {8, 8} is at least as good as {8, −1} plus q lives at level 1, for every q. Here one 8 covers the debt of 1, so the barely positive lives count.

WQA’s witness for the background {8} alone is −9, not −1. Slide a down to −9 and the debt stops being paid. The proof reuses the level it got for the empty background on a different background, which the printed order does not allow.

The check

Machine-checked in Lean 4

The five conditions are written out from pp. 7–9 of the 2011 chapter in a specification file. A second file defines V and proves it meets each condition, for every population and every welfare level, not a sample.

-- Statements.lean: the theorem as printed
def ArrheniusTheorem2011 : Prop :=
  ∀ R : Pop → Pop → Prop, IsQuasiOrder R →
    ¬ (ED R ∧ GNEP R ∧ NE R ∧ WNS R ∧ WQA R)

-- Proof.lean: the debt-first ranking
def V (p : Pop) : Int := H p - D p + (if D p ≤ H p then L p else 0)

theorem refutation : Refutation :=
  ⟨Rv, rv_quasi, rv_perm, rv_ED, rv_GNEP, rv_NE, rv_WNS, rv_WQA, rv_not_strong⟩

theorem arrhenius2011_false : ¬ ArrheniusTheorem2011

The build has no sorry, and #print axioms lists only Lean’s standard three. The refutation also proves the ranking fails the stronger WQA, so it does not contradict the version of the theorem Thomas and Thornley repair.

Two choices in the formal statement differ from the literal text. Egalitarian Dominance is read for non-empty populations, because with zero lives it would demand that the empty population be better than itself. Weak Non-Sadism requires at least one added negative life, which only makes the condition harder to meet.

What this is and isn’t

I’m not a philosopher. I enjoyed philosophy of economics in college and use AI to keep learning, so I pointed Claude at open problems in the area. Claude proposed this ranking. Separate Claude instances checked the case analysis by hand and with 200,000 random trials per condition, compared the Lean statement line by line against Arrhenius’s text, and searched for earlier work. The search found no countermodel in print.

None of that is peer review. Arrhenius’s book manuscript was not checked and may already address the point.
