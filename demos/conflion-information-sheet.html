What one person can build

Conflion started with one question: what would a conflicts and intake system look like if it were designed by someone who knows how the work is actually done? Myles wrote the requirements, studied fourteen competing products, and turned the day-to-day of a conflicts desk into rules the software follows.

Over three and a half weeks it grew from a conflicts search into software that runs the rest of the firm on the same clients, matters and people. Every screen respects the same ethical walls, every change lands in a tamper-evident audit trail, and the AI only suggests. A person always decides.

Every firm, person and matter in the demos is invented. The demo firm is Ashford & Vale LLP.

The core: conflicts and intake

From first question to open matter

  • A quick check of names only, which can grow into a full intake without losing a decision
  • One-page intake with a live conflicts preview as each party is typed
  • Search that catches misspellings, sound-alikes, nicknames, aliases, former names and corporate families
  • Every search gets a permanent number (S-2026-000123) that can be cited
  • Hits ranked by risk, each with a plain reason and the earlier decisions on that party

Decisions that hold up

  • Approval routes built from the firm's rules, routed around anyone with a declared interest
  • Waivers and engagement letters that follow the matter's state rules
  • Ethical walls enforced on the server, so a screened person is never sent the matter
  • Billing waits on the signed copies, and Conflion never signs for anyone
  • Lateral hires screened before the start date, with walls already up

One system for the whole firm

Each area works from the same records and the same permissions, and every decision waiting on a person shows up in one firm inbox with a short morning brief for their role.

Conflicts and intake

Quick checks, intake, pro bono, search, review, approvals, waivers, walls and laterals.

Matters and docketing

Matter workspaces, tasks and timelines, and court deadlines counted from the rules, each step citing its rule.

Documents

Matter folders at opening, check-out and check-in, document profiles, and email filed from Outlook.

Time and billing

Time entry, pre-bills, bill review, invoices, LEDES exports, collections and a pricing desk.

Accounting links

Journal entries to the firm's own ledger, vendor bills, a bank feed, payroll imports, a cash forecast and partner draws.

People and CLE

One record per person, bar admissions, CLE reminders, reviews, recruiting, and joiners and leavers.

Client relationships

Contacts, key clients with account plans, feedback at matter close, events, panels, RFPs and referral sources.

Risk and the GC's office

Claims against the firm, insurance renewals, a risk register, a promise ledger and client due diligence.

Legal holds and records

Holds with acknowledgments and reminders, and file destruction review that a hold always stops.

Knowledge

"Who has done this" from the firm's own matters, a clause bank, precedents, closing sets and privilege logs.

IT and facilities

A help desk, the devices and software each person holds, rooms, visitors, post and off-site boxes.

Reporting

Standard reports, a report builder, an ethics pack, and a reporting layer that Power BI can read.

AI that suggests, while a person decides

The AI is optional and runs on the firm's own hardware by default, through local model software such as Ollama. Every suggestion is marked on screen, follows the same walls as everything else, and is recorded in the audit trail. Search results, approvals, walls and signed documents never depend on it, and Conflion works fully with no model connected.

Ask Newton

One question box for the firm, with answers that link to the records behind them and respect each person's access.

Conflicts copilot

Suggests whether a hit is the same party and why it may matter, for the analyst to accept or reject.

Matter pulse

Flags live matters that may need a look, such as deadlines with no recent work or a client who has gone quiet.

Obligation calendar

Helps list the obligations in a signed deal so a lawyer can check them and put them on the calendar.

Early case read

Helps a lawyer think through exposure and cost ranges at intake, with its sources shown.

Client risk radar

Watches public sources for news about the firm's clients and suggests who might want to reach out.

Bench notes

Gathers public docket history for a judge or opposing counsel into notes a lawyer reviews.

Rule change watch

Reads agency and Federal Register updates and suggests which clients and practice groups each one may touch.

Built for confidential work

  • The server decides what each person is sent. A walled matter never reaches a screened person's browser, in any screen, search, export or AI note.
  • Clients and matters can be marked confidential or highly confidential, with named people allowed in.
  • Sign-in through the firm's Microsoft 365 accounts, with an app lock and a second administrator required for sensitive changes.
  • Every change goes into an audit log the database refuses to edit, with each entry linked to the one before so tampering shows.
  • Uploaded files are encrypted before they are stored, and backups are encrypted too.
  • Tests inspect what each kind of user is sent and check that no walled name, number or file appears in it.

Measured, then made faster

Performance is tested, not assumed. A load test played all 229 people of a simulated 200-lawyer firm at once against 250,000 party names, and a change reached every open screen in under a second in typical runs. The latest round of work then cut search times across the board.

The billing, ledger, HR and document systems stay the systems of record. Conflion reads from them, sends opened matters back, and brings in a firm's existing conflicts history through a checked import with a dry run and undo.

How it was built

Myles's part

  • The product idea, the requirements and the competitor research
  • Every workflow rule, from who approves what to when billing may start
  • Every feature call, design choice and review before release
  • The house rules: AI suggests and a person decides, nothing is signed for an attorney, and no real firm appears anywhere

The method

  • Claude Code writes the code and tests under his direction
  • Each piece of work gets its own branch, its own tests and a written summary
  • Every bug fix adds a test that would have caught it
  • Releases go out in batches, with the full test suite run on the final combined build
  • Regular stress tests look for what could go wrong before anyone else does
