Two Hypotheses
17 futures · 111,201 instrument-days · 2000–2026

Two hypotheses, chosen before looking

Both were picked from published research, not by searching this data. One survived. One did not. That is the point of registering the prediction first.

H1 · Time-series momentum

An instrument's own trailing 12-month return predicts the sign of its next month. Monthly rebalance, volatility-targeted to 10%, 3bps costs. Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012).

Verdict

Survives. Sharpe 0.43, t = 2.29 over 334 months, and the direction beats randomised signs at p = 0.005. Real, and far too small to trade.

H2 · Short-horizon reversal

Trailing 5-day returns predict the opposite sign over the next week, as compensation for providing liquidity. Same machinery, same costs.

Verdict

Rejected. Sharpe 0.03, t = 0.29 across 6246 weekly periods, with an 72.8% drawdown. No effect at this horizon on this universe.

The data is the weak link. These are Yahoo front-month continuous series, not back-adjusted. Before cleaning, Yen showed a 230% one-day move and Platinum 57% — contract rolls and spec changes, not returns. Discontinuities beyond 15% were neutralised as level shifts, which lifted H1's Sharpe from 0.36 to 0.43. Smaller roll gaps remain. Properly back-adjusted contracts would move these numbers, in an unknown direction.

Not tradeable, and not advice. A 2% annual return at 4.6% volatility across 17 simultaneous futures positions is not a retail strategy — it is faint confirmation that a documented academic effect is visible even in poor free data. Nothing here is a recommendation to trade.
