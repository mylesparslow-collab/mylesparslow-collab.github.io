Why a person reviews every photo. On the first try at a lower-level rec room, the AI quietly removed the staircase. A second try opened the curtains and invented a view with a utility pole. Either one would break MLS rules. Each staged photo gets checked against the original before it goes to the MLS, and that check is part of the service.

Empty the room first, then stage it

Most sellers are still living in the house when the photos get taken. The same tool that adds furniture can take it away: clear out the boxes, laundry and toys, then stage the clean room. The local MLS allows removing the seller's personal property as long as nothing that conveys with the house is removed, so the emptied photo also gets a person's review before it goes anywhere. Compliance

What the local MLS and state rules require

The local MLS's image policy allows adding digital images of personal property (furniture, art, plants) and requires virtually staged photos to be disclosed. It prohibits changing anything about the property itself. The state real estate commission treats misleading listing photos as deceptive advertising, and the national Code of Ethics requires a true picture in marketing.

Furniture, rugs, art, plants, lamps

The core of virtual staging. Personal property only.

Paint, flooring, fixtures, counters

Changing wall color or swapping floors changes the house, not the staging.

Removing the seller's clutter

Taking out personal belongings is fine, as long as nothing that conveys is removed.

Hiding defects or changing views

No erasing cracks, stains, power lines, neighboring buildings or replacing a gray sky.

Sources disagree on the details. The MLS policy text confirms disclosure is required, and some summaries say it must appear in both Public and Private Remarks. Vendor blogs also claim a photo caption or watermark is required, which others dispute. The safe practice below covers every version. The broker should confirm with MLS support before the first staged listing goes live.

Disclosure copy

Ready-to-use wording

Public & private remarks
Some photos have been virtually staged, as local MLS rules require, to show possible furniture layouts. Furnishings are not included. Unstaged photos of each room are included in the gallery.
Photo caption
Virtually staged, as local MLS rules require. Furniture is digital and not included.
Gallery order
Place each staged photo directly after the unstaged photo of the same room.
How it runs

From photo shoot to MLS in one afternoon

  1. Shoot as usualThe photographer delivers the full gallery, including empty rooms.
  2. Pick roomsThe agent flags the 4–6 rooms that benefit most: living, dining, primary bedroom, basement.
  3. Stage and captionEach room is staged in a style matched to the price point and neighborhood, then captioned.
  4. Review and uploadThe agent checks every photo against the rules above and uploads both versions with the remarks line.
