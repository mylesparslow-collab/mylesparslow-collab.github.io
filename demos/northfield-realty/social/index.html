How it works

Sources in, approved posts out

The engine watches four feeds and writes a draft the moment something changes. Each agent gets their own queue in their own voice. Nothing publishes until a person taps approve.

Trigger

Something happens

A listing goes live, drops in price, goes under contract or closes. A new 5-star review lands. A guide is published. A neighborhood comes up in rotation.

Draft

The engine writes

Caption, hashtags, image pick, carousel slides or a 30 to 60 second video script, in the firm voice or the agent's voice, with the listing number and sources attached.

Check

Every draft is screened

Fair-housing language, MLS attribution, commission wording, no made-up facts. Anything flagged is held for a human.

Approve

Dana or the agent taps approve

Drafts land in a shared queue. Edit, approve or skip from a phone in under a minute.

Post

Scheduled at the best times

Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok, with each agent's handle tagged on their own posts. Results feed back into next week's mix.

Voice rules

What every draft is held to

Sounds like a top-tier brokerage

Confident, warm, specific. Boutique and independent, never "small". The track record shows up in the details, not as a boast.

Fair-housing safe by construction

Homes and neighborhoods are described by what they are: architecture, light, parks, commute, price. Never who they are "for", never schools, safety or demographics.

Facts come from the feed

Prices, addresses and listing numbers are pulled, never typed. Beds, baths and square footage appear only when the listing record supplies them. No invented client stories.

Fees stated carefully, never as the hook

Fee structures are mentioned in context when a seller topic calls for it, worded exactly as on the site, with "commissions are negotiable". They never lead.

Each agent sounds like themselves

Agents get first-person drafts tied to their own listings and reviews. The broker's personal account is a separate, optional track that stays off until she opts in.

Always a next step

Every post ends with one clear action: see the listing, read the guide, get a home valuation or message the agent.

If Northfield says yes

What turning it on involves

Week 1: connect the sources

The listing feed from the local MLS, sample client reviews, the guides on the website, and each agent's handles.

Week 2: voice samples and approval

Each agent records a two-minute voice memo about how they talk to clients. Drafts flow into a shared queue; Dana sets who approves what.

Week 3: live, with a safety net

Posts go out on schedule. Anything the screen flags is held. A weekly summary shows what performed and adjusts next week's mix.

Later: listing launch kit

The same trigger that drafts a "just listed" post can also produce the flyer, the email to the agent's sphere and the open-house reminder.
