Seller disclosure vs. disclaimer: what sellers must tell buyers

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

In many states, sellers choose between completing a property condition disclosure and offering a disclaimer that sells the home as-is. Even with a disclaimer, certain items, such as known lead paint, must still be disclosed. Ask your agent which applies where you live.

In this article
  1. What is a disclosure?
  2. What is a disclaimer?
  3. Items that always apply
  4. Practical advice
  5. Frequently asked questions

What is a disclosure?

A written statement of what you know about the home's condition: roof, water, systems, pests and more. It protects you by putting your knowledge on record.

What is a disclaimer?

A statement that you make no representations about condition. The buyer typically relies on inspections. It does not excuse hiding known material defects.

Items that always apply

Safety and federal items, such as lead paint disclosures, must be disclosed regardless of the form chosen.

Practical advice

Be honest and thorough. An advance inspection helps you disclose accurately. Consult an attorney for legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I be sued for an omission?

Failure to disclose known material defects can create liability. Ask an attorney.

Do I need to disclose repairs?

Generally yes, if they relate to material conditions.
