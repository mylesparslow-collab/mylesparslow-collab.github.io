Living in Elm Hollow: a neighborhood guide

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

Elm Hollow is known for shingle and Tudor homes around a village green. This guide covers its history, typical homes, things to do nearby and what to consider when buying, all described by architecture, parks and amenities.

Where is Elm Hollow?

Elm Hollow sits on the north side of the city, organized around Hollow Green, a small village square with a bandstand, a handful of independent shops and a seasonal market. Tree-lined streets radiate out from the green in a gentle, irregular pattern.

What is the history of Elm Hollow?

The neighborhood was laid out in the early twentieth century as a planned residential district. Its designers kept the existing elm and oak canopy, curved the streets to follow the land and reserved the green as a shared gathering place. Many original street trees are still standing, and a neighborhood association maintains the green.

What kinds of homes are in Elm Hollow?

Shingle-style and Tudor houses with deep porches are the signature here, along with Colonial Revival and Arts and Crafts designs, most on mature lots. A smaller number of attached homes and apartments sit near the square.

What is there to do in and around Elm Hollow?

Hollow Green hosts a weekend market in warm months and an evening concert series. Cafes, a bookshop and a bakery are a short stroll from most addresses, and a leafy loop trail circles the neighborhood edge.

How do you get around from Elm Hollow?

A main avenue links Elm Hollow to downtown, and local bus routes stop near the green. Cyclists use a marked route toward Brookside.

What should you know before buying in Elm Hollow?

Many homes are a century old, so choose an inspector comfortable with plaster, original windows and older wiring. Some blocks have design guidelines for exterior changes; ask for them before planning an addition. Well-kept homes draw steady interest, so alerts and an early pre-approval help.
