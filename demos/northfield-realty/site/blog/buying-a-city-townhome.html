Buying a city townhome: a buyer's guide

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

City townhomes offer central locations and architectural character, often at lower prices than detached houses. Because they share walls, roofs and sometimes utility lines, a careful inspection and a review of any rules or party-wall agreements matter.

In this article
  1. Why consider a townhome?
  2. What to inspect
  3. Shared elements and agreements
  4. Planning renovations
  5. Frequently asked questions

Why consider a townhome?

Townhomes often sit close to restaurants, parks and transit, and they carry less exterior maintenance than a detached lot. Layouts are typically narrow and tall, with a main level, upper bedrooms and sometimes a finished lower level.

What to inspect

Roofs, flashing and rear additions deserve attention, along with basement moisture, original plumbing and the condition of shared walls. Our inspection checklist covers the details.

Shared elements and agreements

Ask whether there is a party-wall agreement, a homeowners association or shared drive. Confirm who maintains fences, courtyards and common roofs, and how repair costs are divided.

Planning renovations

Before you plan a kitchen or an addition, ask about permits and any neighborhood design review. Older homes can hide surprises behind walls, so build a contingency into your budget. Review our historic neighborhoods overview for areas with design guidelines.

Frequently asked questions

Are townhomes cheaper to insure?

Often, but premiums depend on age, materials and claims history. Get quotes before you offer.

Is parking included?

It varies by property. Confirm what is deeded and what is street parking.

Sources

  • Your local assessor, recorder or municipal office (confirm current rules)
  • A licensed attorney, tax professional or lender for your situation
  • The local MLS and your Northfield Realty agent for current market conditions
