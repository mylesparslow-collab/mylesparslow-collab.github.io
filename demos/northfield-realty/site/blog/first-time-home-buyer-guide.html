Buying

A first-time buyer's guide to the city

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 8 min read ·
Quick answer

Buying your first home goes more smoothly when you work in order: set a realistic budget, get pre-approved, learn which assistance programs you may qualify for, then tour homes with a clear list of must-haves. Plan for closing costs and a cash cushion as well as the down payment.

How much home can you afford?

Start with your monthly comfort level, not the maximum a lender will approve. Add principal, interest, property taxes, insurance and any association dues, then leave room for maintenance.

A common rule of thumb is to set aside one to two percent of the purchase price each year for repairs. Your agent can walk through a sample budget with you, and a lender can confirm numbers for your actual credit profile.

Why get pre-approved first?

A pre-approval letter shows sellers you are a serious buyer and tells you exactly what you can spend. It also surfaces credit or documentation issues while there is still time to fix them.

  • Gather two years of tax returns, recent pay stubs and bank statements
  • Avoid new credit lines or large purchases before closing
  • Compare offers from at least two lenders

What assistance programs exist?

Many states and cities offer down-payment help, reduced-rate loans or closing-cost grants for eligible first-time buyers. Eligibility usually depends on income, purchase price and completion of a short education course. See our overview of assistance programs and local incentives for what to ask.

How should you tour homes?

Make two lists: must-haves and nice-to-haves. Tour a few neighborhoods in different price ranges so you learn the trade-offs. Our neighborhood checklist helps you compare consistently.

What goes into an offer?

Your agent will review recent comparable sales and recommend a price, then propose contingencies for inspection, financing and appraisal. Understanding the timeline after acceptance makes the weeks ahead feel less uncertain.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I save for closing costs?

Closing costs commonly run a few percent of the purchase price and include lender fees, title services, prepaid taxes and insurance, and transfer taxes where they apply. Your lender provides a written estimate.

Do I need twenty percent down?

No. Many loan types accept much lower down payments, though smaller down payments may mean mortgage insurance.
