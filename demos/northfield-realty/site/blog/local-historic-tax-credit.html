Local historic tax credits: a homeowner's guide

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

Many cities and states offer a tax credit for approved renovation work on historic homes. The details vary, but the pattern is similar: confirm the property qualifies, get plans approved before work begins and keep thorough records.

In this article
  1. What is a historic tax credit?
  2. Which homes qualify?
  3. The usual steps
  4. If you are buying
  5. Frequently asked questions

What is a historic tax credit?

It is a reduction in property or income tax tied to qualified rehabilitation of a designated historic building. Credits are meant to encourage owners to preserve original features rather than replace them.

Which homes qualify?

Typically a home must be listed in a historic district or individually designated, and the work must meet preservation standards. Interior work may or may not be included.

The usual steps

  • Confirm designation with the local preservation office
  • Submit a plan before any work starts
  • Document the work with photos and receipts
  • File the claim after completion and approval

If you are buying

Ask whether any credit is already in place, transfers to a new owner or requires continued compliance. See our historic neighborhoods overview and homestead credit guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need approval first?

Almost always. Starting work before approval can disqualify it.

Are credits guaranteed?

No. Programs have caps, deadlines and funding limits; confirm with the administering office.

Sources

  • Your local assessor, recorder or municipal office (confirm current rules)
  • A licensed attorney, tax professional or lender for your situation
  • The local MLS and your Northfield Realty agent for current market conditions
