Buying a home with a well and septic system

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

Homes outside municipal service often rely on a private well and a septic system. Both are very manageable with the right inspections: a water-quality and flow test for the well, and a pump-out and camera inspection for the septic tank and drain field.

In this article
  1. Evaluating the well
  2. Evaluating the septic
  3. Local rules
  4. Living with them
  5. Frequently asked questions

Evaluating the well

Order a water test for bacteria, nitrates and other contaminants, plus a flow test to confirm the well delivers enough water. Ask for the age of the pump, pressure tank and casing.

Evaluating the septic

A qualified inspector should locate the tank, pump it, inspect baffles and assess the drain field. Ask for the maintenance history and the permit on file.

Local rules

Some jurisdictions require testing or a certification at sale. Check with your agent and the local environmental office. Replacement or repair costs can be significant, so negotiate informed.

Living with them

Plan to pump the tank every few years, avoid pouring grease or harsh chemicals down drains and keep heavy vehicles off the drain field. Our larger lot neighborhoods guide notes where private systems are common.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a septic be pumped?

Many systems benefit from pumping every three to five years, depending on size and use.

Can lenders require testing?

Some loan types do. Ask early.
