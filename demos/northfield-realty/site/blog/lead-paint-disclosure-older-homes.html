Lead paint and pre-1978 homes: what buyers and sellers must know

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 4 min read ·
Quick answer

Federal law requires sellers of most homes built before 1978 to disclose known lead-based paint hazards and give buyers a pamphlet and a chance to test. Many older homes are fine when paint is intact; the goal is to understand and manage the risk.

In this article
  1. What does the law require?
  2. If you are buying
  3. If you are selling
  4. Local requirements
  5. Frequently asked questions

What does the law require?

Sellers and agents must provide the lead hazard information pamphlet, disclose what they know and attach a disclosure form to the contract. Buyers typically have a period to arrange a lead inspection or risk assessment.

If you are buying

Consider testing, especially if you plan renovations. Intact, well-maintained paint is lower risk than chipping paint or friction surfaces such as windows. Ask contractors to use lead-safe work practices.

If you are selling

Gather records of past testing or abatement and complete the disclosure honestly. Addressing visible peeling paint before listing can reduce friction in negotiation.

Local requirements

Some jurisdictions add rental or registration rules beyond federal law. Confirm with the local housing office. Our disclosure guide explains related seller duties.

Frequently asked questions

Does a lead disclosure kill the sale?

No. It is a standard step, and buyers often proceed with a plan for safe maintenance.

Who pays for testing?

Typically the buyer, but terms are negotiable.
