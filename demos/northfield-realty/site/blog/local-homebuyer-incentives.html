Local homebuyer incentives: what to ask about

NR
Northfield Realty teamSample article · 5 min read ·
Quick answer

Cities, employers and community groups sometimes offer incentives such as closing-cost grants, employer-assisted down payments or property tax relief for new owners. Availability and amounts change, so confirm each one directly with the program administrator before relying on it.

In this article
  1. What kinds of incentives exist?
  2. How do you find them?
  3. When should you apply?
  4. Fine print worth reading
  5. Frequently asked questions

What kinds of incentives exist?

  • Closing-cost grants for buyers who purchase in designated areas
  • Employer-assisted programs for staff of large institutions
  • Matched savings programs that grow a down payment
  • Temporary property tax relief for newly renovated or purchased homes

How do you find them?

Start with your lender and your agent, then check the city housing office and your employer's benefits portal. Local nonprofit housing counselors often maintain an up-to-date list and can review your eligibility at no cost.

When should you apply?

Many incentives must be reserved before you sign a contract or before closing, and some require pre-purchase counseling. Ask about deadlines when you first talk to a lender. Our first-time buyer guide shows where incentives fit in the timeline.

Fine print worth reading

Some incentives require you to live in the home for a set period or repay a share if you sell early. Others limit the price or the property type. Read the agreement in full, and ask an attorney or counselor if anything is unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Are incentives taxable?

It depends on the program. Ask a tax professional about your situation.

Can I use more than one?

Sometimes. Programs may allow stacking up to a combined limit.

Sources

  • Your local assessor, recorder or municipal office (confirm current rules)
  • A licensed attorney, tax professional or lender for your situation
  • The local MLS and your Northfield Realty agent for current market conditions
