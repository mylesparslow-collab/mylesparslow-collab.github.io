What the screen looks for

Based on Fair Housing Act advertising guidance and commonly added state protected classes
  • Words that describe who should live there instead of the property (family, kids, singles, professionals, retirees, couples).
  • Familial-status cues: "perfect for a growing family," "empty nesters," nursery, "mother-in-law suite."
  • Religion and national origin: churches or places of worship as selling points, ethnic descriptions of an area.
  • Disability cues: "able-bodied," "handicap," "no wheelchairs." Accessibility features are fine to list as features.
  • Steering language: "safe," "low crime," "exclusive," "good schools," school ratings. Name parks, transit and shops instead.
  • Source-of-income and other added classes: "no Section 8," "no vouchers," gender identity or marital-status references.
  • Brand voice: "master bedroom" becomes "primary," and Northfield is never described as "small."
