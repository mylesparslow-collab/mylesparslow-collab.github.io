Northfield Realty

Past-client nurture

A monthly home value and equity note for every past client

Each client who bought or sold with Northfield gets a short, personal email once a month: what their home is worth, how much of it is theirs, what sold nearby, and one thing worth knowing. It keeps the agent in the picture for years, so the listing comes back when they move.

How it runs each month

1

Client list from the CRM: name, address, closing date, price, loan terms (collected at closing, with their OK).

2

Value estimate for each home from an AVM feed, refreshed monthly, with a likely range instead of a single number.

3

Loan balance computed by amortizing the original loan. The email invites the client to correct it if they refinanced.

4

Market snapshot and comps pulled from the same data file that powers the neighborhood market reports, so the two always agree.

5

Agent review: a one-page queue showing every figure before send. Anything that moved more than 5% in a month gets flagged.

6

Send from the client’s own agent through the brokerage’s email tool, with replies landing in that agent’s inbox.

Why it earns listings

Most brokerages lose touch within a year of closing. A note that is actually about the client’s own house gets opened, and the reply button sits next to the agent’s name every month. When the client decides to sell, Northfield is the obvious call, and the pricing analysis is already half done.

  • Soft asks only: a pricing analysis or a reply. No listing pitch in the body.
  • Every number has a plain-language caveat and an unsubscribe link.
  • The agent, not the broker, signs it. Clients keep the relationship they already have.

What’s reusable

One HTML template with merge fields such as {{first_name}}, {{value_mid}}, {{equity}}, {{mkt_median_price}} and {{comps_rows}}, plus a small renderer that fills them from the client list and the market file. The same fields map onto common email-tool merge tags.

Market figures in this demo are placeholders. Clients, addresses, loans and comps are fictional.
